code_surface: openChart (the open_chart Frappe application in opensoft/openChart-code). In scope: openChart's core clinician-authored clinical record. It has one act model for six act classes: encounter, clinical note, diagnosis of record (encounter diagnosis and problem-list condition), procedure or service performed, a generic order, and the prescription record. The model covers the identification and provenance of every addition to a chart, and the recommendation record for a decision made without authority (owner ruling 80); authorship with an act author type of `clinician`; status lifecycles; signing as the practitioner's sign-off with openWallet and openSig (owner ruling 151); amendment by new versions; entered-in-error handling, a co-signature hook, provenance and audit, and an act-type registry. It also covers clinical roles, the medical director designation and role grants recorded in openChart, with practice-supplied, unverified licensure and prescriber facts; explicit, attributed creation of clinician records from patient-reported intake; codings from practice-supplied code sets; the canonical content schema `openchart-clinical-act-content/1.0`; a FHIR R4-compatible mapping at contract level; and publication of signed, final encounters, their diagnoses and procedures to openPractice under `openpractice-ehr-clinical-facts/1.0`, by reference, with forward-only corrections. Out of scope: results ingestion; care plans and treatment-plan records; immunizations; clinical decision support; allergy and interaction checking (the required later change `define-prescribing-safety-checks`); electronic transmission of prescriptions to pharmacies, controlled-substance electronic prescribing, and controlled-substance prescribing (ruling 83); order transmission; AI authorship of acts (`define-ai-authored-clinical-records`); a path for device data; openWallet's licence verification and openSig's attestation, which this change consumes and does not define; billing and coding validation (openPractice); and any change to the ratified `expanded-patient-intake` requirements.

## Why

openChart is the public, neutral clinical chart, and the doctor's clinical interface on mobile or workstation (rulings 34 and 35). Its built scope is patient intake only:
- `OC Patient` and `OC Patient External Identifier`;
- `OC Intake Submission`;
- eight patient-reported statement families.

There are no clinician encounters, notes, diagnoses, procedures, orders or prescriptions. Every statement is patient-reported, and the ratified `expanded-patient-intake` capability keeps it so.

Three draft changes, none yet on main, wait for this foundation:
- **`define-ai-authored-clinical-records`** (openChart-spec#4) lists it as its required prerequisite, with twelve minimum contents. Its Tier 2 adds AI authorship, an authority grant, clinician co-review and reversal on top of the acts defined here. This change defines no AI authorship of acts: under ruling 80, act content an AI adds is entered as a recommendation (AN-09).
- **`define-openpractice-boundary`** (openChart-spec#2): its `openpractice-clinical-fact-publication` capability reports "no billable facts" until a clinical-encounter capability exists (CF-06, Open Question 2). openPractice needs final, signed encounter documentation, diagnoses and procedures, with forward-only corrections and references rather than copies (`opensoft/openPractice-spec` `define-mvp-practice-foundation`, EB-13 to EB-16).
- **`define-clinical-message-inbox`** (openChart-spec#3) records clinician replies as clinical communications (MI-12). It says that only a separately governed clinical workflow may record a clinical fact of record.

This change is that workflow: openChart's core clinician-authored clinical record. Under ruling 80, it also holds the general rule for every addition to a chart, because it holds the act model and the single grant model (ruling 92).

## What Changes

The scope below is ruled (owner ruling 73). Additions to the chart are ruled by rulings 80 and 151; the design calls that encode them are proposed.

- **Additions to the chart** (AN-01 to AN-14; rulings 80 and 151).
  - Any human or system the chart can identify may add to a patient's chart: a clinician, other staff, the patient, an AI, a device or another system. An addition whose writer it cannot identify, or whose writer is an unregistered system or device or is suspended, is refused, and nothing is stored. Another party it cannot identify is recorded as not identified. An AI consult for an AI that a registered relay's registration does not cover is kept in quarantine, outside the chart, in front of a clinician, and the practice is alerted; it is refused only when the relay itself cannot be identified (ruling 194). The administrative role or a clinician releases such a consult once the registration is fixed, and it stays on the clinician's review list until a clinician has read it (ruling 201); only a clinician dismisses it, with a recorded reason about its content (ruling 202). An AI consult that arrives without the reference to the patient's consent is kept, marked not accepted, in front of a clinician and flagged to the medical director (ruling 199). Extending these to every addition for such an AI is proposed.
  - Each writer is identified by its kind: a named account, the patient's verified link, an AI's openWallet ID with its model and version, a device's make, model and serial or a resolvable device ID, or a registered system and its operator. Another organization's clinician or system is named by its own identifier, vouched for by the system that delivered it. Systems and devices are registered first, by the practice's administrative role, and that role or the medical director can suspend any of them (ruling 159).
  - Every addition records when it was received and when the information arose, where it came from, what wrote it and on whose behalf. That record is permanent and readable by anyone who can read the chart.
  - Decision content takes effect only when its class and act type are in force in the act-type registry. Otherwise it becomes a recommendation, never refused and never unmarked information. A note is information (ruling 187). A clinical note's content, or any other record of information, is information only when it validates against its kind's closed, published shape (this change publishes the note shape and the document shape of ruling 204: a title, a date, text, and attached PDFs or pictures that are only displayed), and anything else is a recommendation (proposed beyond ruling 204's documents). Recording that an order was carried out is a record of work done (ruling 187); a prescription is never completed in this version (proposed). Content is kept, as entered, as a recommendation, and only content the chart cannot read at all is refused (ruling 188). openChart determines the class, and a class the writer claims decides nothing.
  - A decision counts only when its decision maker held the authority at that moment and a practitioner signed it off in their own logged-in session, with their openWallet and openSig (ruling 151), in openChart only in version 1 (ruling 186). An AI only ever makes recommendations until a practitioner signs (ruling 157). Signing a note or an encounter, stopping or correcting a decision, a reply to a patient and a review of an AI consult need the sign-off; inbox housekeeping does not (rulings 158 and 187). A decision made on someone's behalf counts only with their own sign-off.
  - A decision made without authority is never refused for that reason. It is kept in full, as given, as a recommendation record, marked "not an authorized decision", naming the missing authority, with no effect. Its submitter is told at once, and it goes to a clinician who holds the authority, who adopts it as a decision of their own or declines it.
  - A failed sign-off never loses what the author wrote. A failure the author can cure leaves the draft open, and a failure of authority makes a recommendation tied to the draft, which is held for it. A note from a writer without the authority to sign it is kept as information, and its writer is told at once.
  - openWallet and openSig do not yet specify, in ratified text, the licence verification and the attestation that ruling 151 needs. Both are now drafted, unratified: openWallet-spec change `add-practitioner-profile-and-licence-verification` (draft pull request #10, at `834db41`) and openSig-spec change `add-wallet-bound-attestation` (draft pull request #4, at `92b097e`). CA-04's checks follow those drafts (proposed): openChart verifies the practitioner's proof itself, so a forged attestation never stands in for it; a failed sign-off is one of four kinds, and only a missing authority makes a recommendation; the verifier is independent of the practice (ruling 210), and for the first practice it is a verification service the owner runs, with its key shipped in openChart (ruling 211); an old revocation list never blocks a sign-off, and the medical director is alerted once the newest list held is over 72 hours old (ruling 212); a verification is relied on until the licence expires, and at most 12 months (ruling 213); a sign-off holds up against forgery even against whoever runs the server, not against deleting records or back-dating (ruling 214). A practitioner signs with a passkey or a security key (ruling 208); the openWallet proof profile for them, `wallet-webauthn-proof`, is drafted as openWallet-spec change `add-webauthn-proof-profile` (draft pull request #11, at `fbb74f0`), unratified, and until it is ratified no practitioner can sign. A passkey that syncs counts only on a device the practitioner has enrolled, and a security key always (ruling 209); beyond the ruling's words, a key whose backup eligibility the authenticator reports clear counts without an enrolled device, as a security key does, and what enrolling means is proposed, both put to the owner. Proposed too: the custody the ratified profile gives each key ruling 209 admits, and no other, is accepted; a verifier key is relied on only for the licensing authorities it is held for; revocations are permanent; the ceremony the profile allows, with the verifier's related origin; the signer shown from the sealed licence verification, never from the account alone; and stated limits of ruling 214, with two controls: no profile detects a deployment showing one statement while the key signs another; a verification names the practitioner's own key only if the verifier identified the practitioner in the session in which the key proved itself; and whoever administers or can recover the device, browser, extension or passkey provider used to register a key or to complete the verifier's session, the account a passkey syncs through among them, can get a key named that the practitioner does not hold, against which the binding records the practitioner's declaration, put to the owner. Until openWallet's registry has a custody model for a passkey that syncs, none can be bound, and a security key with no PIN or biometric set can neither be bound nor sign; both are put to the owner. No real decision is made until both exist; openChart is built and tested against stand-ins meanwhile, which never make a decision in a live chart (ruling 152), and the medical AI stays switched off for patients, advice-only included, until a clinician's review of an AI consult can be signed off (ruling 207). Until then a practitioner's drafts stay drafts, and openChart publishes no billable facts (tension CT-20; CD-24, proposed).
- **One act model** (CA-01 to CA-15). Six act classes share one envelope and one canonical content schema, `openchart-clinical-act-content/1.0`, pinned by SHA-256 in `contracts/SHA256SUMS`, with synthetic fixtures and a self-test. Every act has:
  - a named practitioner author, with the author type `clinician`. Decision content from any other author is entered as a recommendation record, never refused for that reason, and a note's content from any other author is kept as information;
  - a closed status lifecycle;
  - a signature that is the practitioner's sign-off: re-authentication in their own session, their openWallet key's proof, with openSig's attestation of it, over the SHA-256 of a statement that binds the act, the action, the signer's session account, the time, a single-use challenge and, for an encounter, the manifest of every record its signing view showed, the wallet's licence verification and the grant in force (ruling 151). A discontinuation, an entered-in-error mark, a co-signature and the cancellation of a signed order are sign-offs too. An audit verifier recomputes every digest from the stored record;
  - amendments as new versions, never destructive edits;
  - entered-in-error handling;
  - a co-signature hook (before effect, or after the fact within a window, escalating to the medical director);
  - full provenance and audit;
  - an act type from a registry that carries reserved and emergency-management flags.

  Acts are created only by a named practitioner through the versioned service boundary. Nothing is created from intake automatically. Codes come from practice-supplied code sets; openChart bundles none and asserts no license status. Acts have a FHIR R4-compatible mapping at contract level, with no conformance claim.
- **Authority** (AU-01 to AU-07). openChart defines clinical roles (`clinician`, `nurse`) and one medical director per practice. The designating account is never the designee, and the designee's role rests on a licensure attestation recorded by another named human. The first medical director is set up by one audited bootstrap act. Grants per act class and action, plus a `review` scope over `ai_consult_record` for AI consult reviews, are recorded by the medical director, and a self-grant is flagged, independently attested and shown. Licensure and prescriber facts are attestations supplied by the practice, never recorded by their own subject, and not verified by openChart. Under ruling 98, a second person confirms the first designation, or a sole administrator records a licence verification against the licensing board's public record, and these acts are listed for the medical director too. Under ruling 92, inbox authority lives in this grant model, set by the medical director: AU-06 adds the clinical message inbox's ownership, reply and clinical-need marker scopes, none of which reaches an act. A practitioner binds their own wallet to their account, only in their own session, with each key's own proof of possession, and enrols the devices from which a passkey that syncs may sign (AU-07; rulings 208 and 209). Every action is checked against its decision maker's authority in force, whatever kind of decision maker it is. An action without that authority takes no effect and is entered as a recommendation, and reductions apply at once. Only a named human practitioner signs off a decision. No AI, agent, device or plain integration holds decision authority today, and no agent or automated process authors, signs or co-signs (ruling 67; ruling 80, clause 19).
- **Encounters and notes** (EN-01 to EN-06):
  - an encounter lifecycle (in progress, final, amended);
  - an optional appointment reference only, since openPractice owns the appointment (ruling 31);
  - signed notes and addenda;
  - finalization only when documentation is complete;
  - a signed encounter amendment for every later change;
  - no requirement depends on form factor (ruling 35).
- **Diagnoses and procedures** (DX-01 to DX-03): coded, clinician-asserted encounter diagnoses and problem-list conditions, kept distinct from patient-reported conditions, and coded procedures performed.
- **Publication to openPractice** (PB-01 to PB-05). This change is the capability that CF-06 waits for. Until ruling 152's condition is met, it records no real decision, so it publishes no billable facts and reports no billable-facts capability, and openPractice's manual entry applies (proposed, CD-24). Once it is met, a signed, final encounter, with its coded diagnoses and procedures, is published under `openpractice-ehr-clinical-facts/1.0`:
  - by reference;
  - with codings carrying the system and code only;
  - with encounter diagnoses only when `confirmed`, and a retraction if a published one is later refuted (ruling 87);
  - with forward-only corrections and retractions, queued whatever the link state and delivered when the link returns to `active`. Retractions still reach openPractice, as pointers only, after a link is revoked or superseded (ruling 88). A fact whose latest version could no longer be published is retracted, and every withdrawn encounter reads the same.

  openChart still works with no openPractice.
- **Orders** (OR-01 to OR-04). One generic order record covers laboratory, imaging, referral, procedure and other orders. Its lifecycle is draft, pending release, released, completed, discontinued, cancelled and entered in error. A released order is never amended. A prescription has the same lifecycle without completed: in this version it ends only by a signed discontinuation. openChart transmits no order and ingests no result.
- **Prescriptions** (RX-01 to RX-04). The record of the prescribing decision carries a controlled-substance classification in which unknown counts as controlled.
  - **Binding gate (ruling 74):** no prescription is signed, co-signed or released in any deployment where the prescribing-safety gate of `define-prescribing-safety-checks` is not met.
  - Controlled or unknown prescriptions are refused, and controlled-substance prescribing goes into a later change alongside electronic prescribing (ruling 83).
  - There is no pharmacy transmission, no controlled-substance electronic prescribing and no dispense record.
