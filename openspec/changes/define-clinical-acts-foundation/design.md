### 3. The approved wording

**The rule in one line:** anyone or anything the chart can identify may add to it, the chart records exactly where each addition came from, and only a decision by someone authorized to make it counts as a decision.

**A. Who may add**
1. Any human or system may add to a patient's chart: a clinician, other staff, the patient, an AI, a device or another system. [One sentence of the approved wording stands here. It names an earlier rule of a private companion repository, which this ruling replaces in part, and is recorded in full in that repository's change.]
2. Nothing is added unless the chart can identify what is adding it. An addition it cannot identify is refused, and nothing is stored. *(added)*

**B. How each is identified**

| Who or what | Identified by |
|---|---|
| A clinician or other staff member | Their own named account in the chart *(added)* |
| The patient or their representative | The verified link they come through *(added)* |
| An AI | Its openWallet ID, plus the model and version that produced the content *(model and version added)* |
| A device | Its make, model and serial number, or a medical asset or device ID the chart can resolve to them |
| Any other system: an app, an integration, a relay | Its registered system identity and who operates it *(added)* |

3. Every system is registered with the practice before it can add, and the practice can suspend any system or device at once. *(added)*

**C. What every addition records**

4. **When:** the time the chart received it, and the time the information itself arose where that differs, such as when a reading was taken.
5. **Where the information came from:** the patient's own words, a device reading, an AI consult, a clinician's observation, another organization's record or an earlier chart entry, with a reference to it.
6. **What added it:** the human or system that wrote it into the chart.
7. **On whose behalf:** when a system adds something for another system or a human, the chart records both, and every hand it passed through.
8. This record is permanent and visible to anyone who can read the chart. Nothing in it is edited or deleted; a correction is a new dated addition that points to the old one. *(added)*

**D. Information and decisions**

9. Every addition is either information or a decision. *(added)*
   - Information is an observation, a reading, a statement, a message, a summary or a recommendation.
   - A decision changes the patient's care or what the chart holds as established: a diagnosis, an order, a prescription, a referral, a treatment plan or a sign-off.
   - The chart keeps the list of what counts as a decision.
10. Information needs only the identification above.
11. A decision counts as a decision only when its decision maker held the practice authority for that kind of decision at that moment. The chart records the authority each decision rested on.
    - For a human, that is their licence and what the medical director has granted them.
    - For an AI, it is the mode and scope the medical director has approved for it.
    - A device or a plain integration holds none.
12. When a system adds a decision on someone's behalf, authority is judged on the decision maker, never on the system that carried it. The decision counts as theirs only when the chart can confirm they made it, by their own sign-off or an approval of theirs it can check. Otherwise it is entered as the carrying system's recommendation. *(added)*

**E. A decision made without authority**

13. It is still entered in the chart, in full, as a recommendation and not a decision. Wherever it is shown, it is clearly marked "not an authorized decision" and says which authority was missing.
14. It has no effect. Nothing is ordered, prescribed, sent, billed or treated as established because of it. *(added)*
15. Whoever or whatever submitted it is told at once that it was entered as a recommendation. *(added)*
16. It goes in front of a clinician who holds the authority. They can adopt it, which creates a new decision of their own that points back to it, or decline it. Either way the recommendation stays in the chart as written. *(added)*
17. This applies to humans and systems alike: a staff member who enters an order they are not authorized to give is treated the same way as an AI.

**F. What does not change**

18. Missing authority is never a reason to refuse an addition. These still are: it cannot be identified, the patient cannot be matched, the content is malformed or test data, or the practice has suspended the writer. *(added)*
19. An AI holds no decision authority today. Advice only is the only mode that can be switched on, and limited and full practice stay specified and off (ruling 67). So everything an AI adds now is information or a recommendation.
20. An AI never manages an emergency, and the urgent hand-off to a human stays as it is.
21. Consent, the patient's link, the medical director's pause and narrowing, and clinician review of what an AI told a patient all still apply.
22. [Clause 22 of the approved wording stands here. It keeps two other parts of that private rule as they are, does not bear on the chart, and is recorded in full in the private companion change.]

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Give openChart a clinical record that any identified human or system may add to. It records where each addition came from, only a practitioner's authorized sign-off makes a decision (rulings 80 and 151), and its authorship, signing, amendment, entered-in-error, co-signature and audit rules are the same for every act class.

## Decisions

### 1. One act model, one content schema

Six act classes share one envelope (CA-01, CD-1).

| Act class | Content | FHIR R4-compatible resource |
|---|---|---|
| `encounter` | types, period, participants, location, optional appointment reference, reasons | Encounter |
| `clinical_note` | encounter, note kind and type, text, addendum link, attachments by digest | DocumentReference (CD-26) |
| `diagnosis_of_record` | role (`encounter_diagnosis` or `problem_list`), coding, verification and clinical status, rank, dates | Condition |
| `procedure_performed` | encounter, coding, time, performer, quantity, body site | Procedure |
| `order` | category, text, optional coding, priority, reasons, intended performer, release time | ServiceRequest |
| `prescription` | medication, controlled-substance classification, dosage, quantity, refills, release time, transmission status | MedicationRequest |

The envelope holds:
- act identifier and version, class and type, and the openChart patient triple;
- status, author and recording time;
- signatures, amendment links and the co-signature requirement;
- derivation provenance, the restricted mark and lifecycle events.

`contracts/openchart-clinical-act-content-1.0.schema.json` is the canonical representation of one act version, one recommendation record and one information record, told apart by `record_kind`. It follows the convention of the sibling contracts: JSON Schema draft 2020-12, a URN `$id`, closed objects and `contracts/SHA256SUMS`. It is pinned by digest. `contracts/examples/` holds 166 synthetic fixtures: 49 that must validate, 99 that must fail the schema, and 18 that pass the schema but must fail the integrity verifier of CA-08, each for one reason stated in `examples/expected-failures.json`. `contracts/selftest.py` checks them, checks that each invalid fixture fails with exactly one error, and runs the verifier over every valid fixture: it recomputes each content, provenance, sign-off statement and encounter manifest digest, with each act's status at the time of signing worked out from its lifecycle, and checks calendar times and dates, a defaulted time, signed events, the dating of an item released from quarantine and that a held item ends once, that each sign-off's evidence agrees with itself (the attestation's challenge, digest and key, the licence the grant names, the reliance bound and the revocation list's age), holds, and challenges within each record and across the valid fixture set, which stands in for one store. It passes on jsonschema 4.10.3 and 4.26.0 (task 1.2). The fixtures' declared content schema, `syn-declared-ai-content`, is synthetic and names no real schema.

Every rule holds whether or not a validator asserts `format`: a calendar date is checked by the pattern `^[0-9]{4}-(0[1-9]|1[0-2])-(0[1-9]|[12][0-9]|3[01])$`, and a timestamp by the pattern `^[0-9]{4}-(0[1-9]|1[0-2])-(0[1-9]|[12][0-9]|3[01])T([01][0-9]|2[0-3]):[0-5][0-9]:([0-5][0-9]|60)(\.[0-9]{1,9})?(Z|[+-]([01][0-9]|2[0-3]):[0-5][0-9])$`, which allows one to nine fractional-second digits and leaves a day the month lacks to the verifier.

Each class's content is a named definition (`$defs/encounterContent` and so on), so a MINOR version of `openchart-ai-clinical-record-write` can pin it for `act_content` (CD-28).

A record of information holds its kind's closed shape (AN-06, CD-65): `$defs/clinicalNoteContent`, or `$defs/documentContent`, the minimal document shape for summaries, letters and imported records (a title, a date, text, and attachments by reference and SHA-256, each of media type `text/plain`, `application/pdf`, `image/png` or `image/jpeg`). `document` is a kind of record of information, not an act class.

### 2. Author type, the writer, and the hook for Tier 2

Every act version carries an author type, the author's reference, role and authority basis (CA-02). An act's author type is `clinician`: a named human with an openChart clinical role grant, which covers the `clinician` and `nurse` roles, as MI-12 does (CD-3). Under ruling 80, act content from any other author is not refused: it is entered as a recommendation record that names its author by `kind` (AN-02, AN-09). Every addition, an act included, also names its writer and every party on whose behalf it was written (AN-04). The authority basis is the role grant and its version.

`define-ai-authored-clinical-records` Tier 2 would add the author type `ai` for acts, with an authority basis pointing at its own grant, in a new MINOR version of both contracts and through its own governed change. Ruling 157 settled Open Question 12: an AI only ever makes recommendations until a practitioner signs, and limited and full practice as designed would need a new ruling. Until such a ruling, an AI's act content is a recommendation record. Nothing in this change's act model needs to change for that.

### 3. Lifecycles

`signed` is an event, not a resting status (CD-4). It moves a draft to `final` (documentation classes), or to `pending_release` or `released` (orders and prescriptions).

| Class | Resting statuses | FHIR status mapping (contract level; to be checked in planning against the FHIR R4 specification) |
|---|---|---|
| `encounter` | `in_progress`, `final`, `amended`, `cancelled`, `entered_in_error` | Encounter.status: `in-progress`; `finished`; `finished`; `cancelled`; `entered-in-error` |
| `clinical_note` | `draft`, `final`, `amended`, `cancelled`, `entered_in_error` | DocumentReference.docStatus: `preliminary`; `final`; `amended`; not exposed; `entered-in-error` |
| `diagnosis_of_record` | as for notes | Condition.verificationStatus from content; `entered-in-error` when entered in error |
| `procedure_performed` | as for notes | Procedure.status: not exposed while draft; `completed`; `completed`; not exposed; `entered-in-error` |
| `order` | `draft`, `pending_release`, `released`, `completed`, `discontinued`, `cancelled`, `entered_in_error` | ServiceRequest.status: `draft`; `on-hold`; `active`; `completed`; `revoked`; `revoked`; `entered-in-error` |
| `prescription` | as for orders, without `completed` in this version (RX-01, CD-58) | MedicationRequest.status: `draft`; `on-hold`; `active`; `stopped`; `cancelled`; `entered-in-error` |

A lifecycle change appends an attributed, dated event. Content changes only by a new version (CA-05).

### 4. Signing: the practitioner's sign-off (ruling 151)

A signature binds these items (CA-04, CD-9, CD-48):
- the signer and the signer's role;
- the role grant and its version;
- a coded attestation statement and its version, whose wording the medical director maintains per act class;
- the time;
- the SHA-256 of the RFC 8785 canonical JSON of the content signed;
- ruling 151's sign-off evidence: the `openwallet_id`; the key that signed, by DID URL and fingerprint; the licence verification relied on, by reference and SHA-256, with its licence facts, what the grant relied on was made against, the revocation list used and the verifier key's provenance; the openSig attestation itself; the SHA-256 of the provenance; the custody model of the key that signed, with its basis `declared`; and the check's result (CD-76, PROPOSED).

The attestation statement (CD-9) is the practice's coded wording of what the signer attests. The practitioner's proof, openWallet's ratified `wallet-challenge-proof`, is made over the SHA-256 of the sign-off statement only, and openSig's wallet-bound attestation is ruling 151's record that the proof came from that wallet; openSig never receives the statement, the patient or any content (CD-75, PROPOSED, against openWallet-spec change `add-practitioner-profile-and-licence-verification` (draft pull request #10, read at `4cfef0a`) and openSig-spec change `add-wallet-bound-attestation` (draft pull request #4, read at `b7d37a5`), both unratified drafts whose field names this change re-checks when they settle). openChart accepts it by four checks of its own: the attestation verifies under openSig's attestation key held in the deployment, with result `verified`; openChart verifies the sealed proof under the sealed public key over its own recomputed statement digest; the sealed key fingerprint is a key bound to the signer's account and named by the licence verification relied on; and the sealed challenge reference is the statement's. A forged attestation never stands in for the proof. The statement binds the act and version, the patient, the class and type, the action, the signature role, the signer, the attestation, both digests, the time, a single-use challenge issued at re-authentication and, for an encounter's signature, the digest of the manifest of every record its signing view showed, acts and information records alike (CD-53). The signer is the account whose session re-authenticated. An author signature is a sign-off with action `sign`, and a co-signer's has role `cosigner` and action `cosign`. An audit verifier recomputes every digest from the stored record, the manifest included, and checks calendar times, challenges and holds. It runs on demand, at export, before publication to openPractice and before an interoperability read, and treats a mismatch as an integrity failure (CA-08). openChart requires re-authentication at the moment of signing; the mechanism is decided in planning (CD-10). The practitioner signs off in their own logged-in session (CD-52), with a wallet bound to their account in their own session (AU-07) and a key whose custody evidences that they acted (CD-48). A discontinuation, an entered-in-error mark and the cancellation of a signed order carry the same evidence on their lifecycle events, with role `actor`. A co-signature's `cosigned` event carries an identical copy of the co-signer's signature evidence. Authority is checked first. A failed sign-off is one of four kinds (CD-79, PROPOSED): the author can cure it by trying again; only the deployment can cure it, and the administrative role is alerted; only a new binding can cure it; or the authority is missing, and the content becomes a recommendation tied to the draft. None is a refusal, and none cancels the draft (AN-14, Decision 19).

### 5. Amendment, entered in error and drafts

- **Amendment.** A new version references its predecessor, states a reason and carries its own author and signature. Any clinician with sign scope for the class may amend. Every prior version stays readable (CA-05, CD-5).
- **Released orders and prescriptions** are never amended. A change is a discontinuation plus a new act (OR-03, CD-6).
- **Entered in error** is the void. It is marked by the author, a clinician with sign scope, or the medical director, with a coded reason. Content stays for audit (CA-06, CD-7).
- **Drafts** may be revised before they are submitted, with every revision audited; a draft is not yet an addition (CD-60). An abandoned draft is `cancelled`, never deleted (CD-8). A failed sign-off never cancels a draft or an encounter (AN-14, CD-46).

### 6. The co-signature hook (ruling 62)

A co-signature requirement attaches to one act version (CA-07, CD-11). It records:
- its source;
- its mode: `before_effect`, where the act is held as `pending_release` (orders and prescriptions only in this change), or `after_the_fact`;
- a due time;
- a co-signer, who is a named clinician other than the author with co-sign scope;
- one outcome: co-signed, amended by the co-signer, or entered in error.

A co-signature adds the co-signer's signature, with role `cosigner` and action `cosign`, and a `cosigned` event carrying an identical copy of its sign-off evidence, which the verifier checks (CA-08). While a `before_effect` requirement is pending or escalated, the act is never `released`, `completed` or `discontinued`; the contract enforces both.

When the due time passes, the requirement escalates to the medical director and keeps alerting. It is never closed by a timer, a default or an agent.

In this change the only source is the author's role grant, for example a supervised clinician or a nurse. This maps onto `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` AA-07 and AA-08 one for one:

| AA-07 / AA-08 | This hook |
|---|---|
| Queue at once, with a due time from the grant's window | Requirement with mode `after_the_fact` and due time = signing time + window |
| Confirm | Outcome `cosigned` |
| Amend: a new, dated, clinician-authored act that visibly supersedes the AI act | Outcome: an amendment by the co-signer (CA-05). A new version may have a different author. |
| Void | Outcome: entered in error (CA-06) |
| Overdue escalates to the medical director, re-alerts, never closed by default | Escalation rule of CA-07 |
| No AI or agent co-reviews; an attempt is a recommendation | AU-05, AN-09 |

Tier 2 adds its authority grant as a second `source` value in a MINOR version. An encounter whose signature has an open co-signature requirement is not published until it is co-signed (PB-04).

### 7. Authority

- **Roles.** openChart defines two roles, `clinician` and `nurse`, plus a medical director designation: one per practice, designated by an audited act of an administrative role named in planning (AU-01, CD-12).
- **Independence (CD-34).** No single account can reach clinical signing on its own say-so:
  - the designating account is never the designee;
  - the designee's `clinician` role rests on a licensure attestation recorded by another named human;
  - nobody records an attestation about themselves (AU-03);
  - a grant the medical director records for themselves is flagged `self_granted`, rests on an independently recorded attestation, is shown on every act signed under it, and is listed for the administrative role (AU-02).
- **The first medical director.** One audited bootstrap act by the designating account records three things: the designee's licensure attestation, the `clinician` role and the designation. The role confers no act-class authority, so the first medical director then self-grants each act class under the rule above.
- **Confirming the first designation (ruling 98; CD-38).** A second named human, neither the designating account nor the designee, confirms the first designation. A practice with only one administrator instead records a licence verification against the licensing board's public record. Designations, attestations and self-grants are listed for the administrative role and for the medical director once one exists. This closes the one-person-two-accounts path to prescribing.
- **No medical director, nothing signable.** With no medical director, no grant can be recorded, so nothing can be signed.
- **Inbox scopes (AU-06, ruling 92; CD-39, CD-40).** As ruling 92 directs, the single grant model, set by the medical director, also carries the clinical message inbox's scopes. None of them reaches an act:
  - ownership: actions `own`, `acknowledge`, `reassign`, `raise`, `return`, `split` and `close` over `clinical_inbox_item`, in a `physician` or `nurse` lane, with the medical director's nurse-lane scope for the nurse lane. A `physician`-lane grant goes only to a holder of the `clinician` role, and a nurse's grants stay in the nurse lane and its scope;
  - patient reply: action `reply` over `clinical_communication`, per role and lane, on an attestation;
  - clinical-need markers: actions `mark` and `withdraw` over `clinical_need_marker`, for the `clinician` role only.

  There is no on-call designation: the inbox's cover roster is a schedule that may name only holders of the lane's ownership grant.
- **Grants.** Grants are per person, per act class and per action (`draft`, `sign`, `cosign`, `complete`, `discontinue`), optionally limited to act types. Each grant has an interval, rests on a practice-supplied attestation and may carry a co-signature requirement. Only the medical director records them (AU-02). `System Manager` confers no clinical authority. For a sign-off, a grant confers authority only together with the licence verification in the practitioner's openWallet (CA-04; ruling 151).
- **Consult review scope (CD-37).** A grant may instead carry the scope `review` over the record class `ai_consult_record`, for a person holding the `clinician` role. It lets the holder record AI consult reviews under `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` AG-11. It is not an act class, creates no act and gives no authority over any act. Every other grant rule applies, the self-grant rule included. This settles that change's tension AT-12.
- **Attestations.** Licensure and prescriber facts are attestations supplied by the practice, with an expiry, recorded by someone other than their subject. openChart does not verify them and says so (AU-03, CD-13). Under ruling 151, a sign-off also relies on the licence verification in the practitioner's openWallet. Ruling 160: "Both must hold, and ruling 98's check stays." (Tension CT-18, settled.)
- **Prescriptions fail closed.** A prescription-signing grant needs a prescriber attestation in force, and recording one without it is refused (practice configuration, CD-49). An expired attestation stops the grant at once, and a later signing attempt takes no effect and is entered as a recommendation.
- **Checks at the moment of action.** Every action is checked against its decision maker's authority in force at that moment, whatever kind of decision maker it is. An action without that authority takes no effect, and is entered as a recommendation, never refused for that reason. A completion of an order is a record of work done, not a decision: without the `complete` scope it changes nothing and is kept in the order's trail as a report. A prescription is never completed in this version; it ends only by a signed discontinuation (CD-58). Reductions apply at once (AU-04).
- **Wallet binding (AU-07, CD-54).** A practitioner binds their own wallet to their account, only in their own re-authenticated session, with a proof of possession from a key the wallet declares with custody `isolated_per_use_authorized`. Whether the wallet's holder, and the subject of its licence verification, is the account's person is Open Question 9. One wallet binds to one account. An administrator may suspend a binding but never create one.

### 8. Act-type registry

The registry lists, per class, the act types a practice uses (CA-09, CD-14). Each type has a code, a display name, an interval, a version and two explicit flags, `reserved` and `emergency_management`. The medical director maintains it. The flags do nothing for clinician acts in this change. They are the data that `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` AA-04 to AA-06 read. Under ruling 80, the registry, together with AN-06, is also the chart's list of what counts as a decision: decision content takes effect only when its class and act type are in force in it. Decision content whose class or act type the chart does not define or hold in force, including while the registry is empty, is entered as a recommendation with `no_decision_authority` when it arrives without a draft, never refused and never kept as unmarked information (AN-06, CD-44). A sign-off of a draft whose act type was retired or is not defined is refused with `act_type_not_in_force`, and the draft stays a draft (CD-51).

### 9. Patient-reported content stays patient-reported

A clinician may create a draft act from an intake statement only by an explicit action, recorded as derivation provenance (`derived_from`: kind, reference, version, who and when) (CA-11, CD-22). The same applies to a clinical communication record under MI-12, once `define-clinical-message-inbox` lands.

Nothing is created automatically, not even a recommendation. The source statement is never changed, keeps `assertion_kind` patient-reported, and its `verification_state` is never set by this change. The clinician's record is a separate act, signed off by the clinician. This is how the ratified "No intake, supplement statement, API response or extension event SHALL create a prescription, diagnosis, order…" is honored: a draft act is created only by a named clinician's authenticated command through the versioned boundary, and takes effect only by a practitioner's sign-off, its author's or an authorized clinician's adoption of it (CA-04, AN-11), and never by intake, a statement, an API response or an extension event (CA-10; tension CT-1).

### 10. Encounters and notes

- **The encounter.** It is opened `in_progress` with one primary performer (EN-01).
- **The appointment.** openPractice owns the appointment (ruling 31). The encounter may hold an opaque appointment reference, and nothing more (EN-02, CD-17). The 1.0 contracts carry no appointment events, so the reference is entered by an authorized user or left absent until a later MINOR version of the link contract adds events. A walk-in has none (openPractice EB-19).
- **Signing the encounter.** It needs all of these: the signer is a participant with encounter sign scope, at least one final note, no attached drafts, and an end time (EN-04, CD-15). The encounter signature is the documentation-final signature.
- **After finalization.** Every change to attached acts goes through a signed encounter amendment, which publication treats as a forward-only correction (EN-05, CD-16).
- **Notes and replies.** Notes are signed by their author, and addenda are separate notes. Clinician replies to patients stay MI-12 communications (EN-03).

### 11. Diagnoses and procedures

- **Diagnoses.** A diagnosis of record is either an encounter diagnosis (with an encounter and an optional rank) or a problem-list condition (with a clinical status). It is always coded and carries a verification status. An uncoded diagnosis cannot be signed (DX-01, CD-25).
- **Kept apart from reports.** Diagnoses stay distinct from patient-reported conditions in storage, display and reads (DX-02).
- **Procedures.** Procedures are coded, attached to an encounter and performed by a participant (DX-03).

### 12. Publication to openPractice: how CF-06's placeholder is replaced

CF-06 reads: "Until a separately governed openChart capability records clinician-authored encounters, diagnoses and procedures, openChart SHALL report `billable_facts_supported: false`…" This change is that capability (PB-01). It replaces the placeholder by meeting CF-06's own condition, not by editing `define-openpractice-boundary`:

1. In a deployment where features (2) and (3) are enabled, the boundary change's publication surface is built, and ruling 152's condition is met, `capability.result` reports `true` (CD-24).
2. In every other deployment CF-06 still governs: the result is `false` and reads return `no_billable_facts`.
3. Disabling feature (3) returns the result to `false` at once.

**Ruling 152 and billing (CD-24, PROPOSED).** Until ruling 152's condition is met, openChart records no real decision, so it has no signed diagnosis or procedure to publish: it publishes no billable facts, reports `billable_facts_supported: false` in its `capability.result`, and every facts read returns `no_billable_facts`, so openPractice's manual entry applies (`define-mvp-practice-foundation`, owner rulings 21 and 116). Ruling 152's condition is met when openWallet and openSig can do their part, so a practitioner's sign-off under CA-04 can make a real decision (task 2.7). A sign-off checked against a stand-in never meets it (CD-69). This consequence is design call CD-24, PROPOSED; no contract changes for it. A test deployment, one that accepts stand-in sign-offs and holds only synthetic patients, may report `true` and publish synthetic facts, but only to an openPractice endpoint the administrative role has recorded as a test double; openChart refuses any other link from it, so the contract's supported path can be tested and a test deployment never publishes to a live openPractice (CD-69, PROPOSED). The closed `capability.result` payload of `openpractice-ehr-clinical-facts/1.0` already carries `billable_facts_supported`, and CF-06 already says what holds while it is false, so neither the contract nor `define-openpractice-boundary` needs to change for this.

What is published, field by field, against the canonical schema at `6b2071fe…` (PB-02):

| Contract field | Source in this change |
|---|---|
| `encounter.documentation_final` notification | The encounter becomes `final`. Resource references only, written to PL-14's durable store, and delivered at once on an `active` link or held as a pending final while the link is in `identity_review` (PB-02, PB-03). |
| `encounter.id`, `version` | Encounter act identifier and act version |
| `encounter.status` | `final`/`amended` → `finished`. Facts are read only for those statuses: a non-final encounter, and every withdrawn one (entered in error, restricted, or unrepresentable), reads as `no_billable_facts` (CD-24) |
| `encounter.type`, `period`, `location_ref`, `participants` | Encounter content. Codings as system and code; practitioner references opaque. |
| `conditions[]` | Each `final`/`amended` `encounter_diagnosis` on the encounter whose verification status is `confirmed` (ruling 87; CD-33): category `encounter-diagnosis`, coding (system, code), rank, encounter reference |
| `procedures[]` | Each `final`/`amended` procedure: coding, quantity, `performed`, `performer_ref`, encounter reference |
| `documentation_final` | `final: true`, `signer_ref` and `signed_at` from the encounter version's author signature |
| `clinical_fact.corrected` | An encounter amendment: new versions in `resource_refs`, replaced versions in `supersedes` (PB-03) |
| `clinical_fact.entered_in_error` | A published diagnosis, procedure or encounter marked entered in error; a published diagnosis later refuted, or otherwise amended away from `confirmed` (ruling 87); or a restricted act added to a published encounter (PB-03, CD-33, CD-36). It stays the only retraction until ruling 89's reason code lands (CT-16) |

**Corrections whatever the link state** (PB-03, CD-35). Every correction or retraction of a published fact is written to the durable store whatever the link state:
- on an `active` link, it is delivered at once;
- while the link is in `identity_review`, it is held together with pending finals, and all are delivered in order when the link returns to `active`. Pending finals are re-evaluated first;
- if the link ends `revoked` or `superseded`, retractions are delivered as pointers only, corrections are delivered as retractions of the superseded versions, pending finals are dropped, and no read is served (ruling 88; tension CT-15).

**Withdrawn facts** (PB-03; CA-N2, CA-N3). A published fact whose latest version could no longer be published is retracted with `clinical_fact.entered_in_error`, the only retraction until ruling 89's reason code lands (CT-16), whatever the reason: no longer `confirmed`, moved to the problem list, a coding the schema cannot hold, entered in error, or, for a whole encounter, a restricted act. Every withdrawn encounter then reads as `no_billable_facts`, whatever the reason, so a read never reveals a restricted act.

So openPractice never keeps billing a fact that openChart has corrected or withdrawn.

What is never published (PB-04):
- patient-reported statements and communications;
- encounter diagnoses that are not `confirmed` (ruling 87);
- the problem list, which the contract has no category for;
- notes, orders and prescriptions;
- drafts;
- restricted acts;
- recommendation records, information records, and any addition that is not a decision signed off with authority (AN-09, AN-14);
- encounters awaiting co-signature;
- anything the schema cannot represent. In that case nothing is published for the encounter, and an integration exception is recorded.

A read of a non-final or withdrawn encounter returns `no_billable_facts` (CD-24). Codings drop the version and display, because CF-01 and FD-11 allow a system and a code only (tension CT-11).

openChart still works with no openPractice, and a delivery failure never blocks a clinical act (PB-05).

### 13. Orders

There is one generic record, with five categories (OR-01, CD-18):
- `laboratory`, `imaging`, `referral` and `other`, from the brief;
- `procedure`, from `define-ai-authored-clinical-records`' minimum list.

Release follows signing unless a `before_effect` co-signature or a future release time holds the order in `pending_release` (OR-02). `released` means in force inside openChart. openChart transmits nothing and ingests no result; completion is a named person's explicit act (OR-04, CD-19).

### 14. Prescriptions and the prescribing-safety gate (ruling 74)

The prescription records the prescribing decision (RX-01).

**Gate.** RX-02 makes ruling 74 binding. No prescription, and no new version of one, is signed, co-signed or released in a deployment where the prescribing-safety gate is not met for that step. A deferred release that the gate refuses stays `pending_release`, and a work item goes to the signer; no timer releases it. The co-signer sees the alerts and overrides recorded so far. The gate is met only when three conditions hold:
1. `define-prescribing-safety-checks` is ratified;
2. its capability is built and enabled in that deployment;
3. the signing meets that change's signing-time conditions. Under rulings 76 to 78 these are a connected, licensed or verified knowledge source and a completed check, or the clinician-only outage path with a recorded statement.

Ruling 74 says "specified". This design reads that as ratified, and it adds the runtime conditions so that a deployment cannot sign in the gap between ratification and a working check (CD-30). Feature (5), prescriptions, is not buildable before that change is ratified (tasks §2.4).

**Controlled-substance classification.** The classification is required. `unknown` counts as `controlled`. Signing a prescription classified `controlled` or `unknown` is refused (ruling 83; RX-03, CD-20), and `define-prescribing-safety-checks` refuses it in its gate too. Controlled-substance prescribing goes into a later change alongside electronic prescribing (ruling 83). No rule about controlled substances is asserted.

**No transmission.** There is no transmission, no controlled-substance electronic prescribing, no pharmacy-facing document and no dispense record, and the transmission status is fixed at `none_recorded` (RX-04, CD-21).

### 15. Coding and licensed intellectual property

A coding stores the system, the code, the code-system version (where the practice's code set declares one), the display and the reference of the practice-supplied code-set import (CA-12, CD-23). openChart bundles no code set and asserts no license status for any code system. Bundling anything needs license verification and a governed change. This is consistent with openPractice FD-11's "never licensed content in the repo". openChart goes further than FD-11 in one respect: it does not adopt FD-11's list of license-free sets, because FD-11 governs openPractice (tension CT-12). Synthetic fixtures use synthetic code systems only.

### 16. FHIR R4-compatible mapping (contract level, no conformance claim)

| Act | Resource | Key mappings |
|---|---|---|
| Encounter | Encounter | status (§3); type; period; participant.individual = practitioner reference, participant.type from participation; location; appointment reference only as an identifier |

A recommendation record is never exposed (CA-13) |

Drafts, cancelled acts and restricted acts are never exposed (CA-13). openChart claims no FHIR certification and no profile or implementation-guide conformance.

### 17. Standalone operation, logs and restricted acts

- **Standalone.** Every capability works with no external system, with three exceptions. A decision's sign-off needs the practitioner's openWallet and openSig (ruling 151), the one named exception to openWallet's never-a-prerequisite rule (ruling 154), checkable inside the practice's own deployment (ruling 156; tension CT-19, settled), and prescription signing also needs the gate (CA-14). The rest of the chart works without a wallet. Where a check cannot be made, a sign-off attempt leaves its draft open and is recorded (AN-14). Third, openChart fetches each verifier's revocation list when the deployment can; the fetch never blocks a sign-off, and a reliance on a list older than the freshness bound (72 hours unless the practice sets another) stands, flagged and raised (CD-81, PROPOSED). The golden-intake tests stay the regression gate.
- **Logs.** Logs and coded reasons carry identifiers only.
- **Restricted acts.** Sensitive categories and minors' records are an open question for counsel (Open Question 3). Meanwhile, a clinician may mark an act `restricted`. A restricted act, and any encounter carrying one, never leaves the chart (CA-15, CD-27). No legal rule is asserted.

### 18. Handoff shape and feature order

There are five ordered features, each gated on ratification (ruling 85; CD-31, CD-41):
1. clinical authority (AU-01 to AU-07), its own first feature (ruling 92), together with the general rule for additions (AN-01 to AN-14; CD-41, PROPOSED), built first and on its own, so that `define-clinical-message-inbox` and `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` Tier 1 can rely on it before any act exists (#3 MT-22);
2. encounter, note and signing (CA-01 to CA-15, EN-01 to EN-06);
3. diagnoses, procedures and publication;
4. orders;
5. prescriptions, gated on ratification of `define-prescribing-safety-checks` (ruling 74).

The shared workflow protocol's default is exactly one Spec Kit feature per OpenSpec change. Ruling 1 approved an ordered multi-feature exception for openPractice. Ruling 85 allows this change the same ordered multi-feature exception (tension CT-14, settled).

### 19. Additions to the chart (rulings 80 and 151)

The capability `chart-additions` (AN-01 to AN-14) holds the general rule for every addition to a patient's chart, whoever or whatever makes it. The other openChart drafts cite it.

- **Who may add (AN-01, AN-03).** Any identified human or system may add.
  - An addition is refused, and nothing is stored, only for a reason that concerns its writer: the chart cannot identify the writer (clause 2), the writer is a system or device that is not registered (clause 3), or the practice has suspended it.
  - Another party that cannot be identified is recorded as `not_identified` and never causes a refusal (CD-66).
  - The case of an AI that an identified, registered writer speaks for, whose openWallet ID is missing or not covered by that writer's registration: it is not added. Ruling 194 decides this for an AI consult: it is kept in quarantine, outside the chart, in front of a clinician, the practice is alerted to fix the registration, and it is refused only when the relay itself cannot be identified (Open Question 22, closed). Extending it to every addition for such an AI, an act included, is PROPOSED (CD-61). So, PROPOSED, are an AI that is not identified in the form the writing path reads, malformed or with a chain of parties the path cannot read (`ai_not_identified`, CD-70), and a party whose coverage the path's registration defines and does not give (`source_system_not_covered`, CD-71). Quarantined in the chart and held in quarantine outside the chart are two kinds, defined once in AN-01; the first admits, PROPOSED beyond ruling 90's own case, a write with no consent reference and an AI's information write that fails a consent or grant check.
  - A held item is kept as it arrived and never deleted; its writer is told at once; the administrative role is told the AI's identification and the cause, without reading the item; it is not an addition while held. It ends once, by release into the chart, by a clinician's own record, or by a clinician's dismissal with a reason from the path's closed list, and each outcome is recorded on it. A released item stays selected for clinician review. Only an item that itself carries a complete, well-formed identification that the registration now covers can be released, with the writer identified, registered and not suspended and every check applied as of release; an item held as `openwallet_id_missing` or `ai_not_identified` never is, and its content reaches the chart only through a corrected write, linked by `corrects_held_item_ref`, or a clinician's own record; the addition is dated by its release, and its provenance carries `released_from_quarantine` with the first arrival (CD-61).
  - Systems and devices are registered with the practice first, and the practice can suspend any of them at once (CD-43).
  - Practice configuration, the wallet binding of AU-07 included, is not an addition (CD-49).
  - A draft is not yet an addition. The addition is the record openChart creates on submission: a signed act version, a recommendation or an information record (CD-60).
- **Identification (AN-02).** Each party has a `kind`: `human`, `patient`, `ai`, `device` or `system`, with the identifying fields of the ruling's table and an `identity_basis` (CD-42). A writer is always verified by the chart.
  - A human is identified by their named account only. Role, licence attestation and wallet are recorded where they apply, and their absence never makes a writer unidentified (CD-55).
  - Another organization's clinician or system is named by `external_ref`, asserted by the system that delivered it. It is never the writer, and such a clinician holds no openChart role (CD-57).
  - A relay that delivers an AI's record is the writer, and the AI is named on whose behalf it wrote. An AI with no openWallet ID, or with one the relay's registration does not cover, is held in quarantine outside the chart (ruling 194), and so is an AI that is not identified (CD-70, PROPOSED). An AI's `model` has the form `^[A-Za-z0-9._:/@+-]{1,128}$` that the AI write path and the inbox use.
  - A party other than the writer that cannot be identified, such as an outside letter's author, is recorded with `identity_basis` `not_identified` and `described_as`, and no account, role, licence attestation or wallet (CD-66). It is never a recommendation's decision maker. A writer, an AI and a patient never are `not_identified`.
  - A wallet identifier never becomes a person's identifier, as openWallet's ratified rule requires.
- **Provenance (AN-04, AN-05).** Each addition records `received_at`, `occurred_at`, `source` (a kind and a reference; `clinician_reply` is the kind for a clinician's reply to a patient), `added_by` and `on_behalf_of`. A writing path may supply a missing source as the kind its path implies, marked `defaulted` and with no reference, and a missing or future time as `received_at`, marked `occurred_at_defaulted`; nothing is refused for either (CD-73, PROPOSED). It may carry the writer's `submission_ref`, with `submission_ref_conflict` when it reused one (CD-64), `released_from_quarantine` for an item released from quarantine (CD-61), and `corrects_held_item_ref` for a corrected write (CD-70). The record is permanent from submission and readable in openChart by anyone who can read the chart. A correction points back to what it corrects (`corrects`). The ratified intake path keeps its own identity and audit until a later change aligns it (CT-17).
- **Information and decisions (AN-06, CD-44).** Decision content is:
  - act content of the classes other than the clinical note;
  - content submitted as an act of a class the chart does not define;
  - the kinds clause 9 names;
  - every sign-off.

  Decision content takes effect only when its class and act type are in force in the registry, and otherwise it becomes a recommendation with `no_decision_authority`. A clinical note is information, while signing a note or an encounter, and stopping or correcting a decision, are decisions (ruling 187; CD-63). Recording that an order was carried out is a record of work done (ruling 187), and a prescription is never completed in this version (CD-58, PROPOSED). openChart determines the class itself; a declared class is kept but decides nothing. Every kind of record of information has a closed content shape: the note shape and the minimal document shape here (title, date, text, and attachments by reference and digest, limited to media types that are only displayed), or one published by the change that owns the path. Content declared as a kind is information only when it validates against that kind's shape; content of a kind with no published shape, or that does not validate, is always a recommendation with `no_decision_authority`, whatever its field names. Text inside any record of information never creates, counts as or authorizes a decision (CD-65).
- **A decision (AN-07, AN-08, CA-04, AU-04).** A decision counts only when its decision maker held the authority at that moment and a practitioner signed it off under ruling 151.
  - A decision added on someone's behalf counts only with that practitioner's own sign-off, which in version 1 is made in openChart only (ruling 186; CD-52; Open Question 17, closed). Otherwise it is a recommendation. For a decision claimed for a person, the carrying system is its decision maker and the person the claimed decision maker (CD-67).
  - An AI covered by the delivering relay's registration is the decision maker of what it sends, its identity asserted by the relay, with no claimed decision maker (CD-61, CD-67). A decision maker that is not a human always carries `no_practitioner_sign_off`.
- **The practitioner's sign-off (CA-04, CD-48, CD-53).**
  - The practitioner signs off in their own logged-in session, with re-authentication, and their openWallet and openSig sign and validate the entry.
  - openChart checks four things at that moment, all inside the deployment (CD-48, CD-75, PROPOSED, against the two drafts):
    - the account is bound to the key that signs (AU-07), each key bound individually by its own `wallet-challenge-proof` (CD-54);
    - the licence verification relied on passes openWallet's whole check: the verifier's signature, every recorded proof of possession, that it names the signing key, its reliance bound, supersession, the newest revocation list held, the held wallet record's state and the verifier key's state; and it is for the licence the grant was made against (CD-78);
    - the sign-off is accepted: the attestation verifies with result `verified`; openChart verifies the sealed proof itself over its recomputed digest of the sign-off statement, which binds the act, version, patient, action, role, signer, attestation, both digests, the time, a single-use challenge and, for an encounter's signature, the manifest of every record its signing view showed; the sealed key fingerprint is bound and named by the verification; and the sealed challenge is the statement's;
    - the medical director's grant is in force, made against the licence verified.
  - It accepts only a key of custody `isolated_per_use_authorized`, for the sign-off and for the wallet binding's proof (AU-07).
  - The signature, or for an action on an existing act its lifecycle event, records the evidence (`sign_off`). The signer is the session account. An author signature is `sign`; a co-signer's is `cosign` with role `cosigner`, and the `cosigned` event carries an identical copy.
  - An audit verifier recomputes every digest from the stored record, the manifest included, and checks calendar times, challenge reuse and holds. It re-verifies openSig's signature, the sealed proof and the licence verification, checking revocation against the list recorded on that reliance, and has a mode that takes verifier keys only from a release and runs on an exported copy (CD-77, PROPOSED). It runs on demand, at export, before publication and before an interoperability read, and treats a mismatch as an integrity failure (CA-08).
- **When a sign-off fails (AN-14, CD-46).**
  - A failed sign-off is one of four kinds (CD-79, PROPOSED): one the author can cure by trying again (openSig unavailable, an attestation not verified for this attempt, re-authentication failed, a changed statement digest); one only the deployment can cure (openWallet's `not_checkable`, an attester or attestation-key fault, openSig refusing openChart's own request), which alerts the administrative role; one only a new binding can cure (custody not accepted); and a failure of authority. The first three leave the draft open, with the failure on the attempt trail and no recommendation. A check openWallet or openSig does not yet specify cannot be made, so while they specify nothing, drafts stay drafts.
  - The checks run in this order: the draft's act type, then authority, then the rest of CA-04, then any gate. A sign-off of a draft whose act type was retired or is not defined is refused at the first check, whoever wrote it, and the draft stays a draft, with no recommendation (CD-51; Open Question 23).
  - A failure of authority enters the content as a recommendation tied to the draft. The draft is held, with `held_for` and a `held` event, and cannot be revised, resubmitted or cancelled while held. The hold ends with `hold_released`, by adoption or decline. An encounter keeps everything attached to it.
  - A recommendation is never gated.
  - A draft that blocks an encounter's signature and is not held, including one returned after its recommendation was declined, is listed for its author and the primary performer with a due time, and then escalates to the medical director, who may close it as `cancelled` (`closed_to_finalize_encounter`); the author and the primary performer are told, and its content stays readable (CD-68). A held draft is not reached by this: it ends by adoption or decline, and its review item escalates.
- **The recommendation record (AN-09 to AN-11, CD-45, CD-47, CD-51, CD-56).**
  - It is `record_kind` `recommendation`. It holds:
    - the proposed action, and the act class and act type as given, whether or not the chart defines them;
    - the content exactly as given, checked only for being well formed, with `content_schema` where another schema declared it, for any action but a co-signature. Content declared a note or a document that validates as that shape is never a recommendation (CD-65);
    - for an action on an existing record, the act version as `target`, the writing path's own reference as `target_as_given`, and any reason or content as given (CD-51);
    - the same provenance as any addition;
    - `not_authorized_decision` and the coded `missing_authority`, with any `path_reason`;
    - the `decision_maker`, and the `claimed_decision_maker` only for AN-08;
    - the `receipt`;
    - its `review`, with every failed adoption.
  - It is never an act of record and is never gated.
  - It goes to a clinician who holds the authority. It escalates as a co-signature requirement does.
  - Adoption applies the act-validity rules and is a sign-off of the clinician's own, carrying `adopted_from`:
    - on a draft the recommendation is tied to. Adopted unchanged, the draft becomes the clinician's act, with the drafter kept as writer. Changed in any way, it becomes a new version of the clinician's own, which supersedes the held draft and points back to the recommendation (CD-47);
    - as a new draft, where there is none;
    - as the clinician's own action, for a proposed action on an existing act.
  - A failed adoption is recorded on the recommendation and creates no second one. Declining is recorded with a reason.
  - A repeated submission with the same content digest is answered with the first result. A reused `submission_ref` with different content is stored as a new addition flagged `submission_ref_conflict`, and the writer is told at once (CD-64).
  - Adopting an encounter signs off the manifest of the records its signing view showed, as every encounter sign-off does (CD-53).
- **A note from a writer without the authority to sign it (AN-14, CD-63)** is kept as information under its writer's name (`record_kind` `information`, marked not signed off), only when its content validates as note content (CD-65). Its writer is told at once (`receipt` `kept_as_information`). It is never a recommendation, never a final note and never published.
- **Still refused (AN-12).** An addition is still refused, with nothing stored, when:
  - the writer cannot be identified;
  - the writer is a system or device that is not registered (clause 3);
  - the patient cannot be matched;
  - the content is malformed or test data;
  - the practice has suspended the writer.

  A party other than the writer that cannot be identified never causes a refusal (CD-66), and an AI that the writer's registration does not cover is held in quarantine (CD-61). Ruling 188: content is kept, as entered, as a recommendation, and only content the chart cannot read at all is refused; the clinician who adopts it completes the missing parts (Open Question 18, closed). An addition whose envelope cannot be read is unreadable (CD-56, PROPOSED). A reused `submission_ref` is kept and flagged, not refused (CD-64). Test data is recognized only by a declared context or a test patient (CD-62). RX-02's prescribing-safety gate is a safety condition, not authority: a gated signature leaves the prescription a draft, and nothing the clinician wrote is lost.
- **Devices (AN-13).** How a device is identified and what a reading records are defined here. How device data enters the chart is not (Open Question 8).

**What openWallet and openSig specify today** (read on 2026-11-15; both repositories are public):
- **openWallet.** In `opensoft/openWallet-spec` at `a2d554d`, the canonical capability `openxwallet` says:
  - a wallet is a signing key, or a declared set of keys, held by a holder, and a holder may be a practitioner;
  - using a grant needs a signature from the holder's wallet key over the request;
  - custody is declared per key and bounds what a signature evidences;
  - every exercise is attributed to the key that presented it, and revocation is checked at use;
  - a wallet SHALL NOT become a prerequisite for reconstructing a record, resolving a subject or operating a domain, and a wallet identifier is attestation, never identity.

  Its contracts (`opensoft/openWallet-code` at `bb0e9da`) publish the ratified closed custody registry, `openxwallet-custody.registry.yaml`, version 1. In it, `holder_readable` and `isolated_invocable` evidence only the holder's environment, and only `isolated_per_use_authorized` evidences that the holder acted. The contracts also name a practitioner profile as a successor, "gated on a consumer of its own". No requirement or contract of openWallet defines a licence verification yet.
- **openSig.** In `opensoft/openSig-spec`, `main` at `23ebbfe` holds no specification. Two pull requests are open, neither merged:
  - #1 (`8668f30`) is a non-normative brainstorm;
  - #2 (`a05fcee`) is a corporate signing MVP for PDF envelopes with an organizational seal. Its non-goals include personal PKI enrollment and patient or clinical production workflows. It leaves ledger-anchoring wallets out of its anchoring interfaces, and its consumer scenario says that a consumer of a signing completion "cannot independently attest a clinical record".

  Both pull requests' agent instructions say to "Keep clinical attestation and amendments in openChart". Neither openSig's specification leg nor its two pull requests mentions openWallet. Nothing in openSig yet attests that a signature is from a wallet.

So ruling 151's sign-off depends on a licence verification and an attestation that neither project specifies yet (tension CT-20; Open Questions 9 and 11). Rulings 152 to 156 answer what follows: no real decision until both exist, with building and testing against stand-ins allowed (ruling 152; CD-69); a practitioner profile with a licence verification in openWallet and a wallet-bound attestation in openSig (ruling 153), which now exist as drafts, unratified: openWallet-spec change `add-practitioner-profile-and-licence-verification` (draft pull request #10, read at `4cfef0a`) and openSig-spec change `add-wallet-bound-attestation` (draft pull request #4, read at `b7d37a5`); one named exception to openWallet's never-a-prerequisite rule, a practitioner's sign-off of a medical decision (ruling 154); clinical attestation stays in openChart, and openSig only attests that the signature came from that wallet (ruling 155); and the sign-off is checkable inside the practice's own deployment (ruling 156). CA-04 follows those drafts, PROPOSED (CD-75 to CD-82), and re-checks their field names when they settle. Until both are ratified and realized, those two checks of CA-04 cannot be made, every sign-off attempt fails without a decision, and a practitioner's drafts stay drafts. This change invents nothing about either project beyond what the drafts state.

## Required later change: prescribing-safety checks

**`define-prescribing-safety-checks`.** It is required before any prescription can be signed (ruling 74), and is being drafted under ruling 75. Feature (5), prescriptions, depends on its ratification. RX-02 refuses every prescription signature until its gate is met. Its minimum contents:

1. **Clinician-confirmed lists.** A clinician-confirmed allergy and intolerance list and an active medication list. Each reuses this change's act model (author type, signing, amendment, entered in error, provenance) through new act classes in a MINOR version of `openchart-clinical-act-content`. It defines no second signing model.
2. **Reconciliation from intake.** The lists are reconciled from patient-reported medication, supplement and allergy statements only by an explicit, attributed clinician action (CA-11). Nothing is promoted automatically.
3. **Inputs.** A check reads both clinician-confirmed entries and patient-reported statements. A patient-reported entry is shown as patient-reported and is never ignored because it is unconfirmed. An uncoded or free-text entry that cannot be checked produces an explicit "could not be checked" warning and is never skipped silently.
4. **The checks (ruling 77):**
   - allergy (ingredient and class);
   - drug–drug interaction;
   - duplicate therapy;
   - drug–supplement interaction, only where the source declares support.

   Later changes will add dose range, drug–condition, pregnancy and lactation, renal and hepatic adjustment, and paediatric and geriatric rules.
5. **Behaviour.**
   - Checks run when a prescription is drafted or changed, and again at signing.
   - Each alert has a severity.
   - An override needs a recorded reason, and the most severe class needs an explicit override for each alert, never in bulk.
   - Each check records its inputs, the source and its version, the alerts raised and the clinician's response.
   - Alerts are labelled as advice, and the record shows the clinician as responsible.
6. **The knowledge source (ruling 78).**
   - openChart bundles no drug knowledge.
   - A versioned adapter contract, with a canonical schema and SHA256SUMS, covers normalization to ingredients, allergy cross-reference, interaction lookup, a capability declaration and source identity with version and effective date.
   - Nothing is asserted about any vendor, product, database or license; each must be verified before use.
   - Fixtures are synthetic.
7. **Gates, failing closed.**
   - No prescription is signed without a connected, verified source (ruling 78), and a practice that never had a verified source cannot sign (ruling 82). A verified source that is stale or down uses the outage path, with the medical director alerted, and signing stops after a staleness limit set by policy (ruling 82).
   - The outage path is for a clinician only: a recorded statement, a flag, and a re-check when the source returns. A non-clinician author, including any future AI author, never uses it (ruling 76).
   - Go-live needs a recorded legal and regulatory review, which states what it covers and asserts no rule.
   - Prescriptions classified controlled, or of unknown classification, are refused (ruling 83).
8. **The interface RX-02 calls.** One gate decision per signing attempt, with a coded reason, that this change's signing path can rely on.
9. **Boundaries.**
   - Everything except prescription signing works with no source.
   - No clinical content goes to openPractice.
   - A FHIR R4-compatible mapping at contract level (AllergyIntolerance, MedicationStatement or MedicationRequest, DetectedIssue), with no conformance claim.

### Proposed clarifying amendment (ruling 84)

Ruling 84 reads the ratified sentence narrowly: "it bars automatic creation from intake, and an alert to the clinician isn't a treatment recommendation." It also directs "a small clarifying amendment to the ratified text, so it can't be misread later". The ratified sentence is in the canonical `expanded-patient-intake` capability, requirement "Intake does not confer clinician or autonomous authority":

> No intake, supplement statement, API response or extension event SHALL create a prescription, diagnosis, order, administration, treatment recommendation or autonomous action.

Proposed wording, to follow that sentence in the requirement:

> This bars automatic creation from intake: it does not bar a clinician signing through the API, and an alert to the clinician is not a treatment recommendation.

The owner applies it to the ratified text at ratification, through that text's own governance. This change edits neither the canonical capability nor `specs/001-expanded-patient-intake/`, and adds no MODIFIED delta. The ratified feature text, `specs/001-expanded-patient-intake/spec.md` FR-008, states the same bar in other words. If the owner wants the two texts to match, the same sentence fits there.

## Design calls for owner review

**For the owner first, in plain words.** These are the PROPOSED calls with the most consequence:
- **Releasing a held AI item (CD-61).** Only an item whose openWallet ID the registration now covers can be released, by the administrative role or a clinician; it is dated by its release, and stays selected for clinician review until a clinician has reviewed it.
- **Dismissing a held item (CD-61).** Only a clinician dismisses, for a reason about the content; a decision not to register an AI is never by itself a reason; a wrong-patient item is restricted, reported to the administrative role and sent back to its writer.
- **Open Question 23.** A sign-off of a draft whose act type is not in force is refused, and the draft kept, rather than entered as a recommendation.
- **CD-68.** The medical director may close another person's overdue draft that blocks an encounter's signature; the author and the primary performer are told, and nothing is lost.
- **CD-65's consequence.** Content that fits no published shape becomes a "not an authorized decision" recommendation, even when it is not a decision; the document shape keeps plain summaries and letters out of that path, and a decline can say the content was never a decision.
- **CD-67.** A relay's registration is what confirms that an AI's content is that AI's.
- **The encounter manifest covers orders (CD-53).** An order shown at signing may later change by its own signed act without an encounter amendment.
- **`no_practitioner_sign_off` for every decision maker that is not a human (CD-67).**
- **No billable facts until ruling 152's condition is met (CD-24).** Until openWallet and openSig can do their part, openChart has no signed diagnosis or procedure, reports no billable-facts capability, and openPractice enters charges manually.
- **The sign-off seam with the openWallet and openSig drafts (CD-75 to CD-82).** openChart verifies the sealed proof itself, so a forged attestation never stands in for it; a failed sign-off is one of four kinds, and only a missing authority makes a recommendation; held verifier keys ship only with a release; an old revocation list flags and raises a sign-off and never blocks it, 72 hours by default.
- **Signing on a phone (CD-80).** It waits for a successor proof profile that is not drafted (Open Question 26).
- **The grant is the scope a licence covers (CD-78).** No scope-of-practice table; two questions for counsel (Open Questions 27 and 28).
- **Closing an item with no reply, and withdrawing a marker early, need the sign-off (CD-50; Open Question 25).**
- **What an AI adds as information gets a writing path's controls (CD-74; Open Question 29).**
- **Holding a write whose AI is not identified, or from an uncovered source system (CD-70, CD-71; Open Question 30).** Such a hold for an unidentified AI is never released.
- **Free text cannot be policed (CD-65).** Text inside a valid record of information never takes effect, but a clinician reading it may be misled.

Each item goes beyond the rulings and is proposed, not ruled. Where an item implements a ruling, it cites the ruling, and its mechanics stay a proposed design call. Where a ruling is silent on something consequential, the binding text fails closed and cites the design call.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **[A wrong-patient or wrong-content signature]** → Re-authentication at signing; a content digest bound to the signature; entered-in-error handling; nothing deleted.
- **[Patient-reported content mistaken for a clinician's]** → Separate records, explicit derivation, the statement never changed, and labels on every surface.
- **[A recommendation mistaken for a decision]** → No act status. It is never signed, released, published or billed, it is marked "not an authorized decision" wherever it is shown, and it never counts where a decision would (AN-09).
- **[An addition from a writer the chart cannot trust]** → Identification, registration and suspension, an identity basis on every party, and the AI's openWallet ID checked against the delivering system's registration (AN-01 to AN-03).
- **[The openWallet and openSig drafts change before they are ratified]** → The seam is PROPOSED and cites each draft at a head; every field name is re-checked when they settle, and nothing takes effect until both are ratified and realized (ruling 152).
- **[No decision can take effect until openWallet and openSig specify what ruling 151 needs]** → Every sign-off attempt fails closed, and a practitioner's drafts stay drafts, with every attempt recorded. Nothing is lost, and the same draft can be signed off once the checks can be made (ruling 152; CT-20, tasks §2.7). Stand-ins are for tests only and never make a decision in a live chart (CD-69).
- **[A captured attestation replayed for another action]** → The attestation is over a statement that binds the action, the act, the time and a single-use challenge (CD-53).
- **[Stored sign-off evidence altered or moved after the fact]** → An audit verifier recomputes every content, provenance, statement and manifest digest from the stored record, with the signer taken from `signer_ref` or `by_ref`, checks challenges, calendar times and holds, runs before publication and interoperability reads, and reports a mismatch as an integrity failure (CA-08).
- **[Decision content declared as information to avoid the marking]** → Every kind of record of information has a closed shape. Content declared as a kind it does not validate as, or as a kind with no published shape, is a recommendation with `no_decision_authority`, whatever its field names, and the contract requires that code for anything declared as other than a decision class (CD-65). Free text inside a valid record of information is the residue no shape can police: it never takes effect, but a clinician reading it may be misled (Open Question 20; listed for the owner).
- **[A released item back-dated into a signed encounter]** → A released item is dated by its release, keeps its first arrival in `released_from_quarantine`, and the verifier checks the order of the three times (CD-61, CA-08).
- **[An administrator binding one person's account to another's wallet]** → Only the practitioner binds, in their own session, with proof of possession; an administrator can only suspend (AU-07).
- **[Prescribing without safety checks]** → RX-02 refuses every signature until `define-prescribing-safety-checks` is ratified, built and satisfied (ruling 74). Controlled and unknown classifications are refused (ruling 83; CD-20).
- **[Over-privileged users]** → Grants per class and action, recorded by the medical director. `System Manager` confers nothing. Reductions apply at once. Independent attestation, a designator who is never the designee, and visible self-grants (CD-34). The first designation is confirmed by a second person or backed by a recorded licence verification (ruling 98; CD-38, gate §2.6), and listings go to the medical director too. Residual risk: two colluding people. Account provisioning and identity proofing are decided in planning and governed by the constitution.
- **[Billing from unfinished documentation]** → Only final, clinician-signed encounter facts are published, after any co-signature, by reference, with forward-only corrections.
- **[Code-set licensing]** → Nothing bundled; practice-supplied sets; verification before any bundling.
- **[Co-signature backlog]** → Escalation to the medical director, who can narrow grants.
- **[A large first build]** → Five ordered features, each gated, with clinical authority first.
- **[No constitution]** → Planning is blocked until a constitution is adopted.

18. **What "malformed" means in clause 18** (CD-56). **Closed by ruling 188:** content is kept, as entered, as a recommendation; only content the chart cannot read at all is refused; the clinician who adopts it completes the missing parts. How the addition's envelope is treated, as against its content, is CD-56, PROPOSED.
19. **Whether an interoperability reader is "reading the chart"** (CA-13, CD-59). Reading A: it is not, so recommendations and information records stay out of interoperability reads. Reading B: it is, so they must be exposed, marked. FHIR R4 already has fields that could carry the marker in a later version: DocumentReference `docStatus` `preliminary` for an information record, and ServiceRequest `intent` `proposal` for a recommended order. Recommendation: leave them out in this version, and let a later version expose them marked through such fields. Meanwhile, they are left out (CD-59).
20. **Whether a clinical note's content is a decision** (CD-63). **Closed by ruling 187:** "A note is information and only records who wrote it." What follows for the owner's attention, all PROPOSED in CD-65: Reading A: the chart keeps the list, and a note is an act class, so its content is a decision and a nurse's unsigned note is a "not an authorized decision" recommendation. Reading B: clause 9 calls observations and summaries information, so a note's content is information, and only the practitioner's signature on it is a decision. Under reading B, a note kept as information carries no "not an authorized decision" marking: it is marked only "not signed off", goes to no clinician for adoption, and does not record which authority the failed sign-off lacked, so clauses 13, 15 and 16 do not reach it. Its writer is told at once (CD-63), and only note content is kept this way (CD-65). The same holds for every kind of record of information: only content that validates against its kind's closed, published shape is kept as information, and anything else, including content of a kind whose owning change has published no shape yet, is a recommendation. Text inside any record of information never creates, counts as or authorizes a decision, so an order a note or a summary describes exists only once a practitioner signs one off; a note saying "start amoxicillin" changes nothing in the medication record or the prescribing-safety checks (CD-65). What the chart cannot police is free text inside any valid record of information, not only inside a note: a clinician reading it may be misled, though nothing takes effect because of it. This change publishes a minimal closed document shape for summaries, letters and imported records; structured content, and any kind whose owning change has published no shape yet, is entered as a recommendation, and a decline can say it was never a decision and is kept for reference.
21. **Whether recording a completion is a medical decision** (CD-58). **Closed for orders by ruling 187:** "Recording that an order was carried out is a record of work done." Still open for prescriptions, as the owner was told with ruling 187: for a prescription a completion would take the drug out of the prescribing-safety checks with no sign-off, and nothing in this version records a dispense or administration to show it was carried out. Recommendation: a prescription is never completed in this version and ends only by a signed discontinuation, or, later, its completion is a sign-off (CD-58, PROPOSED). Meanwhile, a prescription is never completed.
22. **An AI that a registered writer speaks for, but whose openWallet ID is missing or not covered by the writer's registration** (AN-01, AN-08, CD-61). **Closed by ruling 194** for an AI consult: "Keep it in quarantine, outside the chart, in front of a clinician, and alert the practice to fix the registration. The patient has already been told something, and the doctor should see it. Refuse only when the relay itself cannot be identified." Extending this to every addition for such an AI, an act included, and the minimum every hold outside the chart meets, are CD-61, PROPOSED. This affects `define-ai-authored-clinical-records`.
23. **Whether a sign-off of a draft whose act type is not in force is refused rather than entered as a recommendation** (CA-09, AN-14, CD-51). The act type is checked first, before authority, and the sign-off is refused for any drafter; the draft stays a draft. By this change's own taxonomy a type not in force is `no_decision_authority`, and clause 13 says a decision made without authority is still entered in the chart. Against that: the draft is not an addition, so the refusal refuses no addition (clause 18), nothing the author wrote is lost, and such a recommendation could never be adopted while the type is out of force. Recommendation: keep the refusal. Meanwhile, the refusal applies.
24. **An AI named by its openWallet ID** (AN-02; `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` AT-16). **Closed by ruling 183:** "a wallet ID may name an AI as the author of a record. It still never identifies a patient or a person, and nothing needs the wallet to be reachable to read the chart." The clarifying change to openWallet's specification is in openWallet-spec change `add-practitioner-profile-and-licence-verification` (draft pull request #10, read at `4cfef0a`), unratified.
25. **Closing an inbox item with no reply** (AU-06, CD-50). Ruling 158 names taking and reassigning as housekeeping. Recommendation: a close that follows a signed-off reply or a re-route is housekeeping; a close that ends an item with no reply records a clinical judgment and needs the sign-off. The same holds for withdrawing a clinical-need marker before its `valid_until`: recommendation, it needs the sign-off, because it narrows access to care; otherwise the marker ends on its `valid_until`.
26. **How a practitioner signs before a phone can make the proof** (EN-06, CD-80). No phone or browser authenticator can make openWallet's ratified `wallet-challenge-proof`, and the successor proof profile openWallet's draft names is not drafted. Recommendation: authorize the successor profile as its own openWallet change; until it is ratified, EN-06's phone scenario is not met and nothing signed on a phone is a decision. A workstation-only version 1 would be a new decision.
27. **For counsel: must the patient's location at the time of a decision match the licence's jurisdiction?** (CD-78). Recommendation: counsel decides. Meanwhile openChart checks that the grant's jurisdiction is one the practice has recorded as a place it practises, and does not check the patient's location.
28. **For counsel: should openChart later ship limits by jurisdiction and licence class?** (CD-78). Recommendation: no, until counsel advises; the scope a licence covers is the medical director's grant (ruling 92; ruling 80, clause 11).
29. **Does ruling 80, clause 21 reach what an AI adds as information?** (CD-74). Reading A: everything an AI adds through a writing path, information included, is subject to that path's consent check, the medical director's pause and narrowing, and clinician review; clause 10 says what information needs in order to be added and exempts nothing. Reading B: clause 10 alone governs information, and clause 21 reaches only what an AI told a patient. Recommendation: reading A, which the AI write path now takes. Meanwhile, reading A applies.
30. **Holding, never refusing, a write whose AI is not identified, and a write from a party the path does not cover** (CD-70, CD-71). Ruling 194 decides an AI that is not registered. Recommendation: hold both, as AN-01 now says; a hold for an AI that is not identified is never released, and its content reaches the chart only through a corrected write or a clinician's own record.
