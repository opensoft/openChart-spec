## Why

The practice's medical director will set an external clinical AI system to one of three modes: advice only (the default), limited practice, or full practice (ruling 59). Its exchanges with patients are recorded in the chart through that system's own governed path, never through openPractice (ruling 53). Every consult must be in the chart for the patient's doctor (ruling 57). And each mode stays off until the medical director has approved its scope, a legal and regulatory review covering that mode is on record, and, for the two practice modes, the chart can accept an AI-authored order, prescription or diagnosis recorded with the authority it acted under (rulings 58 to 62).

On 2026-11-15 the owner narrowed the scope. Advice only is a patient-consult class: information, an assessment and next steps, with no diagnosis language and no treatment directives (ruling 66). The ratified texts stand, and the practice modes stay specified but off, to be revisited only through a future governed amendment after advice-only has run and counsel's review is in (ruling 67). Tier 1 is therefore the only tier in scope to build.

That last condition is this change. openChart is the doctor's clinical interface, on mobile or workstation (ruling 35). Its built code holds patient intake only: `OC Patient`, `OC Patient External Identifier`, `OC Intake Submission` and eight patient-reported statement families. There are no clinician encounters, orders, prescriptions or diagnoses in openChart yet, and nothing records AI-authored content.

Two draft openChart changes need this one. `define-clinical-message-inbox` (not yet on main) holds all AI-authored content as communication only until this change is ratified (its MI-26), and leaves openChart's receiving side of the AI system's governed chart path to a separate change (its Open Question 4). `define-openpractice-boundary` (not yet on main) waits for a clinical-encounter capability (its Open Question 2).

This change is neutral. It speaks of "an external clinical AI system operating under a declared authority". openChart must still operate with no such system present. The Medx-specific binding is a separate private companion.

## What Changes

- **A versioned write path.** openChart accepts AI-authored records only through `openchart-ai-clinical-record-write/1.0`, a canonical JSON schema owned by this change and pinned by SHA-256 in `contracts/SHA256SUMS`. Writes are idempotent, every write gets a receipt, the writing system can read back what the chart stored by digest, and no PHI appears in errors, logs or notifications (AW-01 to AW-12).
- **openChart's own record of authority (ruling 65).** The chart keeps its own record of the medical director's authority grant: practice, mode, configuration version, allowlist entries or the full-practice grant, the approving medical director, the recorded legal and regulatory review for that mode, expiry, review policy and co-review window. It checks every write against that record and never trusts the AI system's claim. It fails closed on anything missing, expired, paused, narrowed or mismatched. Only the medical director, a named human, approves, narrows, pauses or resumes. A reduction of authority from any source applies at once; a widening is only ever the medical director's act in openChart (AG-01 to AG-10).
- **Tier 1: AI consult records (buildable now).** The chart records each consult as the full exchange with an optional, labelled AI summary (design call AD-2). The record carries the model and version identifiers, the evidence references, the mode in force and the authority. AI authorship is always distinguishable from clinician-authored and patient-reported content. A consult record is AI-generated communication: never a diagnosis and never a clinical fact of record. In advice-only mode its AI content gives information, an assessment and next steps, with no diagnosis language and no treatment directives (ruling 66), and every display of it carries a label saying so (AC-04). It is visible to the patient's doctor, and it supports the medical director's review sample and always-review categories, with review status, reviewer and outcome. It cites the clinical communication record by requirement ID, MI-12 of `define-clinical-message-inbox` (openChart-spec#3), and defines no second one; it defines no conversation retrieval either (ruling 69) (AC-01 to AC-10).
- **Tier 2: AI-authored clinical acts (specified, refused, not in scope to build).** For orders, prescriptions, diagnoses of record and treatment-plan changes under limited or full practice, this change specifies the authorship, authority, co-review and reversal layer that applies to any act type. An act is accepted only under a current grant. The reserved list is refused whatever mode is claimed, unless the grant's recorded legal review cleared that act. Emergency management by an AI is always refused. Every accepted act enters a clinician co-review queue with a window the medical director sets; the clinician confirms, amends or voids it, and an overdue item escalates to the medical director. A clinician can void or countermand an AI act. An AI act never overwrites or supersedes clinician-authored content. On narrowing or pause, pending AI acts are held and handed to a clinician, or cancelled, and never executed (AA-01 to AA-12).
- **Tier 2 is blocked.** Tier 2 needs both the required prerequisite change, the clinical-acts foundation (being drafted, ruling 72), and a future governed amendment (ruling 67). Until then every `act.write` is refused with `tier2_not_in_force`, and `define-clinical-message-inbox`'s MI-26 hold continues in substance.
- **Sources.** A write's source token is MI-12's token for the source system where there is one. `healthlinc`, `openpractice_agent` and `openchart` are refused. A relay may write for one source system, as ruling 70 provides for the medical AI's records (AW-02).
- **Isolation and labels.** Only operational writes are recorded; a scenario or simulation write is refused and stores nothing. Every AI-authored record states the authority it rested on, and every display of it, the patient's included, labels AI authorship.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `ai-write-path` (AW-01 to AW-12): the contract, integration identity, patient resolution, context isolation, idempotency, receipts, no-PHI rule, durable notification delivery, AI-authorship labels, standalone operation, contract tests and readback by digest.
- `ai-authority-grants` (AG-01 to AG-10): openChart's own record of the medical director's authority grant and its gates.
- `ai-consult-records` (AC-01 to AC-10): Tier 1.
- `ai-clinical-acts` (AA-01 to AA-12): Tier 2. Specified and refused; not buildable until the clinical-acts foundation is ratified and a future governed amendment puts it in force (ruling 67).

### Modified Capabilities

None. The ratified `expanded-patient-intake` requirements are cited and left unchanged. Two conflicts with them are recorded as tensions AT-1 and AT-2 in `design.md`; under ruling 67 the ratified text stands and Tier 2 stays refused.

## Required prerequisite change (being drafted)

**`define-clinical-acts-foundation`**, being drafted by another author (ruling 72). This change does not edit it. It must give openChart clinician encounters, orders, prescriptions, diagnoses of record and treatment plans, with the minimum contents listed in `design.md` under "Required prerequisite change". Tier 2 is not buildable, and neither practice mode can be enabled, until it is ratified and built and a future governed amendment puts Tier 2 in force (ruling 67).

## Impact

- **openChart:** a write-path integration role per AI system; an authority-grant record, a legal-review record and a review policy, all approved by a named medical director; consult records on the clinical-communication record; review workflow; and, later, the Tier 2 layer over the foundation's acts. Every new component is optional.
- **Dependencies:**
  - `define-clinical-message-inbox` (openChart-spec#3, not yet on main): Tier 1 cites its clinical communication record, MI-12, by requirement ID. Its `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0` (ruling 69) is not redefined here. Its MI-26 and Open Question 4 are answered here (tensions AT-5 and AT-6).
  - `define-openpractice-boundary` (not yet on main): its durable outbound pattern (PL-14) and no-PHI rule (PL-13) are reused; CF-02, CF-05 and CF-06 are unchanged.
  - `define-clinical-acts-foundation` (required, being drafted, ruling 72): Tier 2 waits for it and for a future governed amendment (ruling 67).
  - Cross-references: `MedxSoft/medXfactory` change `define-patient-medical-ai-connection` (drafted, not yet on main) owns the medical-AI mode framework and lists this change as a required companion; `MedxSoft/MedxEHR-spec` change `define-medical-ai-record-overlay` is the Medx-specific overlay on this contract.
- **Governance:** openChart's constitution is still the unfilled template. A Constitution Check, and with it the plan phase, needs a constitution adopted through separate governance first.
- **Safety:** every mode off by default; synthetic fixtures only; no PHI in logs, errors, receipts or notifications; no AI act without a current grant, a recorded legal review and clinician co-review; no emergency management by an AI.
- **Tensions:** ten are recorded in `design.md` as AT-1 to AT-10. Ruling 67 disposes the two conflicts with ratified openChart text (AT-1, AT-2): the ratified text stands. Each tension's disposition is in `design.md`.
