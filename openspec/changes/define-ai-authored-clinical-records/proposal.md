## Why

The practice's medical director will set an external clinical AI system to one of three modes: advice only (the default), limited practice, or full practice (ruling 59). Its exchanges with patients are recorded in the chart through that system's own governed path, never through openPractice (ruling 53). Every consult must be in the chart for the patient's doctor (ruling 57). And each mode stays off until the medical director has approved its scope, a legal and regulatory review covering that mode is on record, and, for the two practice modes, the chart can accept an AI-authored order, prescription or diagnosis recorded with the authority it acted under (rulings 58 to 62).

On 2026-11-15 the owner narrowed the scope. Advice only is a patient-consult class: information, an assessment and next steps, with no diagnosis language and no treatment directives (ruling 66), whose next steps mean how to get care and general self-care information, never a treatment instruction for the patient's own condition (ruling 81). The ratified texts stand, and the practice modes stay specified but off, to be revisited only through a future governed amendment after advice-only has run and counsel's review is in (ruling 67). Tier 1 is therefore the only tier in scope to build.

Later the same day the owner ruled that any human or system the chart can identify may add to a patient's chart; that every addition records when it was received, where it came from, what added it and on whose behalf; and that a decision made without authority is still entered, as a recommendation marked "not an authorized decision" that has no effect. Missing authority is never a reason to refuse an addition (ruling 80). He added that every medical decision needs the practitioner's sign-off, made by a logged-in user with their openWallet and openSig (ruling 151). For this change that means every write identifies the AI by its openWallet ID, and an AI act attempted without authority, which today is every act, is recorded as a recommendation instead of being refused.

The owner then accepted decisions 152 to 194 as rulings. For this change: an AI only ever makes recommendations until a practitioner signs, so limited and full practice as designed need a new ruling (ruling 157); a consult review needs the practitioner's sign-off, and no decision is real until openWallet and openSig can do their part (rulings 158 and 152); only content the chart cannot read at all is refused (ruling 188); and a consult whose AI's openWallet ID is missing or not registered for its relay is kept in quarantine outside the chart, in front of a clinician, and refused only when the relay itself cannot be identified (ruling 194).

That last condition is this change. openChart is the doctor's clinical interface, on mobile or workstation (ruling 35). Its built code holds patient intake only: `OC Patient`, `OC Patient External Identifier`, `OC Intake Submission` and eight patient-reported statement families. There are no clinician encounters, orders, prescriptions or diagnoses in openChart yet, and nothing records AI-authored content.

Two draft openChart changes need this one. `define-clinical-message-inbox` (not yet on main) holds all AI-authored content as communication only until this change is ratified (its MI-26), and leaves openChart's receiving side of the AI system's governed chart path to a separate change (its Open Question 4). `define-openpractice-boundary` (not yet on main) waits for a clinical-encounter capability (its Open Question 2).

This change is neutral. It speaks of "an external clinical AI system operating under a declared authority". openChart must still operate with no such system present. The Medx-specific binding is a separate private companion.

## What Changes

- **A versioned write path.** openChart accepts AI-authored records only through `openchart-ai-clinical-record-write/1.0`, a canonical JSON schema owned by this change and pinned by SHA-256 in `contracts/SHA256SUMS`. Writes are idempotent, every write gets a receipt, the writing system can read back what the chart stored by digest, and no PHI appears in errors, logs or notifications. Every write names its writer and the AI on whose behalf it writes, by its openWallet ID with the model and model version, and every record kept carries when it was received, when it arose and where it came from, permanently (ruling 80). A write is refused only on a ground that ruling 80, clause 18 keeps: a writer that cannot be identified, is not registered, is suspended or writes for another practice, content the chart cannot read at all (ruling 188) or declared test data, or a patient that cannot be matched. Missing authority never refuses a write, a declaration a write leaves out is recorded as absent, and a write whose AI or source system the writer's registration does not cover is held in quarantine outside the chart, in front of a clinician, with its ending reported to the source (ruling 194; AW-01 to AW-16).
- **openChart's own record of authority (ruling 65).** The chart keeps its own record of the medical director's authority grant: practice, mode, configuration version, allowlist entries or the full-practice grant, the approving medical director, the recorded legal and regulatory review for that mode, expiry, review policy and co-review window. It checks every write against that record and never trusts the AI system's claim. It fails closed on anything missing, expired, paused, narrowed or mismatched. Only the medical director, as `define-clinical-acts-foundation` AU-01 designates (the single definition), approves, narrows, pauses or resumes. A reduction of authority from any source applies at once; a widening is only ever the medical director's act in openChart (AG-01 to AG-10).
- **Tier 1: AI consult records (the only tier in scope to build).** The chart records each consult as the full exchange with an optional, labelled AI summary (design call AD-2). The record carries the model and version identifiers, the evidence references, the mode in force and the authority. AI authorship is always distinguishable from clinician-authored and patient-reported content. A consult record is AI-generated communication: never a diagnosis and never a clinical fact of record. In advice-only mode its AI content gives information, an assessment and next steps, with no diagnosis language and no treatment directives (ruling 66), its next steps meaning how to get care and general self-care information, never a treatment instruction for the patient's own condition (ruling 81), and every display of it carries a label saying so (AC-04). It is visible to the patient's doctor, and it supports the medical director's review sample and always-review categories, with review status, reviewer and outcome. A review is recorded only by a named clinician holding a consult review grant (AG-11), signed off with their openWallet and openSig (ruling 158), and no mode is switched on for patients until such a sign-off can be made (AG-04). A consult that fails a grant check, mismatches included, or carries no consent reference, is quarantined in the chart (ruling 90) and answered `quarantined`, labelled with its coded cause, reviewed within a fixed window that needs no grant, listed for the responsible clinician or, failing that, every clinician authorized for the chart, and never accepted (AC-11). An integration identity can be suspended manually or deregistered at once; a per-source cap refuses nothing, but folds a flooding source's listings into a digest and alerts the medical director (AW-13). Every change to the source's standing is announced to it, under a 15-minute notice clock, and the source can read back its own authority as a content-free heartbeat (AW-08, AW-13, AW-14). It cites the clinical communication record by requirement ID, MI-12 of `define-clinical-message-inbox` (openChart-spec#3), and defines no second one; it defines no conversation retrieval either (ruling 69) (AC-01 to AC-10).
- **Tier 2: AI-authored clinical acts (specified, out of force, not in scope to build).** For orders, prescriptions, diagnoses of record and treatment-plan changes under limited or full practice, this change specifies the authorship, authority, co-review and reversal layer that applies to any act type. An act is accepted only under a current grant. An AI prescription is also accepted only on an `open` prescribing-safety gate decision for its exact content, never on the outage path (`define-prescribing-safety-checks` PG-01, ruling 76). The reserved list is never accepted from an AI, whatever mode is claimed, unless the grant's recorded legal review cleared that act. An AI never manages an emergency. An act that fails any authority check is recorded in full as a recommendation, marked "not an authorized decision", with no effect, and listed with its content for a clinician who may adopt it, by a sign-off of their own (ruling 151), or decline it (ruling 80; AW-16). Every accepted act enters a clinician co-review queue with a window the medical director sets; the clinician confirms, amends or voids it, and an overdue item escalates to the medical director. A clinician can void or countermand an AI act. An AI act never overwrites or supersedes clinician-authored content. On narrowing or pause, pending (`pending_release`) AI acts are held and handed to a clinician, or cancelled, and never released (AA-01 to AA-12).
- **Tier 2 is blocked.** Tier 2 needs both the required prerequisite change, the clinical-acts foundation (drafted as openChart-spec#5, ruling 72), and a future governed amendment (ruling 67). Until then every `act.write` is recorded as a recommendation with `tier2_not_in_force`, and `define-clinical-message-inbox`'s MI-26 hold continues in substance. Ruling 157 adds that an AI only ever makes recommendations until a practitioner signs, so limited and full practice as designed also need a new ruling.
- **Sources.** A write's source token is MI-12's token for the source system where there is one. `healthlinc`, `openpractice_agent` and `openchart` are refused. A relay may write for one source system; it is then the writer, and the write names the AI and the source system on whose behalf it writes. A source whose owner has ruled that it writes only through a relay is registered relay-only, and a direct identity for it is refused (AW-02; rulings 70 and 80).
- **Isolation and labels.** Only operational writes are recorded; a scenario or simulation write is refused and stores nothing. Every AI-authored record states the authority it rested on, and every display of it, the patient's included, labels AI authorship; a recommendation is marked "not an authorized decision".

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `ai-write-path` (AW-01 to AW-16): the contract, integration identity and the identification of every writer and AI, patient resolution, context isolation, idempotency, receipts, no-PHI rule, durable notification delivery, AI-authorship labels, standalone operation, contract tests, readback by digest, provenance, and the recording of an AI act attempted without authority as a recommendation.
- `ai-authority-grants` (AG-01 to AG-11): openChart's own record of the medical director's authority grant and its gates, and the consult review grant.
- `ai-consult-records` (AC-01 to AC-11): Tier 1, including quarantine.
- `ai-clinical-acts` (AA-01 to AA-12): Tier 2. Specified and out of force, every AI act being recorded as a recommendation meanwhile; not buildable until the clinical-acts foundation is ratified and a future governed amendment puts it in force (ruling 67).

### Modified Capabilities

None. The ratified `expanded-patient-intake` requirements are cited and left unchanged. Two conflicts with them are recorded as tensions AT-1 and AT-2 in `design.md`; under ruling 67 the ratified text stands and Tier 2 stays out of force. A third, AT-14, raised by ruling 80, is open for the owner.

## Required prerequisite change (drafted as openChart-spec#5)

**`define-clinical-acts-foundation`**, drafted by another author as openChart-spec#5 (ruling 72), not yet ratified. This change does not edit it. **Its feature (1), clinical authority (AU-01 to AU-06), is a prerequisite of Tier 1**: the medical director (AG-02) and the consult review grant (AG-11) rest on it. **The same feature carries the general rule for every addition (AN-01 to AN-14), its recommendation record included**, which ruling 80 gives it: the write path cannot be enabled before that record exists (AW-16). This change cites the foundation at its head `d9a9c63` and pins its content contract, `openchart-clinical-act-content/1.0`, at SHA-256 `73a0d983afc571ae3395779be0576d06b4eb2237117d60409e45e41f7374b3dc`. It must give openChart clinician encounters, orders, prescriptions and diagnoses of record, with the minimum contents listed in `design.md` under "Required prerequisite change"; treatment-plan records are left to a later change (ruling 73). **For parts of Tier 2:** a later treatment-plan change (not drafted) is a prerequisite of the `treatment_plan_change` act class, and `define-prescribing-safety-checks` (openChart-spec#6), with its gate `open`, is a prerequisite of any AI prescription (ruling 74). This change asks for no dispense or transmission state; those wait for a later e-prescribing change. Tier 2 is not buildable, and neither practice mode can be enabled, until it is ratified and built and a future governed amendment puts Tier 2 in force (ruling 67).

## Impact

- **openChart:** a write-path integration role per AI system; an authority-grant record, a legal-review record and a review policy, all approved by the medical director (the foundation's AU-01); consult review grants (AG-11); quarantined consults (AC-11); consult records on the clinical-communication record; review workflow; and, later, the Tier 2 layer over the foundation's acts. Every new component is optional.
- **Dependencies:**
  - `define-clinical-message-inbox` (openChart-spec#3, not yet on main): Tier 1 cites its clinical communication record, MI-12, by requirement ID. Its `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0` (ruling 69) is not redefined here. Its MI-26 and Open Question 4 are answered here (tensions AT-5 and AT-6).
  - `define-openpractice-boundary` (not yet on main): its durable outbound pattern (PL-14) and no-PHI rule (PL-13) are reused; CF-02, CF-05 and CF-06 are unchanged.
  - `define-clinical-acts-foundation` (openChart-spec#5, required, ruling 72): Tier 1 waits for its feature (1): clinical roles and authority, and the general rule for additions with its recommendation record, cited at its head `d9a9c63` (AU-01 to AU-07, AN-01 to AN-14, CA-04); Tier 2 waits for it and for a future governed amendment (ruling 67).
  - `define-prescribing-safety-checks` (openChart-spec#6, not yet on main): its PG-01 gate governs the acceptance of any AI prescription (AA-03).
  - A later treatment-plan change (not drafted, ruling 73): a prerequisite of `treatment_plan_change`.
  - Cross-references: `MedxSoft/medXfactory` change `define-patient-medical-ai-connection` (drafted, not yet on main) owns the medical-AI mode framework and lists this change as a required companion; `MedxSoft/MedxEHR-spec` change `define-medical-ai-record-overlay` is the Medx-specific overlay on this contract.
- **Governance:** openChart's constitution is still the unfilled template. A Constitution Check, and with it the plan phase, needs a constitution adopted through separate governance first.
- **Safety:** every mode off by default; synthetic fixtures only; no PHI in logs, errors, receipts or notifications; no AI act without a current grant, a recorded legal review and clinician co-review; no emergency management by an AI; what an AI attempts without authority is recorded as a recommendation that has no effect until a clinician adopts it with a sign-off of their own (rulings 80 and 151).
- **Tensions:** nineteen are recorded in `design.md` as AT-1 to AT-19. Ruling 67 disposes the two conflicts with ratified openChart text (AT-1, AT-2): the ratified text stands. Ruling 84 reads AT-1's sentence narrowly (it bars automatic creation from intake, and an alert to the clinician is not a treatment recommendation); with ruling 81 that disposes AT-1's residual reading for Tier 1, and Tier 2 stays off under ruling 67. Ruling 80 resolves AT-7 and AT-10 in substance and raises AT-14 to AT-17; the foundation's AU-03 answers AT-17 and its AN-09 answers AT-18, which the reconciliation with the foundation raised; the follow-up raises AT-19; ruling 183 answers AT-16. Each tension's disposition is in `design.md`.
