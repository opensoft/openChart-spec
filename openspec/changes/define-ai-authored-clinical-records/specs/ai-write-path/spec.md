## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: AW-01 AI-authored records enter openChart only through the versioned write-path contract
openChart SHALL accept content authored by an external clinical AI system only as messages under contract `openchart-ai-clinical-record-write/1.0`, owned by this change. Its canonical schema is:
- `openspec/changes/define-ai-authored-clinical-records/contracts/openchart-ai-clinical-record-write-1.0.schema.json`, `$id` `urn:openchart:contract:openchart-ai-clinical-record-write:1.0`, at the SHA-256 recorded in this change's `contracts/SHA256SUMS`.

The digest identifies the schema even after archival moves the file. openChart SHALL validate every message it receives or sends under the contract against the schema at that digest. The messages are `consult.write`, `act.write`, `authority.notice`, `write.receipt`, `act.status`, `consult.reviewed`, `record.readback_request`, `record.readback`, `grant.readback_request`, `grant.readback`, `held.ended` and `message.ack`. Implementations are compatible exactly when they share the MAJOR version. openChart SHALL accept a `1.x` message at a MINOR version it has declared, refuse a message whose MAJOR version it does not support with reason `unsupported_major_version`, and refuse a message that fails the schema with reason `schema_invalid`. A refusal SHALL store no clinical content. Only content the chart cannot read at all is refused (ruling 188): of a write the schema requires only its envelope, its writer, its declared context, its patient, its record identifiers and its content object, which is what the foundation's AN-12 calls malformed when it is missing or unreadable. **Apart from the writer, the patient, the declared `context`, the record identifiers and the content object, no value refuses a write (design call AD-74, PROPOSED under rulings 188 and 194).** Every other field of a write is a declaration whose absence takes the outcome the schema and AW-16 state, and never refuses the write: a write that names no AI, or names it incompletely or in a form this path cannot read, is held by AW-06's AI check; one with no `source` is given the source this path implies; and one that gives no time its information arose, or gives one this path cannot read or that is later than its receipt, is given the time of its receipt (AW-15; design calls AD-60, AD-63 and AD-70). A declaration whose value fails its form is set aside: it takes the outcome its absence takes, failing closed; it is kept as given with this change's record of the write, a value openChart could not read: never used or displayed in place of its field, readable on request by anyone who can read the chart, marked as a value openChart could not read, and never in an alert, a notification, a log line, a diagnostic or an acknowledgement (AW-07); and the receipt names it in `set_aside`, by field name only, so that the sender learns of it (AW-06). A member the schema does not define is set aside unread, named `unknown_property`, and raises an integration exception to the administrative role (AW-07). What openChart judges beside the schema, every declaration against its form in `declarationForms`, the AI's identification, the parties of `on_behalf_of`, the times a write gives and the turns of a consult (design call AD-75), the schema accepts as bounded values of any type, so that only a value beyond the bounds is refused, as a write beyond the size bounds (AW-02; design calls AD-60 and AD-74). What is still refused is design call AD-63's closed list, which AW-06 applies. The foundation's AN-12 states this rule for every addition, PROPOSED as a reading of rulings 188 and 194 (its design call CD-56); this change applies it to its path. A breaking change SHALL require a new MAJOR version and a new digest, recorded in this change and in every companion that cites it. Until this change is ratified, the draft 1.0 schema MAY still change, as it did for ruling 80 and again to fit the clinical-acts foundation's recommendation record; each change records a new digest here and in every companion that cites it (design call AD-48). Every rule of the schema SHALL hold whether or not the validator asserts `format`: timestamps and UUIDs are checked by pattern only, and the timestamp pattern is the clinical-acts foundation's own, character for character (its design, section 1), so that no receiver's validator setting changes what is valid. A timestamp that names a day its month does not have, such as 30 February, passes the pattern; openChart SHALL refuse a message other than a write that carries one as malformed, with `schema_invalid`, by a check beside the schema, as the foundation's verifier checks calendar time (its CA-08; design call AD-48). In a write such a time is a time that cannot be read, which never refuses it: openChart replaces it with the time of receipt, or shows a turn's time as not stated (AW-15; design calls AD-70 and AD-75).

#### Scenario: Failure: a write carries a property the schema does not define
- **WHEN** a `consult.write` arrives with a property the schema does not define, such as `diagnosis`
- **THEN** openChart does not refuse it: it sets the property aside unread, records the consult as its other checks decide, names `unknown_property` in the receipt's `set_aside`, and raises an integration exception to the administrative role that carries nothing taken from the write

#### Scenario: Failure: a declaration is outside its form
- **WHEN** a registered relay sends a `consult.write` whose `models` name a model identifier with a space, and whose `emergency_indicated` is the string `yes`
- **THEN** openChart does not refuse it: it records the consult showing the AI's model and model version from `on_behalf_of` only, counts it as an emergency, shown as defaulted, lists it urgently and selects it for review, keeps both values as given with its record of the write, uses and displays neither in place of its field, lets anyone who can read the chart read them on request, marked as values openChart could not read, puts neither in an alert, a notification, a log line, a diagnostic or an acknowledgement, and answers with `set_aside` naming `emergency_indicated` and `models`

#### Scenario: Failure: a write is beyond the size bounds
- **WHEN** a `consult.write` carries a `summary` whose text is longer than 20000 characters
- **THEN** openChart refuses it with reason `schema_invalid` and stores no clinical content, because a value beyond the bounds is not a value written wrong but something else in its place

#### Scenario: Failure: an act write omits every declaration
- **WHEN** a registered relay sends an `act.write` with its envelope, parties, provenance, patient, identifiers, act class and content, and no `act_type`, `authority`, `evidence_refs`, `declared_reserved`, `emergency_indicated` or `consent_ref`
- **THEN** openChart does not refuse it: it records a recommendation with `path_reason` `tier2_not_in_force`, shows `emergency_indicated` and `declared_reserved` as defaulted to true, lists it urgently, and marks that it rests on no evidence reference

#### Scenario: Failure: an unsupported major version arrives
- **WHEN** a `consult.write` with `contract_version` `2.0` arrives
- **THEN** openChart refuses it with reason `unsupported_major_version` and stores no clinical content

### Requirement: AW-02 Only a registered writer may write, and every write names its writer, the AI on whose behalf it writes and the source system
openChart SHALL accept write-path messages only from an authenticated integration identity that is registered for exactly one external clinical AI system, identified by its `source_system` (issuer and namespace), and exactly one practice. The identity SHALL hold a dedicated write-path integration role that is narrower than `System Manager`, is named in planning, and is authorized for subject, tenant, purpose and role, as the ratified API requirement demands. Registering an AI system SHALL be an audited act by a named holder of the practice's administrative role that the foundation's AU-01 names (ruling 159), and SHALL be refused while no AU-01 designation of the foundation is in force for the practice. The registration SHALL record the writer's system identity and its operator, and the openWallet ID of each AI that the source system may write for (ruling 80, clause 3 and part B; design call AD-49). No AI system and no agent SHALL register, widen or re-scope an integration identity, or add an openWallet ID to a registration. Removing an openWallet ID from a registration SHALL be audited and effective at once (ruling 80, clause 3). This requirement applies the foundation's AN-01 to AN-03 (`define-clinical-acts-foundation` at head `9408e11`) to the write path: an addition is refused, with nothing stored, only for a reason that concerns its writer (it cannot be identified, it is not registered, or it is suspended), each kind of party is identified in its own recorded way, and every system is registered with the practice before it adds.

**What every write names (ruling 80, clauses 2, 6 and 7).** Every `consult.write` and `act.write` SHALL name:
- `added_by`: the writer, which is the registered integration identity that delivers the write, by its system identity (issuer and namespace) and its operator, `operator_ref`;
- `on_behalf_of`: every party the write passed through before the writer, in order from the originator to the writer. The first is the AI that produced the content, identified by its `openwallet_id`, with the `model` and `model_version` that produced it; a write that names no AI, names it without any of these or with a value outside its form, or whose `on_behalf_of` cannot be read in the form this paragraph gives, is held by the AI check of AW-06, never refused (ruling 194; design call AD-60). When the writer is a relay, the second is the source system, by its system identity and operator, and its system identity equals the write's `source_system`. When the writer is the source system's own identity, the AI is the only entry. The 1.0 contract admits no other party in `on_behalf_of`, and no human.

openChart SHALL refuse with `unauthorized`, at step 2 of AW-06, and store no clinical content, only a write whose writer it cannot identify or that writes for another practice: a write from an identity that is not registered, whose `added_by` is not the registration of the identity that sent it, or whose `practice_ref` is not the practice that registration is for (ruling 80, clauses 2 and 3; the foundation's AN-01 and AN-03). Ruling 194: "Refuse only when the relay itself cannot be identified." A write whose writer is identified and registered for the practice SHALL NOT be refused because of a party it names. When its `on_behalf_of` is missing or cannot be read in the form above, or names its AI without an `openwallet_id`, `model` or `model_version`, or with a value outside its form, or with an `openwallet_id` that the writer's registration does not cover, or when the source system it names is one that registration does not cover, openChart SHALL hold it in quarantine outside the chart, in front of a clinician, and alert the practice's administrative role (the AI check of AW-06; AC-11; ruling 194; design call AD-60; for an uncovered source system, the foundation's CD-71 at `9408e11`). **What the AI check reads (design call AD-60, PROPOSED).** An `on_behalf_of` that cannot be read in the form above is not malformed: an empty list, parties out of order, a human, two AIs, more than two parties, a party with a property or a `kind` this path does not read, or a system other than the write's source system. The AI check holds such a write with `ai_not_identified`. Ruling 80, clause 7 makes the order of hands meaningful, so openChart never reorders the parties and never guesses which of them is the AI; it keeps them as they arrived, and keeps their values out of every alert (AW-07). A value in `openwallet_id` that does not have the identifier shape of openWallet's wallet record, 1 to 200 characters, a letter or digit first, then letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `:`, `/` or `-`, is not an openWallet ID; a `model` outside the pattern of a model identifier, 1 to 128 letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `:`, `/`, `@`, `+` or `-`, or a `model_version` outside that of a version label, 1 to 64 letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `:`, `+` or `-`, does not identify the model that produced the content. The AI check holds a write with any of these with `ai_not_identified`; the schema's `aiParty` is the form it reads. **The bound.** The schema accepts `on_behalf_of` as any bounded value, a list of at most 16 parties as given among them (`onBehalfOf`); each party is a string of at most 1024 characters, a list of at most 16 values, or a JSON object of at most 16 members, each named in at most 1024 characters, whose values are strings, numbers, booleans or null, or objects or lists of at most 16 of those, every string at most 1024 characters (`partyAsGiven`). 1024 is five times the longest identification the AI check admits, an openWallet ID of 200 characters, so a value that is merely written wrong is held, never refused. An `on_behalf_of` that is not a list, and a party that is not an object, cannot be read in the form above, and the AI check holds them with `ai_not_identified` (design call AD-74). Only a list, a party or a value beyond these bounds is refused, with `schema_invalid`, as a write beyond the size bounds (design call AD-63). openChart compares an openWallet ID exactly with the registration. It names the AI as the author of a record: it never identifies a person or a patient, openChart resolves no subject by it, and nothing needs the wallet to be reachable to read the chart (ruling 183; the foundation's AN-02; design call AD-49). openChart SHALL record the writer as identified by the chart itself (`identity_basis` `verified_by_chart`), and the AI and the source system as asserted by the writer (`asserted_by`), because only the writer authenticates to openChart. The `model` and `model_version` are recorded as the writer states them, in their form (design call AD-49; Open Question 12).

**Relays.** The integration identity MAY be held by a relay that delivers one source system's writes and returns its receipts, as ruling 70 provides for the medical AI's records. The relay SHALL be registered for that one source system and practice. It is the writer: each write it delivers names it in `added_by`, names the source system in `source_system` and second in `on_behalf_of`, and names the AI first (ruling 80, clause 7). The registration SHALL record whether the source is relay-only. A source whose owner has ruled that it writes only through a relay SHALL be registered relay-only, by the named human, citing that ruling. For a relay-only source, openChart SHALL register only the relay's integration identity and SHALL refuse to register a direct identity for it. A write naming it that does not come from its relay SHALL NOT be accepted: from an unregistered identity it is refused with `unauthorized`, and from an identity registered for another source it is held with `source_system_not_covered`, because that registration does not cover the source (ruling 194). A source system's namespace SHALL be the source token that MI-12 of `define-clinical-message-inbox` uses for it, where MI-12 defines one. `healthlinc` and `openpractice_agent` SHALL NOT be write-path systems, whether as the source, the writer or any party named on a write, and `openchart` SHALL NOT be a system namespace. The schema refuses all three as the writer. A write that names one as its source system, or in `on_behalf_of`, is not refused for that: its patient link is resolved in the namespace it names (AW-03), and the AI check holds it with `ai_not_identified`, because no relay's `on_behalf_of` can name such a system as the source system in the form above, and it is never released (design calls AD-30, AD-49, AD-60 and AD-74).

### Requirement: AW-03 A write resolves the patient only through an existing link
openChart SHALL resolve the patient of a write only through links that already exist. The write SHALL name the openChart patient triple (openChart issuer, `openchart`, `OC Patient` identifier) and the source system's reference for that patient on its link with openChart. That reference need not be, and should not be, the source system's internal patient identifier; a value minted for this one link suffices. openChart SHALL accept the write only when an `active` `OC Patient External Identifier` row on that `OC Patient` carries the source system's namespace, issuer and value. A `source_patient_ref` whose issuer and namespace are not the write's `source_system`'s matches no row, so its patient cannot be matched (`patient_link_unknown`). The link is registered through the ratified external-identifier path or a later governed link contract (design call AD-23).

openChart MUST NOT match on names, dates of birth or other demographics. It MUST NOT create, change, merge or re-point a patient or a link while handling a write. The source system's reference MUST NOT replace the openChart patient identifier. This restates the ratified `expanded-patient-intake` rule on external identifiers and does not modify it.

#### Scenario: Failure: the references name different patients
- **WHEN** a write names `OC-PAT-00001`, but the active row for the source system's reference belongs to another `OC Patient`
- **THEN** openChart refuses it with reason `patient_link_mismatch`, stores no clinical content, and records an integration exception holding only identifiers and a coded cause for identity review (design call AD-26)

#### Scenario: Failure: the link is in identity review
- **WHEN** the source system's identifier row on the named patient has status `identity_review`
- **THEN** openChart refuses the write with reason `patient_link_in_review` and changes no identifier

#### Scenario: Failure: no link exists
- **WHEN** no row for the source system's reference exists
- **THEN** openChart refuses the write with reason `patient_link_unknown` and creates no patient or link

### Requirement: AW-04 Only operational writes are recorded; scenario and simulation content never reaches operational records
openChart SHALL record a write only when its `context` is `operational`. A write whose `context` is `scenario` or `simulation` SHALL be refused with reason `non_operational_context` and SHALL store no clinical content (design call AD-18). No operational record SHALL reference a scenario or simulation output as its content, its evidence target or its authority. A refused `scenario` or `simulation` write SHALL never be listed urgently, whatever flags it carries; its refusal record keeps them (AW-06).

#### Scenario: Failure: a scenario write carries the emergency indicator
- **WHEN** a schema-valid `consult.write` with `context` `scenario` and `emergency_indicated` true names a linked patient
- **THEN** openChart refuses it with reason `non_operational_context`, the refusal record keeps the flag, and nothing is listed for that patient's clinicians

#### Scenario: Failure: a scenario consult reaches the write path
- **WHEN** a schema-valid `consult.write` with `context` `scenario` arrives
- **THEN** openChart refuses it with reason `non_operational_context` and creates no record

### Requirement: AW-05 Writes are idempotent and replay-safe
The `message_id` of each inbound message SHALL be its idempotency key. Idempotency and duplicate detection SHALL be scoped to the authenticated integration identity, that is, to its practice and source system (AW-02): a `message_id`, a `consult_id` and an `act_id` are matched only against messages and records from the same practice and source system, never across integrations. openChart SHALL keep, for each `message_id` it has processed, the digest of that message: the SHA-256 of its RFC 8785 canonical JSON without `sent_at`. A message whose `message_id` was already processed with the same digest is a retry: it SHALL be answered with the original receipt and SHALL change nothing. A message that reuses a `message_id` with a different digest SHALL NOT be answered with the original receipt and SHALL NOT be dropped: openChart SHALL process it through AW-06 as a new message, flag what it keeps `message_id_conflict`, with the first message's digest and, where the first produced a record, that record, and say so in its receipt (design call AD-66). Refusing it as malformed was considered and rejected: a different message under a reused key is still readable (ruling 188). A write that repeats a `consult_id` and `consult_version`, or an `act_id` and `act_version`, under a new `message_id` with the same content digest SHALL be answered `duplicate` with the first submission's result: `first_result`, the first `record_ref` and its codes, a quarantined write's `quarantine_cause` or a recommendation's `missing_authority` and `path_reason`. It SHALL add no copy and change nothing (the foundation's AN-10). **A corrected resend (design call AD-71, PROPOSED).** A write held with `openwallet_id_missing` or `ai_not_identified` can never be released (AC-11), so a resend that corrects its AI's identification is not a duplicate of it: a write from the same practice and source system that names the same record, by the same identifiers or a later version of them, with the same content digest as such a held write, and whose `on_behalf_of` differs from the held write's, the AI's identification included, SHALL be checked as a new write through the rest of AW-06 and kept as whatever that makes it. openChart SHALL link it to the held write and flag the held write as having a corrected write, naming the new record, so that a clinician can dismiss the held write as `duplicate_of_existing_record`, naming that record (AC-11). Nothing else about the held write changes: it is never edited or released, and only a clinician ends it. A resend whose `on_behalf_of` is the held write's own is a duplicate as above. The same identifiers with a different content digest SHALL NOT be refused. openChart SHALL check the write as a new addition through the rest of AW-06, keep it as whatever that makes it, and flag it `submission_ref_conflict`, naming the first record's `record_ref` and stored content digest. It SHALL tell the writer at once, in a receipt that carries the same flag. Both records stay, and the first is never rewritten, because a record is permanent and a correction is a new dated addition (ruling 80, clause 8; the foundation's AN-10 and design call CD-64; design call AD-61). A later repeat identical to either record is answered `duplicate` with that record's own result and `record_ref`, and, for the record kept under the flag, with its `submission_ref_conflict` again; a repeat with yet another content digest is kept as another record, flagged against the first. openChart SHALL also list the conflict for the medical director as an integration exception that holds only identifiers and a coded cause (design call AD-26). A quarantined consult, a held write (AC-11) and a recommendation (AW-16) are records for these rules. Nothing the AI system or its relay sends turns a quarantined consult or a held write into an accepted one, or a recommendation into a decision (ruling 151). The `message_id` of every message openChart sends SHALL be a random UUID, reused on every retry. No key SHALL carry PHI.

#### Scenario: Failure: a write is retried three times
- **WHEN** the same `consult.write` arrives three times with the same `message_id`
- **THEN** openChart holds one consult record and answers with the same receipt each time

#### Scenario: Failure: a message_id is reused for different content
- **WHEN** a write arrives whose `message_id` openChart processed earlier for a message with a different digest
- **THEN** openChart does not answer with the earlier receipt and drops nothing: it processes the write as a new message, keeps what it records flagged `message_id_conflict` with the first message's digest, and answers with that flag

#### Scenario: Failure: a consult version is rewritten with different content
- **WHEN** a second write for an existing `consult_id` and `consult_version` carries a different exchange
- **THEN** openChart does not refuse it: it checks the second write as a new addition, keeps it as a new record flagged `submission_ref_conflict` with the first record and its digest, answers with that flag, leaves the first record unchanged, and lists an integration exception holding only identifiers and the coded cause

#### Scenario: Failure: two integrations use the same consult identifier
- **WHEN** two practices' AI systems each write a consult with `consult_id` `SYN-CONSULT-0001` and `consult_version` 1, with different content
- **THEN** openChart records both, each under its own practice and source system, and answers neither as `duplicate` nor flags either as a conflict

#### Scenario: Failure: a recommendation is re-sent
- **WHEN** an `act.write` was recorded as a recommendation, and the AI system re-sends the same `act_id`, `act_version` and content under a new `message_id`

### Requirement: AW-06 Every write and every notice gets exactly one receipt; only an identification, integrity, context or patient-link failure, or a suspended writer, refuses a write, and missing authority never does
openChart SHALL answer every `consult.write`, `act.write` and `authority.notice` it receives with exactly one `write.receipt`, with one of these results:
- `accepted`, with openChart's `record_ref`;
- `duplicate`, for a repeat of a write already recorded, with the first submission's result as `first_result`, its `record_ref` and its codes (AW-05; the foundation's AN-10);
- `recorded_as_recommendation`, only for an `act.write` recorded under AW-16, with the `record_ref`, which is the recommendation's identifier, the foundation's `missing_authority` codes and this contract's `path_reason`, as below, and the flag `consent_ref_missing` when the act carried no consent reference (the foundation's AN-10; design call AD-62);
- `kept_as_information`, the foundation's own receipt code for a record of information, only for an `act.write` declared `clinical_note` or `document` whose content validates as the foundation's note content or document shape and that passes step 8, kept as the foundation's information record (AW-16; the foundation's AN-06 and AN-14; design calls AD-68 and AD-69);
- `quarantined`, for a `consult.write`, or an `act.write` whose content is information, quarantined in the chart under AC-11, or for any write held outside the chart by the AI check below, with the `record_ref` and the `quarantine_cause` code. For a held write, `quarantined` is this path's `held_in_quarantine` (the foundation's AN-01);
- `refused`, with one reason code from the schema and no `record_ref`.

A receipt for a write kept under AW-05's `submission_ref_conflict`, or for a message processed under its `message_id_conflict`, carries that flag. A receipt for a write that set a field aside names each such field in `set_aside`, by field name only, from the closed list `setAsideField`, a member the schema does not define as `unknown_property`, and never the value given; a refused write, which stored nothing, and a notice set nothing aside (design call AD-74, PROPOSED). In 1.0 no receipt says that an act took effect: an act is never `accepted`, nor a duplicate of an accepted act, and no receipt carries a co-review due time (ruling 157). openChart SHALL durably record every receipt it sends. An `authority.notice` is never recorded as a recommendation or quarantined.

When more than one check fails, openChart SHALL check in this order and act on the first that fails (design call AD-29). Steps 1, 2 and 4 to 6 refuse the write, each on a ground that ruling 80, clause 18 keeps and the foundation's AN-12 lists; step 3 answers a repeat and refuses nothing:
1. `schema_invalid`, then `unsupported_major_version`: the content is malformed, because the chart cannot read it at all, and only on this closed list, of which steps 2 and 4 to 6 hold the rest (clause 18; ruling 188; the foundation's AN-12; design call AD-63): a message that is not of this contract or of a MAJOR version openChart supports; a missing or unreadable writer (`added_by`, `practice_ref`); a missing or unreadable patient (`patient`, `source_patient_ref`, and `source_system`, in whose namespace the link is resolved); no readable `context`; no readable record identifiers (`message_id`, and `consult_id` and `consult_version` or `act_id` and `act_version`); no readable content object (a consult's `exchange`, a list of 1 to 500 turns with its `content_sha256`; an act's `act_class` and `act_content.content`); or a value beyond the bounds the schema sets. **Apart from these, no value refuses a write (design call AD-74, PROPOSED under rulings 188 and 194).** A declaration that is missing is not malformed, and nor is one whose value fails its form: openChart sets it aside, keeps it as given with its record of the write as a value openChart could not read, never used or displayed in place of its field (AW-07), gives it the outcome its absence takes, failing closed, and names it in the receipt's `set_aside`. Nor is an AI that is missing or named incompletely or in a form this path cannot read, which the AI check below holds; a missing or unreadable `source`, which openChart supplies; a time that is missing, unreadable or later than its receipt, for which openChart takes the time of receipt (AW-01, AW-15; design calls AD-60, AD-63 and AD-70); or a part of a consult's turns outside the turn's form, which openChart keeps and shows as AC-02 states (design call AD-75). the foundation's AN-12 states this rule for every addition (its design call CD-56);
2. `unauthorized`: the writer cannot be identified or is not registered, or it writes for another practice (AW-02; clauses 2 and 3; ruling 194), or the practice has suspended it: it is manually suspended or deregistered (AW-13; clause 18). Nothing else suspends a writer: the per-source cap refuses nothing (AW-13);
3. a repeat (AW-05): a retried message, the same `message_id` with the same digest, is answered with the original receipt, and an identical repeat under a new `message_id` with `duplicate` and the first result; neither adds anything. A corrected resend of a write held with `openwallet_id_missing` or `ai_not_identified` is not a repeat: it goes on through the steps below as a new write, linked to the held write (AW-05; design call AD-71). A repeat of a record's identifiers with a different content digest goes on through the steps below as a new addition, flagged `submission_ref_conflict` (clause 8), and so does a reused `message_id` with a different digest, flagged `message_id_conflict`;
4. `non_operational_context`: test data, as its writer declares it in `context` (AW-04; clause 18). openChart never infers test data from content, as the foundation's AN-12 requires;
5. `content_digest_mismatch`: the content is malformed, because it does not match its own digest (clause 18);
6. `patient_link_unknown`, `patient_link_mismatch`, `patient_link_in_review`: the patient cannot be matched (AW-03; clause 18).

**The AI check (ruling 194; design call AD-60).** A write that passes steps 1 to 6 SHALL NOT be refused for the AI it speaks for or the source system it names. Where one of the conditions below holds, openChart SHALL NOT add it to the chart: it SHALL hold it in quarantine outside the chart under AC-11, before any authority check, because no authority can be judged for a party the chart cannot identify or whose coverage it cannot confirm. Its `quarantine_cause` is the first of these that holds:
- `ai_not_identified`: its `on_behalf_of` cannot be read in the form this path reads (AW-02): it has none; or it is not a list, or a party is not an object; or its first party is not of `kind` `ai`; or, from the source system's own identity, it names a party after the AI; or, from a relay, it does not name exactly the AI and then the source system, in the form of `systemParty` with the write's `source_system`;
- `openwallet_id_missing`: its AI has no `openwallet_id`;
- `ai_not_identified`: its AI is not in the form of `aiParty`: it has no `model` or no `model_version`; its `openwallet_id` lacks openWallet's identifier shape, so it is no openWallet ID; its `model` or `model_version` lacks its pattern; or it carries a property `aiParty` does not define (AW-02);
- `source_system_not_covered`: the source system it names is one the writer's registration does not cover (the foundation's CD-71 at `9408e11`);
- `openwallet_id_not_covered`: the registration does not cover its AI's `openwallet_id` for that source system.

`ai_not_identified` means that the write does not identify, in the form this path reads, the AI it speaks for and the hands it passed through (ruling 80, clauses 2 and 7). openChart never reorders the parties or guesses which is the AI. Ruling 194 rules this for a consult whose AI's openWallet ID is missing or not registered for its relay: "Keep it in quarantine, outside the chart, in front of a clinician, and alert the practice to fix the registration." Its last words, "Refuse only when the relay itself cannot be identified", bar refusing any such write, as the foundation's AN-01 and AN-12 bar refusing for any party but the writer. Holding a write whose AI is missing, named incompletely or named in a form this path cannot read, holding an act the same way, and holding a write for an uncovered source system follow those words, and are PROPOSED; the foundation's AN-01 at `9408e11` names the same four hold causes, and its CD-71 lets a writing path hold a write for a source system the registration does not cover, which resolves tension AT-20. A `source_system` that is never a write-path system (AW-02) is held the same way, never refused: no relay's `on_behalf_of` can name it as the source system in the form above, so the AI check holds the write with `ai_not_identified`, and it is never released (design call AD-74). A write held with `openwallet_id_missing` or `ai_not_identified` can never be released, because nothing the practice registers can identify its AI (AC-11). A value given as `openwallet_id`, `model` or `model_version` outside its form, and every party of an `on_behalf_of` this path cannot read, is a value openChart could not read: each is kept as it arrived with the held write, SHALL NOT be used or displayed as the AI's identification, is readable on request by those AC-11 lets read the held write and, marked as given and unverified, by the administrative role in its audited view of the identification, and SHALL NOT appear in an alert, a notification, a log line, a diagnostic or an acknowledgement (AW-07).

**Information, with a consult's controls (design call AD-69, PROPOSED).** An `act.write` declared `clinical_note` whose content validates as the foundation's note content (`clinicalNoteContent`), or declared `document` whose content validates as its document shape (`documentContent`), is information (clause 10; ruling 187; the foundation's AN-06 and its design call CD-65). Steps 7 and 9 do not apply to it, because it is not an act that could take effect. Step 8 does, as for a consult: ruling 80, clause 21 says "Consent, the patient's link, the medical director's pause and narrowing, and clinician review of what an AI told a patient all still apply", and this change reads that as reaching everything an AI adds through this path. After the AI check, openChart SHALL make step 8's consent check; then its grant checks against the grant version in force at the write's `occurred_at` and that version's state then, in the order of AG-06's table, where a version whose information scope a narrowing removed fails `mode_not_permitted` (AG-08; design call AD-72, PROPOSED); then, when those pass, the grant's state at the write's `received_at`: `grant_paused` when it is paused then, and `grant_revoked` when it is revoked then. A consult is judged at its `ended_at` alone, because the AI told the patient while the grant was in force, and the record keeps what was said (AC-09). A note or a document is content the AI adds to the chart when it sends it: one that arrives while the medical director's pause or revocation is in force would add the AI's writing against that act, whatever date it declares, so it is quarantined in the chart. An expiry is no such act, and a note written before its grant expired is kept. When one of these checks fails, the write SHALL NOT be refused: it is quarantined in the chart under AC-11, as a consult is, with that check's code as its `quarantine_cause`, and answered `quarantined`. When none fails, it is kept as the foundation's information record and answered `kept_as_information`. Either way it is selected for clinician review under AW-16, and when its emergency indicator is set, or absent and so counted as set (design call AD-63), it is listed urgently for the patient's responsible clinician and the medical director, as AC-10 lists an emergency consult; it is never AI emergency management. The other reading is clause 10's, "Information needs only the identification above." On that reading an AI's note or document is kept with no consent check, no grant check and no review. The owner chooses (Open Question 19).

Steps 7 to 9 check authority. A write that passes steps 1 to 6 and the AI check, and fails one of them, SHALL NOT be refused, because missing authority is never a reason to refuse an addition (ruling 80, clause 18; design call AD-51). An `act.write` whose content is not information is recorded as a recommendation under AW-16; a `consult.write`, or an `act.write` whose content is information, is quarantined in the chart under AC-11. The code of the first check that fails, in this order, is the `quarantine_cause` of a consult or an information write, or an act's `path_reason` code:
7. for an `act.write`: `tier2_not_in_force`, then `act_type_unsupported` (AA-01);
8. `consent_ref_missing`, for a write that carries no `consent_ref` (ruling 199 for a consult; design call AD-62), then the grant checks, in the order of AG-06's table, and for an AI's note or document the grant's state at its receipt (above);
9. for an `act.write`: `emergency_management_refused`, `reserved_act_refused`, `targets_clinician_record`, `allowlist_entry_unknown`, `allowlist_entry_mismatch`, `conditions_unverified`, then, for a prescription, `prescribing_gate_not_open` (AA-03), a code that the MINOR version putting Tier 2 in force adds.

These codes keep their names, two of which say "refused": each names the authority that was missing, and none refuses a write (design call AD-56). For an `authority.notice`, steps 1 to 3 apply, then `notice_would_widen`, which refuses the notice.

**A write with no consent reference (ruling 199 for a consult; the rest design call AD-62, PROPOSED).** Ruling 199 rules it for a consult: "Keep it, marked not accepted, in front of a clinician and flagged to the medical director. The practice's AI has already spoken to the patient, so the practice needs the record. The missing consent is an incident to look into." Ruling 80, clause 21 keeps consent applying, and clause 18's list of refusals does not name it. openChart SHALL NOT refuse a write that carries no `consent_ref`, and a `consent_ref` outside the form of an opaque reference is a missing one, set aside (design call AD-74). A consult is quarantined in the chart with `quarantine_cause` `consent_ref_missing`, as ruling 199 requires, and so, PROPOSED, is an `act.write` whose content is information (design call AD-69). Any other act is recorded as a recommendation that carries the flag `consent_ref_missing` in its receipt, its readback and this change's write record, shown with it, whatever its first failing check; while Tier 2 is not in force that check is always `tier2_not_in_force`, so without the flag its missing consent would never show. Where `consent_ref_missing` is an act's first failing check, under Tier 2, it is also its `path_reason`, which maps to the foundation's `no_grant_in_force`. That mapping is a choice: consent is not a grant, but the foundation's closed list has no code for consent, and `no_grant_in_force` is the code that says the authority the write relied on did not reach this patient; a code of its own would need the foundation to add one. Every write with no consent reference, consult or act, SHALL be flagged to the medical director, with identifiers and the coded cause only, as an incident to look into; how the incident is recorded is decided in planning. **What a consent reference attests.** `consent_ref` is the source system's own reference to the patient's consent, as the source system records it, to the AI service whose output the write records and to that output being written to the practice's chart. The source system, or its operator, holds that consent; openChart holds only the reference, keeps it with the record and shows it. openChart checks only that a reference is present: it does not resolve or verify it, so it cannot tell a reference that names no consent, or a withdrawn one, from a good one (design call AD-14; Open Question 21). For an AI's note or document, and for any other act, refusing instead stays open for the owner (Open Question 15).

**The codes of a recommendation.** A recommendation carries two code sets, as the foundation's AN-09 provides for a writing path that has reasons of its own (its design call CD-51; design call AD-57):
- `path_reason`: this contract's own code for the first check that failed, under the scheme `openchart-ai-clinical-record-write.missing_authority`, version `1.0`. It is kept for the source and the reviewer, and decides nothing;
- `missing_authority`: the code of the foundation's closed list that the path code maps to, and `no_practitioner_sign_off`. Every recommendation on this path carries `no_practitioner_sign_off`, because no practitioner has signed off an AI's act and the 1.0 contract carries no sign-off (ruling 151; the foundation's CA-04).

| `path_reason` code | `missing_authority` |
|---|---|
| `tier2_not_in_force`, `act_type_unsupported`, `mode_not_permitted`, `emergency_management_refused`, `reserved_act_refused`, `targets_clinician_record` | `no_decision_authority`, `no_practitioner_sign_off` |
| `no_current_grant`, `grant_paused`, `grant_expired`, `grant_revoked`, `grant_mismatch`, `mode_mismatch`, `approver_mismatch`, `legal_review_mismatch`, `consent_ref_missing`, `allowlist_entry_unknown`, `allowlist_entry_mismatch`, `conditions_unverified` | `no_grant_in_force`, `no_practitioner_sign_off` |
| `prescribing_gate_not_open`, which the Tier 2 MINOR version adds (AA-03) | `no_practitioner_sign_off`, and `no_decision_authority` while an AI holds none. The gate code goes in `path_reason` only, because a safety gate is never a missing authority (the foundation's AN-09) |

The schema refuses any other combination. The foundation's contract requires `no_practitioner_sign_off` of every recommendation whose decision maker is not a human (its AN-08 and AN-09), and this contract requires it of every recommendation it reports. The foundation's other six codes are never true of an addition on this path: `decision_maker_not_confirmed`, because the AI is always the decision maker named (AW-16); `wallet_not_bound`, `licence_verification_missing`, `attestation_not_verified` and `signing_service_unavailable`, because they concern a practitioner's sign-off attempt, which the 1.0 contract does not carry; and `practice_attestation_not_in_force`, because an AI's grant rests on a recorded legal review, not on a practice attestation. While Tier 2 is not in force, every act's first failing check is `tier2_not_in_force`, so every recommendation carries `missing_authority` `no_decision_authority` and `no_practitioner_sign_off`, with `path_reason` `tier2_not_in_force`. An act with no consent reference also carries the flag `consent_ref_missing`, so that its missing consent shows whatever its first failing check (design call AD-62).

Whatever step refuses a schema-valid write, its refusal record SHALL keep the emergency indicator, and for a consult a declared `red_flag`, when the request carried them, whoever sent it. Such a refusal SHALL be listed urgently only when the write's `context` is `operational` and it comes from a registered identity that is active or manually suspended: never from an unregistered caller or a deregistered identity, and never from a `scenario` or `simulation` context (AW-04; design call AD-43). Where it is listed, it is listed without content, and beyond the per-source ceiling of AW-13 it is collapsed into that source's digest: where the patient resolves through an existing link (AW-03, changing nothing), for the patient's responsible clinician and the medical director, with AC-11's fallback recipients; otherwise for the medical director, or, where none is designated, as an alert to the administrative role that the foundation's AU-01 names carrying an opaque reference and a coded cause. A recommendation, a quarantined consult or a held write is not a refusal: it is listed with its content under AW-16, AA-06 or AC-11, urgently when its emergency indicator is set, or absent and so counted as set (design call AD-63), or, for a consult, when its `review_flags` carry `red_flag`.

A refused write SHALL store no clinical content. For every refused write, openChart SHALL keep a refusal record holding only identifiers, the write kind, the act class where there is one, the claimed mode, the reason code, the time, and the emergency indicator and `red_flag` when the request declared them (design call AD-7). The refusal record SHALL be visible as AW-09, AC-06 and AA-12 state.

### Requirement: AW-07 No PHI in logs, errors, receipts, notifications, diagnostics or keys
openChart's logs, errors, diagnostics, idempotency keys, clinician notifications and every receipt, notice and status message it sends under the contract MUST NOT contain names, dates of birth, contact details, patient identifier values, exchange or summary text, act content or any other clinical content. Diagnostics and log lines SHALL reference a write only by openChart's own references: its `record_ref`, its reference to the message it received, its grant identifiers, and the writer as the practice registered it. A clinician notification on any device SHALL say only that a record awaits attention, with a coded cause and an opaque reference. Opening the record SHALL require an authenticated openChart session. A value given as an `openwallet_id`, `model` or `model_version` outside its form, every party of an `on_behalf_of` that this path cannot read, a time a write declares that openChart does not use (AW-15), every declaration set aside and every member set aside unread (AW-01, AW-06) may hold anything, so none SHALL appear as given in any log, error, diagnostic, alert, notification, receipt, readback or acknowledgement; each is a value openChart could not read, kept and readable as the next paragraph states (AW-06, AC-11).

**A value openChart could not read (design call AD-74, PROPOSED; Open Question 24).** A value openChart could not read SHALL be kept as given with the addition's record, which on this path is this change's record of the write, a held write's included. It SHALL NOT be used or displayed in place of the field it was given for. Anyone who can read the chart SHALL be able to read it on request, marked as a value openChart could not read; for a write held outside the chart, that is everyone AC-11 lets read the held write. It SHALL NOT appear in an alert, a notification, a log line, a diagnostic or an acknowledgement. These values are a declaration set aside (AW-01, AW-06), a member the schema does not define, an AI's identification or a party of `on_behalf_of` this path cannot read, a time a write declares that openChart does not use (AW-15), and a part of a consult's turn kept outside its form, an attachment's reference and media type and a turn's `author_type` as given included (AC-02). A receipt names a field set aside by its field name only, in `set_aside`, and a member the schema does not define only as `unknown_property`; a readback carries codes only; and no other message under the contract carries such a value. Ruling 80, clause 8 makes what every addition records "permanent and visible to anyone who can read the chart", and a value openChart could not read may be the only trace of what the source meant, so it stays readable; marking it, and never putting it in place of its field, keeps it from being taken for what openChart read. The foundation's AN-12 is to carry this rule for every addition at its next head; until it does, this change states it for its path.

**Alerts carry nothing taken from a write (design call AD-77, PROPOSED).** A value taken from a write can carry whatever its sender put there, a patient's details included, even when it passes its form: a `model` that fits its pattern can still spell a name and a diagnosis. So every alert, flag, digest, integration exception and log line about a write, held, quarantined, refused or kept, SHALL carry only the writer as the practice registered it, the coded cause, a count and openChart's own opaque references, and nothing taken from the write: AC-11's alert to the administrative role, the flag to the medical director for a missing consent reference (AW-06), AW-13's digests and alerts, and every integration exception included. Where an alert must show an identification to the administrative role, it SHALL come from the practice's own registration data; the role sees the identification as the write gave it only in the audited view below. Wherever this change says that an alert, flag, digest or exception holds identifiers, it means these. The foundation's AN-01 states the same rule for every writing path (its design call CD-61), and this requirement applies it.

**What else an alert may carry (design call AD-77, PROPOSED).** An alert for `ai_not_identified` MAY say whether the identification was missing or was given in a form this path cannot read, as a coded detail of the cause, and nothing more about it. Alerts about held or quarantined writes SHALL be grouped and counted by writer and cause: one alert for each writer, as the practice registered it, and each coded cause, carrying the count of that writer's writes held or quarantined for that cause, not one alert for each write. An alert MAY carry openChart's own references to the held writes it counts, so that the administrative role can find what to release; they are openChart's references, never values taken from the write. The foundation's AN-01 is to carry these additions for every writing path at its next head.

**How the administrative role learns which AI to register (design call AD-77, PROPOSED; Open Question 25).** No alert carries the identification as the write gave it, so the administrative role SHALL be able to open a held write and see its identification as given: the parties of its `on_behalf_of` and its `source_system` or, where `on_behalf_of` cannot be read as parties, the value given, each marked as given and unverified, beside the writer as the practice registered it. That view SHALL show nothing else of the held write: no patient and no clinical content. Every opening of it SHALL be audited, with who opened it, when and which held write. Nothing it shows is copied into an alert, a notification, a log line, a diagnostic or an acknowledgement. ### Requirement: AW-08 openChart's outbound messages under the contract use durable delivery
Every `write.receipt` that is not returned synchronously, and every `act.status`, `consult.reviewed`, `authority.notice`, `held.ended` and `message.ack` openChart sends, SHALL be written to a durable outbound store in the same transaction as its cause. openChart SHALL deliver them to the registered AI system with retries that reuse the original `message_id`, until the AI system answers with `message.ack` where the message calls for one. This follows the pattern of PL-14 in `define-openpractice-boundary` (not yet on main); the mechanism is decided in planning. Delivery MUST NOT rely on `frappe.publish_realtime` or any other site-local event.

**Notice clock.** openChart owns a clock on every `authority.notice` it sends (design call AD-41). A holder of the administrative role's acknowledgment stops only the administrative role's re-alerts, never the medical director's. ### Requirement: AW-12 The writing source system can read back what openChart stored, by digest and code only
openChart SHALL answer a `record.readback_request` with exactly one `record.readback`, so that the source system that wrote a record, or a relay acting for it, can confirm what openChart stored (design call AD-28). Only the integration identity of the source system that wrote the record SHALL be answered with `found`. For a found record, the answer SHALL carry only: the record reference; the source system's record identifier and version; the stored content digest, recomputed by openChart from what it stored (for a consult, as `exchange.content_sha256` is defined; for an act, over its stored `act_content`); the time openChart received it, `received_at` (AW-15); its current state; for an accepted record, the grant version it was checked against and its review or co-review status; for a recommendation (AW-16), the state `recommendation`, its `missing_authority` codes, its `path_reason` and, where it carried no consent reference, `consent_ref_missing`; for a consult or an information write quarantined in the chart (AC-11), the state `quarantined` and its `quarantine_cause`; for a held write, the state `held`, `released` (with the reference and state of the record it became), `recorded_by_clinician` or `dismissed` (with the reason), and its `quarantine_cause` (design call AD-65); for an act kept as information, the state `information`; and, for a record kept under AW-05's flags, its `submission_ref_conflict` or `message_id_conflict`. A recommendation, an information record and a write quarantined in the chart also carry the grant version checked where one was checked. A readback MUST NOT carry exchange text, summary text, act content, patient identifiers or any other clinical content. A request from any other identity SHALL be answered `unauthorized`, and one for an unknown record `not_found`, each with no record detail. A write quarantined in the chart, a held write, an information record and a recommendation are answered `found`, so that the source can confirm what was kept; none is an accepted record, and the review of none is reported (design calls AD-52 and AD-69). A held write is answered as held while it is held, and by its ending once it has ended; openChart also tells the source of the ending at once with `held.ended` (AC-11; design call AD-65). In 1.0 no readback says that an act took effect: an act is a recommendation, information or a held write, and the states and review statuses of an accepted act are left to the Tier 2 MINOR version (ruling 157).

A readback is not an interoperability read in the sense of the foundation's CA-13: it returns no record, only identifiers, digests and codes, and only to the writing source. So the foundation's audit verifier (its CA-08) does not gate it as it gates publication and interoperability reads. Its `stored_content_sha256` is recomputed at read time from what openChart stored, which is how a source detects a stored record that differs from what it sent, so openChart SHALL answer it even when the verifier has reported an integrity failure on that record (design call AD-28).

### Requirement: AW-13 An integration identity can be suspended or deregistered, and a per-source cap folds a flood into a digest without refusing anything
openChart SHALL provide a manual suspension and a deregistration of an integration identity, each audited and effective at once, and a per-source cap that refuses nothing (design calls AD-37 and AD-64). A manual suspension and a deregistration are the practice suspending a writer, which ruling 80 keeps as a reason to refuse (clauses 3 and 18); the medical director or the practice's administrative role may suspend (ruling 159).

**Manual suspension and deregistration.** A human has decided that the source is unsafe. The medical director, or a holder of the administrative role that the foundation's AU-01 names, MAY suspend an identity manually or deregister it. While it is manually suspended, and after it is deregistered, every message from it SHALL fail at step 2 of AW-06's precedence with reason `unauthorized`, and SHALL store no clinical content, except its `message.ack` of a notice openChart sent it. Only the medical director SHALL lift a manual suspension. A deregistered identity is never re-enabled; only a new registration under AW-02 restores a path.

**The cap (ruling 200; the cap's number, the digest's form and the alert's wording, folding recommendations and held writes into the digest beside consults, and alerting the administrative role where no medical director is designated, are design call AD-64, PROPOSED).** Ruling 200: "No. Keep every consult, group that source's entries into one listing so they do not bury the queue, and alert the medical director, who can suspend the source by hand." openChart SHALL count, per source system and practice, the consults it quarantines in the chart under AC-11 in any rolling 24 hours, leaving out every consult whose emergency indicator is set, or absent and so counted as set, or whose `review_flags` carry `red_flag`, and every write held by the AI check. The practice's policy sets the cap, which SHALL NOT exceed a built-in ceiling of 20; with no policy set, the ceiling applies. Reaching the cap SHALL NOT refuse, suspend or hold anything: consults keep being accepted or quarantined, and acts keep being recorded as recommendations, exactly as below the cap, and an emergency act is recorded and listed urgently as AA-06 says. While a source's rolling count is at or above its cap, the source is over its cap, and openChart SHALL fold that source's non-urgent listings, its quarantined consults, recommendations and held writes, into one digest for each reviewing clinician, so that a flood does not bury the queue. Each item stays recorded, is reachable from the digest, and keeps its own review window and escalation. openChart SHALL alert the medical director, or, where none is designated, the administrative role with no clinical content, to consider a manual suspension, and SHALL NOT suspend the source itself. A source over its cap reads `source_status` `over_cap` in its grant readback (AW-14). The automatic cap suspension of earlier drafts is withdrawn: its only inputs were failed authority and identification checks, so it refused writes for missing authority, which ruling 80, clause 18 forbids. Ruling 200 settles that the chart does not stop taking a source's consults at its limit (Open Question 16, answered).

**Alerts and records.** Every suspension, lift and deregistration, and every time a source goes over its cap or comes back under it, SHALL be recorded with actor, reason and time, and listed for the medical director and the administrative role, naming the writer as the practice registered it, and never a patient or anything taken from a write (AW-07). A refused write that declared the emergency indicator or `red_flag` is listed urgently without content only where AW-06 allows it.

**Notices to the source.** openChart SHALL tell the source of every change to its standing with an `authority.notice` from openChart (design call AD-41), using these `change` values: `suspended` for a manual suspension; `deregistered` for a deregistration, sent as the last notice, after which openChart serves the identity only to deliver that notice and receive its acknowledgment; `suspension_lifted` when a manual suspension is lifted; and `no_medical_director` when no AU-01 designation is in force, so no grant is current (AG-02). Going over the cap is not a change in standing and sends no notice; the grant readback reports it. Each notice carries the grant identifier and configuration version where a grant exists. The notice clock of AW-08 applies. When openChart receives from a source any message other than a `message.ack` after it has sent that source a `suspended` or `deregistered` notice, whoever has acknowledged the notice or its alert, openChart SHALL raise one alert, to the medical director and to the administrative role that the foundation's AU-01 names, carrying an opaque reference to the source and a coded cause, and never a patient or content. It SHALL raise no further such alert for that suspension or deregistration (design call AD-46).

**Urgent-listing ceiling and alarm.** Per source and practice, openChart SHALL list urgent refusals, urgent quarantined consults and urgent recommendations one by one up to a ceiling in any rolling 60 minutes, which the practice's policy sets at no more than 10 (design call AD-43). Further urgent listings from that source in the interval SHALL be collapsed into a digest, sent at once with the first listing over the ceiling, and then once every 60 minutes while further listings arrive (design call AD-46). The digest goes to the medical director, by identifiers only. Where no AU-01 designation is in force, it SHALL instead be sent per patient, without content, to AC-11's fallback recipients for that patient's chart, every clinician authorized for it; the administrative role that the foundation's AU-01 names SHALL receive an alert with opaque references and coded causes only, and a listing whose patient resolves through no existing link reaches that alert only. An urgent listing of a refusal from a manually suspended source, listed alone or in a digest, SHALL be acknowledged by a clinician who received it within 1 hour, the window AC-11 sets for urgent quarantined consults; otherwise it SHALL escalate as AC-11 provides, re-alerting until a clinician acknowledges it. openChart SHALL also count, per source, the consults quarantined with the emergency indicator or `red_flag`. When they reach the practice's alarm threshold, at most 10 in any rolling 24 hours, openChart SHALL alert the medical director, or, where none is designated, the administrative role, to consider a manual suspension. It SHALL NOT suspend the source itself, because an automatic suspension would stop urgent consults reaching the chart.

**Residual.** A patient who really was given emergency guidance by a deregistered or unregistered source is not listed for any clinician. openChart cannot trust anything such a caller sends, and listing it would let that caller raise urgent listings without limit. That source must hand the patient to a clinician itself (design call AD-43).

### Requirement: AW-14 A source can read back its own authority as a heartbeat, with no content
openChart SHALL answer every `grant.readback_request` with exactly one `grant.readback`, from its own records at read time (design call AD-42). Neither message carries patient or clinical content. openChart SHALL answer `unauthorized`, with no other field, to any identity other than the source's registered identity or its relay, and to a manually suspended or deregistered identity. Otherwise it SHALL compute the answer at read time from every grant check of AG-02 and AG-06 that does not depend on what a write contains (design call AD-44): whether an AU-01 designation is in force; whether a grant version is in force for the present time; whether it is paused; whether it, or the legal review it relies on, has expired; and whether it is revoked. **The readback says `grant_current` only if a well-formed `consult.write` within the grant's mode and scope, naming the grant version in force and the configuration version it records, would pass the grant checks now.** It then carries that grant's identifier, version, mode, configuration version, state `active`, effective time and expiry. Otherwise openChart SHALL answer `grant_not_current` with the reason of the first check that fails, in this order: `no_medical_director` where no AU-01 designation is in force (a write gets `no_current_grant`, AG-02); `no_grant` where no grant version is in force, with no grant attached; `grant_paused`; `grant_expired` where the grant or the legal review it relies on has expired, the grant then being in state `expired` (AG-09); and `grant_revoked`. Apart from `no_medical_director`, a write that fails these checks carries the same code, as a consult's `quarantine_cause` or an act's `path_reason`, and is never refused for it; `design.md` §13 maps each check to its write outcome, readback answer and notice. Every reason other than `no_grant` carries the grant, with its state, where one exists. Both answers carry `source_status`: `active` or `over_cap`. `over_cap` means that the source's non-urgent listings are folded into a digest; nothing is refused for it (AW-13). A narrowing or a superseding version shows as the grant version now in force. A grant readback changes nothing. The source can call it at any time, under request limits decided in planning, and bound its own authority to the narrower of its configuration and the answer.

**What openChart never sends.** openChart SHALL never send a message that contradicts itself, and the schema refuses each of these (design call AD-45):
- a `grant.readback` with `not_current_reason` `no_grant` that carries a `grant`;
- a `grant.readback` whose reason disagrees with the state of the grant it carries: `grant_paused` with a state other than `paused`, `grant_expired` with a state other than `expired`, or `grant_revoked` with a state other than `revoked`;
- an `authority.notice` with a standing `change` (`suspended`, `deregistered`, `suspension_lifted` or `no_medical_director`) that carries `new_mode` or `removed_allowlist_entry_ids`.

**What openChart always sends.** On every grant-change notice it sends (`paused`, `narrowed`, `resumed`, `expired`, `revoked` or `superseded`), openChart SHALL include `grant_version`: the version in force once the change applies, which for `narrowed` and `superseded` is the new version (AG-01; design call AD-47). The schema requires `grant_id`, `configuration_version` and `grant_version` on every grant-change notice from openChart. Of the grant-change notices, only `narrowed` carries scope: it names the allowlist entries it removes in `removed_allowlist_entry_ids` and a lowered mode in `new_mode`; the other five carry none, and a superseding version's scope is the configuration version it records. A narrowing of a co-review window or of the reserved acts has no field in 1.0; openChart enforces it, and it concerns only the practice modes, which are off. A narrowing of the information scope (AG-08) has no field either; openChart enforces it, and the source learns it from the receipts of its notes and documents.

`contracts/examples/` holds an invalid example that breaks each of these rules, and openChart's contract tests SHALL show that the schema refuses each one and that openChart never emits one (design calls AD-45 and AD-47).

**The reader's rules.** A source that acts on these messages SHALL apply these rules (design call AD-45). They stay although the schema now refuses every self-contradictory message, because a receiver on an earlier draft digest, or one that does not validate, may still meet one, and because rules 2 to 4 order messages that are each valid:
1. A `grant.readback` or an `authority.notice` that contradicts itself, as the list above describes, is treated as not current.
2. A `grant.readback` whose `read_at` is earlier than the `effective_at` of a notice the source has received never overrides that notice.
3. A notice never widens authority by itself. The source widens its authority, by resuming or by adopting a wider grant version, only on a `grant_current` readback whose `read_at` is not earlier than the `effective_at` of every notice the source has received.
4. A notice whose `effective_at` is earlier than that of a notice the source has already applied never overrides it.

A relay passes these messages unchanged and applies none of these rules.

The 1.0 contract supports no interim version of an open consult: a `consult.write` records an ended consult, whose end, where the write gives none that AW-15 uses, is its receipt, and a new consult version continues or corrects an ended consult (AC-01).

#### Scenario: A heartbeat in normal operation
- **WHEN** an active source with a current grant sends a `grant.readback_request`
- **THEN** openChart answers `grant_current`, with the grant version in force and `source_status` `active`, and no patient or clinical content

#### Scenario: Failure: a manually suspended source sends a heartbeat
- **WHEN** a manually suspended source sends a `grant.readback_request`
- **THEN** openChart answers `unauthorized` with no other field

#### Scenario: Failure: the grant is paused
- **WHEN** the medical director has paused the grant, and the source sends a heartbeat
- **THEN** openChart answers `grant_not_current` with reason `grant_paused` and the paused grant

#### Scenario: Failure: no medical director is designated
- **WHEN** no AU-01 designation is in force, and the source sends a heartbeat
- **THEN** openChart answers `grant_not_current` with reason `no_medical_director`

#### Scenario: Failure: a source over its cap sends a heartbeat
- **WHEN** a source over its cap sends a heartbeat
- **THEN** openChart answers it, with `source_status` `over_cap`, and the source's writes are still recorded as below the cap

#### Scenario: Failure: the legal review behind an active grant has lapsed
- **WHEN** the legal review that the grant relies on has passed its expiry, and the source sends a heartbeat
- **THEN** openChart answers `grant_not_current` with reason `grant_expired` and the grant in state `expired`, the reason a write then gets, and never `grant_current`

#### Scenario: Failure: a readback computed before a suspension arrives after its notice

### Requirement: AW-15 Every record kept from a write states when it was received, when it arose, where it came from, who wrote it and on whose behalf, and is permanent
Every record that openChart keeps from a write, whether an accepted consult record, a quarantined consult, a recommendation or an information record, SHALL record (ruling 80, clauses 4 to 8; design call AD-50):
- `received_at`: openChart's own clock when it received the write, which openChart also returns in the receipt and the readback;
- `occurred_at`: when the information arose. For an `act.write` it is the write's `occurred_at`. For a consult it is the consult's `ended_at`, and the record also keeps `started_at` where the write gives one in this contract's timestamp form, and otherwise shows the start as not stated. **A missing, unreadable or later time (design call AD-70, PROPOSED).** Where the write gives no such time, or one that is not a timestamp in this contract's pattern naming a real calendar day, or one later than `received_at` by more than 5 minutes, openChart SHALL take `received_at` in its place, because ruling 80, clause 4 asks for the time the information arose only where it differs; SHALL show that the value was defaulted, as for `source`; and SHALL keep the value given, if any, unedited with this change's record of the write, never as the time the information arose (AW-07); on the foundation's records the time supplied is marked `occurred_at_defaulted`, and a value given that is a timestamp in this contract's pattern naming a real day, as one later than receipt is, is also kept as the foundation's `occurred_at_as_declared`, which takes a timestamp only, while one that cannot be read stays only with this change's record of the write (the foundation's AN-04 and CD-73 at `9408e11`, under which a defaulted time is the time the path received the item, on this path `received_at`). An `ended_at` earlier than a `started_at` that can be read is a time that cannot be read: the end is `received_at`, the start is shown as not stated, and both values are kept. A `sent_at` that is absent or cannot be read is replaced by `received_at` wherever a sending time is needed, marked as defaulted. Every check that reads that time then reads `received_at`: a consult's grant (AC-09) and an AI note's or document's (AW-06) are judged at receipt. 5 minutes is the clock skew allowed between the writer's clock and openChart's, wide enough for clocks that are not synchronised to the second and narrow enough that a source cannot date a write into the future. For a write released from the hold, the time supplied is its first arrival (`first_received_at`), when the information had already arisen. No write is refused for a time;
- `source`: where the information came from, as a `kind` (`patient_statement`, `device_reading`, `ai_consult`, `clinician_observation`, `external_record` or `chart_entry`) and the source system's reference to it. Every write on this path carries `ai_consult`, because what an AI writes is the AI's own output; what it relied on is in its evidence references. A write that leaves out `source`, or gives one outside its form, is given `ai_consult`, marked `defaulted`, with a consult's `consult_id` as the reference, or for an act the `consult_id` of the consult it names in `consult_ref` in its form, and otherwise with no reference, as the foundation's provenance at `9408e11` allows, so that a path never invents one (its AN-04 and CD-73); the record shows that the value was defaulted, as for the two booleans of design call AD-63 (design calls AD-63 and AD-74, PROPOSED);
- `added_by` and `on_behalf_of`, as AW-02 states, with the identity basis of each;
- the identification of the AI: its `openwallet_id`, `model` and `model_version`.

These fields SHALL be part of the permanent record and SHALL be shown with it to everyone who can read it (AW-09). Nothing in a record kept from a write SHALL be edited or deleted. A correction SHALL be a new dated addition that points to what it corrects: a new consult version that references its predecessor (AC-01), a new act version, or a clinician's own record (ruling 80, clause 8). The general rule for every addition to the chart is the foundation's AN-04 and AN-05 (at head `9408e11`); this requirement applies it to the records this change keeps. On the foundation's recommendation record (AW-16), the writer is `added_by`, a party of kind `system` whose `system_id` is openChart's reference to the writer's registration, with its `operator_ref`; the registration holds the writer's issuer and namespace (the foundation's AN-03; design call AD-58). For a relay, the source system is the second party in `on_behalf_of`. It has no registration of its own in this chart, so it is named by `external_ref`, `asserted_by` the relay: the issuer of the write's `source_system`, the namespace `source_system`, and its namespace token as the value; its `operator_ref` is kept in this change's write record (the foundation's AN-02 and design call CD-57; design call AD-58). This path records no party as `not_identified`: its writer is verified by the chart, and its AI is fully identified and, with its source system, covered by the writer's registration, or the write is held (AW-06). A new version of an AI act is a new recommendation that points to the one it corrects (`corrects`). A write that corrects the identification of a held write (AW-05) names it in `corrects_held_item_ref` on the addition it becomes (the foundation's AN-04 and CD-70; design call AD-71). A held write keeps the `received_at` of its first arrival. The addition it becomes, if it is released, is dated by its release: its `received_at` is the time openChart added it to the chart, never its first arrival, and its provenance carries `released_from_quarantine`, with the held write's reference (`held_item_ref`), its first arrival (`first_received_at`), who released it (`released_by`) and when (`released_at`). No two additions carry the same `held_item_ref`, because a held write ends once (AC-11; the foundation's AN-01 and AN-04; design call AD-65).

- **WHEN** a relay delivers a consult that an AI held with a patient from 09:00 to 09:01, and openChart receives it at 09:02
- **THEN** the record holds `received_at` 09:02, `occurred_at` 09:01 with `started_at` 09:00, `source` `ai_consult` with its reference, the relay as `added_by`, and the AI by its openWallet ID, model and model version, then the source system, in `on_behalf_of`, and every clinician who can read the chart sees them

#### Scenario: Failure: a write omits where its information came from

#### Scenario: Failure: an AI's note gives no time

#### Scenario: Failure: a write is dated after its receipt

- **THEN** openChart does not refuse it: it takes `received_at` as the end, shows the start as not stated, keeps both values given with its record of the write, checks the grant at receipt, and names `ended_at` and `started_at` in the receipt's `set_aside`

### Requirement: AW-16 An AI act attempted without authority is recorded in full as a recommendation that has no effect, and a clinician may adopt or decline it
An `act.write` that passes steps 1 to 6 of AW-06 and its AI check, and fails a check at step 7, 8 or 9, SHALL NOT be refused (ruling 80, clause 18; the foundation's AN-12). openChart SHALL record it in full as the clinical-acts foundation's recommendation record: `record_kind` `recommendation` of `openchart-clinical-act-content/1.0` at SHA-256 `9ae73b150e00ae581012286e3016a5fac6fa044ce5cbf0ce7ba9c36d9204a64d`, as `define-clinical-acts-foundation` (openChart-spec#5) defines it at head `9408e11` in its AN-09 and AN-14 (ruling 80, clause 13; design calls AD-51 and AD-58). The recommendation SHALL hold:
- **the proposed act:** the write's `action` where it gives one in its form and consistent with its target, and otherwise `sign` for new content or `new_version` for a write that names a target record (below; design calls AD-68 and AD-74); the write's `act_class`, kept as declared whether or not the chart defines it, and its `act_type` where it gives one in its form; and its `act_content.content` exactly as given, with `content_schema` naming the schema the write declared it under, where it names one in its form. The foundation checks such content only for being well formed, nothing reads it as an act's content, and the act-validity rules apply only when a clinician adopts it, who completes what is missing (its AN-09, and its design calls CD-51 and CD-56; ruling 188). A write declared `clinical_note` or `document` whose content validates as the foundation's note content or document shape is not a recommendation: it is information, as below;
- **the decision maker:** the AI, by its `openwallet_id`, `model` and `model_version` (the foundation's AN-02);
- **identification and provenance:** `added_by`, the writer, `verified_by_chart`; `on_behalf_of`, the AI and, for a relay, the source system, each `asserted_by` the writer; `received_at`, `occurred_at` and `source`; and `submission_ref`, naming the write's `act_id` and `act_version` (its encoding is decided in planning), with `submission_ref_conflict` where AW-05 flags one (AW-02, AW-15; the foundation's AN-04 and AN-10);
- **the codes:** the marker `not_authorized_decision`, the foundation's `missing_authority` codes and this contract's `path_reason`, mapped as AW-06 states.

**What a missing or set-aside declaration becomes (ruling 188; design calls AD-63 and AD-74).** No write is refused for a declaration it leaves out, or for one whose value fails its form, which is set aside and becomes what its absence becomes, failing closed (AW-06). A missing `act_type` stays missing, and under Tier 2 the act takes `act_type_unsupported`. A missing authority claim, or a missing field of it, takes the code of the check it would have answered (AG-06). Missing or empty `evidence_refs` is recorded, shown as resting on no evidence reference and flagged for review, as AC-03 does for a consult. A missing `emergency_indicated` or `declared_reserved` counts as true, failing closed, and the record shows that the value was defaulted. A missing `content_schema` leaves the content kept as given with no declared schema. A missing `consent_ref` takes the consent step of AW-06 (design call AD-62). Set aside, an `action` outside the foundation's codes, a targeted action that names no target, or `sign` that names one, gives the proposed act the default action, `sign`, or `new_version` where the write names a target in its form, the action given being kept in this change's write record; a `target_record_ref` outside its form is absent; a `consult_ref` outside its form names no consult; a `knowledge_basis` outside its form is not stated; and a `supersedes_act` outside its form points to nothing.

**Information.** An `act.write` declared `clinical_note` whose content validates as the foundation's note content (`clinicalNoteContent`) is information: a note only records who wrote it (ruling 187; the foundation's AN-06 and design call CD-65). So is an `act.write` declared `document` whose content validates as the foundation's document shape (`documentContent`: a title, a date, text, and attachments by reference and digest of the three display-only media types), which the foundation publishes for summaries, letters and imported records (its AN-06 and design call CD-65). Ruling 204 rules how the chart keeps such documents until richer formats are defined: "As information, in one simple format: a title, a date, text, and attached PDFs or pictures that are only displayed. Anything structured still becomes a recommendation." At `9408e11` the foundation limits the media types to `application/pdf`, `image/png` and `image/jpeg`, ruling 204's PDFs and pictures, so a plain-text attachment is not admitted; the field names and the size limits are the foundation's, PROPOSED (design call AD-68). openChart SHALL keep either as the foundation's information record, with `act_class` `clinical_note` or `document`, from a writer without the authority to sign it, marked not signed off, with the identification and provenance above (the foundation's AN-14 and design call CD-63), and answer `kept_as_information`, the receipt code that record carries. Content declared `clinical_note` or `document` that does not validate as that kind's shape is a recommendation, with `no_decision_authority` (design call AD-68): structured content under ruling 204's words, "Anything structured still becomes a recommendation", and a document that fails the shape for a reason that is not structure, such as a missing date, under ruling 188 and design call AD-68, not under ruling 204's words. Content that does validate is kept only after the consent check and the grant checks of AW-06's step 8; one that fails is quarantined in the chart under AC-11 instead (design call AD-69, PROPOSED).

**The review of an AI's information (design call AD-69, PROPOSED).** openChart SHALL select for clinician review, whatever the sample, every information record that an AI adds through this path, every one released into the chart from the hold, and every information write quarantined in the chart. It SHALL list each, with its content, in an authenticated openChart session, for the patient's responsible clinician, or for the medical director where openChart records none, with AC-11's fixed window and fallback recipients for a quarantined one; a notification carries no content (AW-07). The review is the review AC-08 defines for a consult record: recorded by a named clinician holding the AG-11 grant, signed off as AC-08 states, with its outcome, an optional note and the time, due within the review policy's window, or within 1 hour when the emergency indicator is set or counted as set. One review MAY cover a consult and the AI notes and documents of the same patient that name it in `consult_ref`, as AC-08 states, so that overlapping content need not be reviewed twice. Where nobody in the practice holds the AG-11 grant, AC-11's fallback applies to each of these records, as to every item selected for review (AG-11; design calls AD-73 and AD-76, PROPOSED). An overdue review escalates to the medical director, and an `unsafe` outcome alerts the medical director at once and marks a follow-up as owed to the patient. A listing stays open until such a review is recorded, and no timer, default or agent closes it. Its listings fall under AW-13's per-source ceiling and digest as a recommendation's do, except urgent ones. The review creates no act, never turns the information into a decision, and is never reported to the source in 1.0.

What the write carried that the recommendation record has no field for SHALL be kept, unedited, in this change's own record of the write, linked to the recommendation by its identifier and shown with it: the `act_id` and `act_version`, the models, the evidence references, the conditions met, the knowledge basis, the linked consult, the authority the write claimed, a declared reservation and the emergency indicator (design call AD-58). When the write supersedes an earlier AI act of its source system (`supersedes_act`), the new recommendation points to the earlier one as the one it corrects (`corrects`), because no AI act is an act of record (the foundation's AN-05). When the write names a target record (`target_record_ref`), the proposed action is the write's `action`, or `new_version` where it gives none, and the write's reference is kept as given in `target_as_given`. Where openChart resolves it to an act version of this patient's chart, that version is `target`. Where it resolves to an addition that is not an act, a recommendation or an information record, the proposed act is new content (`sign`) that `corrects` that addition, and the action the AI intended is kept in this change's write record. The content is kept as given; an intended co-signature carries none of its own, and the write's content is kept in the write record (the foundation's AN-09, which answers tension AT-18; design call AD-68). A clinician who adopts such a recommendation does so through the foundation's adoption form for its action (its AN-11). Under Tier 2 an AI's action on a clinician-authored record is `targets_clinician_record`, which maps to `no_decision_authority` (AA-10). This path names the AI as the decision maker, its identity asserted by the writer, and never records a claimed decision maker or `decision_maker_not_confirmed`, because the writer's registration covers the AI's openWallet ID (the foundation's AN-08, with its design calls CD-61 and CD-67; design call AD-58). A write whose AI the registration does not cover is held, and no recommendation is recorded while it is held (AW-06, AC-11).

A recommendation SHALL have no effect (ruling 80, clause 14). It is not an act of record: it has no act status, enters no co-review queue, cannot be signed, released, transmitted or published, is never a billable fact, and never satisfies a check or removes a warning that a decision would. Nothing is ordered, prescribed, sent, billed or treated as established because of it. A recommendation is not gated: the prescribing-safety gate applies to a clinician's own prescription when they adopt one (the foundation's AN-09 and RX-02). Wherever it is shown, to a clinician or to the patient, it SHALL be marked "not an authorized decision" and SHALL say which authority was missing (clause 13; AW-09).

openChart SHALL tell the writer at once, with a `write.receipt` whose result is `recorded_as_recommendation`, carrying the `record_ref`, which is the recommendation's identifier, and both code sets (clause 15; the foundation's AN-10). This write path's listing is the review path that the foundation's AN-11 uses for it. openChart SHALL list the recommendation, with its content, in an authenticated openChart session, for the patient's responsible clinician when that clinician holds `sign` scope for its act class, and otherwise for the medical director; a notification carries no content (AW-07). It SHALL list it urgently, for the responsible clinician and the medical director, when its emergency indicator is set (AA-06). These listings fall under AW-13's per-source ceiling and digest (design call AD-46). A listing SHALL stay open until a named clinician adopts or declines the recommendation. Its due time and escalation SHALL work as the foundation's co-signature requirement's do (its CA-07), and no timer, default or agent SHALL close it (its AN-11).

A clinician who holds the authority MAY adopt it. Adoption makes a decision of the clinician's own through the foundation's normal path, and its record names the recommendation in `adopted_from` (ruling 80, clause 16; the foundation's AN-11). It takes the form the foundation's AN-11 gives for the proposed action (its design call CD-47, PROPOSED): for new content (`sign`), a draft of the clinician's own that the clinician may change, then signs off; for a proposed `cosign`, `discontinue`, `enter_in_error` or `cancel` of an existing act, the clinician's own sign-off of that action, whose lifecycle event names the recommendation; for a proposed `new_version`, the clinician's own new version, carrying `adopted_from`, made as the act's class allows: an amendment of a final note, diagnosis or procedure, inside an encounter amendment where its encounter is final; an encounter amendment for an encounter; a new signed version of an order or prescription that is still `pending_release`; and, for one already `released`, a discontinuation of the target and a new act, each signed off and each carrying `adopted_from`. Where the target did not resolve (`target_as_given` only), the clinician adopts by choosing the act it means, or by a new draft. The sign-off is the foundation's CA-04 (ruling 151; its AN-07), made in openChart, in the clinician's own logged-in session, re-authenticated (ruling 186), over the sign-off statement that CA-04 defines, with a single-use challenge (`challenge_ref`) and the openSig attestation over the statement's SHA-256 (`statement_sha256`), from a wallet bound to the clinician's account (its AU-07). Until openWallet and openSig can do their part, no sign-off is a real decision (ruling 152), and the recommendation stays open. A prescription adopted this way meets the prescribing-safety gate at its own sign-off (the foundation's RX-02). An adoption that does not complete is recorded on the recommendation, with the clinician, the time and the reasons, and the recommendation stays open (its AN-11). The clinician MAY instead decline it, with the clinician, the time and a coded reason recorded; the foundation's reason `not_a_decision_kept_for_reference` records content that was never a decision and stays for reference, never a clinician rejecting the facts it holds (its AN-11 and design call CD-65). Either way the recommendation stays in the chart as written.

An AI's recommendation never becomes a decision through anything the AI system or its relay sends: not a repeat, a new version, a later grant, a notice, or a claim inside its content (ruling 151; AW-05). A write that claims to be made on a clinician's behalf counts as that clinician's decision only when it carries that clinician's own sign-off, made as ruling 151 states (the foundation's AN-08). In version 1 a sign-off is made in openChart only; one made in another system counts later, once openWallet and openSig can prove it to the chart (ruling 186). The 1.0 contract carries no such sign-off and admits no human in `on_behalf_of`, so whatever an `act.write` says about a clinician is recorded as the AI's recommendation (ruling 80, clause 12; ruling 151). Wherever a choice could let a recommendation take effect as a decision, openChart SHALL fail closed and treat it as a recommendation.

The write path SHALL NOT be enabled for any practice until the foundation's recommendation record is built, with its feature (1), because an `act.write` could then arrive that this requirement says must be recorded and must not be refused (design call AD-55).

#### Scenario: An AI's document is information
- **WHEN** an identified AI system sends an `act.write` declared `document` whose content validates as the foundation's document shape, a title, a date, text and a PDF attachment by reference and digest
- **THEN** openChart keeps it as the foundation's information record with `act_class` `document`, marked not signed off, answers `kept_as_information`, and records no recommendation

#### Scenario: A clinician declines a recommendation
- **WHEN** a clinician declines a recommendation
- **THEN** the decline is recorded with the clinician, the time and a coded reason, and the recommendation stays in the chart as written, with no effect

#### Scenario: Failure: nobody adopts or declines a recommendation in time
- **WHEN** a recommendation's listing passes its due time with no adoption or decline
- **THEN** openChart escalates it to the medical director and keeps it open

#### Scenario: Failure: an act claims that a clinician approved it
- **WHEN** an `act.write` carries, in its content or its evidence, a statement that a named clinician approved it
- **THEN** openChart records it as the AI's recommendation, because it carries no sign-off of that clinician's own made as ruling 151 states

#### Scenario: Failure: the write path would be enabled before the recommendation record exists
- **WHEN** a plan proposes enabling the write path for a practice before the foundation's recommendation record is built
- **THEN** the plan is out of scope, because an `act.write` could then be neither recorded, as this requirement demands, nor refused
