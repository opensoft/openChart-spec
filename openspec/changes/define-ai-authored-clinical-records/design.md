## Context

openChart is an original Frappe v15 application, `open_chart`, kept in `opensoft/openChart-code`. Its canonical, ratified capability is [`openspec/specs/expanded-patient-intake/spec.md`](../../specs/expanded-patient-intake/spec.md).

What is built today, and what is not:

- **Built:** `OC Patient`, `OC Patient External Identifier` (namespaced, versioned, with an `identity_review` status), `OC Intake Submission` and eight patient-reported statement families, behind the versioned API `open_chart.api.v1`. Every statement is `patient_reported`.
- **Roles:** every built DocType grants permissions to `System Manager` only. No clinician, nurse or medical-director role exists. openChart-spec#5 (`define-clinical-acts-foundation`) defines them in AU-01 and AU-02, and Tier 1 waits for that part of it, its feature (1).
- **Not built:** there are no clinician encounters, orders, prescriptions, diagnoses of record or treatment plans in openChart, and nothing records AI-authored content. This change does not write as if they exist. Tier 2 waits for a prerequisite change that builds them, now drafted as openChart-spec#5 (ruling 72), and for a future governed amendment (ruling 67).

**Two draft openChart changes are related, and neither is on main.**
- `define-clinical-message-inbox` (openChart-spec#3, branch `change/define-clinical-message-inbox`) defines routed patient questions for the human doctor and the clinical communication record. Its MI-12 is the single definition of that record in openChart and names this change as one that references it; its MI-13 keeps AI-authored content distinguishable. Its MI-26 holds all AI-authored content as communication only "until `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` is ratified", and its Open Question 4 leaves openChart's receiving side of the AI system's governed chart path to a separate change. This change answers both (tensions AT-5 to AT-7). Under ruling 69 that change also owns openChart's one neutral conversation-retrieval contract, `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0`; this change defines no conversation retrieval.
- `define-openpractice-boundary` (openChart-spec#2, branch `change/define-openpractice-boundary`) provides the patterns reused here: digest-pinned schemas (PL-04), a durable outbound store (PL-14), no PHI in diagnostics (PL-13), and standalone operation (PL-15). Its Open Question 2 asks which change introduces clinician-authored encounters, diagnoses and procedures; the prerequisite named here is that change.

**Cross-references, not dependencies of the text.** `MedxSoft/medXfactory` change `define-patient-medical-ai-connection` is drafted and not yet on main. It owns the medical-AI mode framework and its patient connection, and lists this change as a required companion. `MedxSoft/MedxEHR-spec` change `define-medical-ai-record-overlay` is the Medx-specific overlay on this contract. Nothing in this change depends on either system's internals. Under ruling 70 the medical AI's records reach openChart through MedxEHR, which relays them with receipts, and medXfactory does not write to openChart directly; the write path admits such a relay without naming it (AW-02, AD-30).

**Constitution status.** openChart's `.specify/memory/constitution.md`, in the assembly root `opensoft/openChart`, is the unfilled Spec Kit template on `main` (checked at revision `d20de75`). There is nothing ratified to check against. This design therefore checks against openChart's `AGENTS.md` and the ratified `expanded-patient-intake` requirements. Planning is blocked until a constitution is adopted.

**The gates (same session).** Each mode stays off until (a) the medical director has approved that mode's scope, (b) a legal and regulatory review covering that mode is recorded, and (c) for limited and full practice, the chart can accept an AI-authored order, prescription or diagnosis recorded with the authority it acted under. Item (c) is what this change specifies. Ruling 67 adds (d) for the two practice modes: a future governed amendment, made after advice-only has run and counsel's review is in, that puts the mode in force. AG-04 encodes all four.

**What the 2026-11-15 rulings change.** Ruling 65 makes design call AD-1 binding: openChart holds its own record of the medical director's authorization and checks every AI write against it, rather than trusting the AI system's claim. Ruling 66 defines advice-only content: information, an assessment and next steps, with no diagnosis language and no treatment directives, delivered without prior sign-off under the medical director's sampled review (AC-04, AC-05, AC-07). Ruling 67 keeps the ratified texts and leaves the practice modes specified but off. Tier 1 is therefore the only tier in scope to build, and Tier 2 stays out of force until both the clinical-acts foundation exists and a future governed amendment puts Tier 2 in force (AA-01, AG-04); meanwhile, under ruling 80, every act an AI sends is recorded as a recommendation with `tier2_not_in_force`. Ruling 72 records that the foundation is being drafted; it is now openChart-spec#5, whose clinical roles and authority (AU-01 to AU-05) Tier 1 also relies on.

**What the second batch of 2026-11-15 changes.** Ruling 81 defines an advice-only answer's next steps as how to get care and general self-care information (see your doctor, book a visit, go to urgent care), never a treatment instruction for the patient's own condition (AC-04, AC-05, AG-03). Ruling 84 reads AT-1's ratified sentence narrowly: it bars automatic creation from intake, and an alert to the clinician is not a treatment recommendation; a small clarifying amendment to the ratified text, which this change does not carry, records that reading. Together they dispose the residual reading under AT-1 for Tier 1; Tier 2 stays off under ruling 67. Ruling 90 keeps AD-33's quarantine scope: every consult that fails a grant check is quarantined, mismatches included, because whatever the patient was told should be in front of a clinician (AC-11). Ruling 95 makes the chart's grant the root, which the AI system's configuration can only narrow, and a pause in the chart stops non-urgent answering at once, as AD-1 and AG-08 already hold. Rulings 91, 92 and 96 are context here.

**What ruling 80 and ruling 151 change.** Ruling 80 replaces the earlier position that an AI could never write to the chart: anyone or anything the chart can identify may add to it, every addition records where it came from, and only a decision by someone authorized to make it counts as a decision. For this change, every write names its writer and the AI on whose behalf it was written, by its openWallet ID with the model and model version (AW-02). Every record kept from a write carries its provenance, permanently (AW-15). Missing authority never refuses a write (AW-06). An AI act attempted without authority, which today is every act, is recorded in full as a recommendation marked "not an authorized decision", with no effect, in front of a clinician who may adopt or decline it (AW-16, AA-01 to AA-12). A consult that fails a grant check is still quarantined, as ruling 90 requires, and is answered `quarantined` rather than `refused` (AC-11). Ruling 151 adds that every medical decision needs the practitioner's own sign-off, with their openWallet and openSig, in a logged-in session; on the write path, an AI's addition never becomes a decision through anything the AI system or its relay sends (AW-05, AW-16). Decision 14 sets out the encoding.

## Ruling 80

Source: Brett Heap, product owner, 2026-11-15. This repository is public, so parts 1 to 4 of the record of the ruling are reproduced below with the approved wording in its form for public repositories: two sentences that describe a rule of a private companion repository are replaced by bracketed notes, and the full wording is recorded in the private companion changes. Everything else is identical. Clauses are cited as "ruling 80, clause N".

### 1. The owner's statement (his words, verbatim)

"for 80, we want the ai or any system to be able to write into a chart. our july rule is not good. lets change the rule so that any system or human that adds to a chart must be identifies. for the ai, that is the openWallet. for a device, that should be make/model/serial or a medical asset/device ID. This is what is important that we have the timestamp and where the information came from, what added it. Then if the added item to a chart is a decision, then that needs to be regulated by the practice authority of the decision maker. if a system is adding this on behalf of another system or human, that needs to be documented. if the human or system making the decision is not authorized to make that clinical decision, then it still is entered into the chart, but as recomendation not decision and clearly identified as not authorized decision."

### 2. His instruction (verbatim)

"for 80, reorganize this and make it more complete. then bring to me to read and approve"

### 3. The approved wording

He approved the wording below, exactly as it was put to him, with the words "approve 80. accept your recs for 136-150" (2026-11-15). Clauses marked *(added)* were added in the reorganization he asked for; everything else restates his statement. The marks are part of what he approved and stay.

**The rule in one line:** anyone or anything the chart can identify may add to it, the chart records exactly where each addition came from, and only a decision by someone authorized to make it counts as a decision.

**A. Who may add**
1. Any human or system may add to a patient's chart: a clinician, other staff, the patient, an AI, a device or another system. [One sentence of the approved wording stands here. It names an earlier rule of a private companion repository, which this ruling replaces in part, and is recorded in full in that repository's change.]
2. Nothing is added unless the chart can identify what is adding it. An addition it cannot identify is refused, and nothing is stored. *(added)*

**B. How each is identified**

| Who or what | Identified by |
|---|---|
| A clinician or other staff member | Their own named account in the chart *(added)* |
| The patient or their representative | The verified link they come through *(added)* |
| An AI | Its openWallet ID, plus the model and version that produced the content *(model and version added)* |
| A device | Its make, model and serial number, or a medical asset or device ID the chart can resolve to them |
| Any other system: an app, an integration, a relay | Its registered system identity and who operates it *(added)* |

3. Every system is registered with the practice before it can add, and the practice can suspend any system or device at once. *(added)*

**C. What every addition records**

4. **When:** the time the chart received it, and the time the information itself arose where that differs, such as when a reading was taken.
5. **Where the information came from:** the patient's own words, a device reading, an AI consult, a clinician's observation, another organization's record or an earlier chart entry, with a reference to it.
6. **What added it:** the human or system that wrote it into the chart.
7. **On whose behalf:** when a system adds something for another system or a human, the chart records both, and every hand it passed through.
8. This record is permanent and visible to anyone who can read the chart. Nothing in it is edited or deleted; a correction is a new dated addition that points to the old one. *(added)*

**D. Information and decisions**

9. Every addition is either information or a decision. *(added)*
   - Information is an observation, a reading, a statement, a message, a summary or a recommendation.
   - A decision changes the patient's care or what the chart holds as established: a diagnosis, an order, a prescription, a referral, a treatment plan or a sign-off.
   - The chart keeps the list of what counts as a decision.
10. Information needs only the identification above.
11. A decision counts as a decision only when its decision maker held the practice authority for that kind of decision at that moment. The chart records the authority each decision rested on.
    - For a human, that is their licence and what the medical director has granted them.
    - For an AI, it is the mode and scope the medical director has approved for it.
    - A device or a plain integration holds none.
12. When a system adds a decision on someone's behalf, authority is judged on the decision maker, never on the system that carried it. The decision counts as theirs only when the chart can confirm they made it, by their own sign-off or an approval of theirs it can check. Otherwise it is entered as the carrying system's recommendation. *(added)*

**E. A decision made without authority**

13. It is still entered in the chart, in full, as a recommendation and not a decision. Wherever it is shown, it is clearly marked "not an authorized decision" and says which authority was missing.
14. It has no effect. Nothing is ordered, prescribed, sent, billed or treated as established because of it. *(added)*
15. Whoever or whatever submitted it is told at once that it was entered as a recommendation. *(added)*
16. It goes in front of a clinician who holds the authority. They can adopt it, which creates a new decision of their own that points back to it, or decline it. Either way the recommendation stays in the chart as written. *(added)*
17. This applies to humans and systems alike: a staff member who enters an order they are not authorized to give is treated the same way as an AI.

**F. What does not change**

18. Missing authority is never a reason to refuse an addition. These still are: it cannot be identified, the patient cannot be matched, the content is malformed or test data, or the practice has suspended the writer. *(added)*
19. An AI holds no decision authority today. Advice only is the only mode that can be switched on, and limited and full practice stay specified and off (ruling 67). So everything an AI adds now is information or a recommendation.
20. An AI never manages an emergency, and the urgent hand-off to a human stays as it is.
21. Consent, the patient's link, the medical director's pause and narrowing, and clinician review of what an AI told a patient all still apply.
22. [Clause 22 of the approved wording stands here. It keeps two other parts of that private rule as they are, does not bear on the chart, and is recorded in full in the private companion change.]

### 4. What it overrides

It supersedes, for additions to the chart, the first sentence of ruling 67 as the private companion change records it. The rest of ruling 67 stands: limited and full practice stay specified and off.

| information, decision | Every addition is one or the other; the chart's list of decisions is the foundation's act registry. A consult record is information (ruling 80, clause 9; AC-04). |
| recommendation | An AI act attempted without authority, recorded in full on the foundation's recommendation record, marked `not_authorized_decision` with a coded `missing_authority`, with no effect, and answered `recorded_as_recommendation` (ruling 80, clauses 13 to 17; AW-16). |
| sign-off | A practitioner's sign-off of a medical decision, made in their own logged-in session with their openWallet and openSig; the foundation's rule (ruling 151). Adopting a recommendation is one (AW-16). |

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Give openChart a neutral, versioned write path for records from an external clinical AI system operating under a declared authority.
- Make Tier 1, the AI consult record, buildable without any orders model, on the foundation's clinical roles and authority (openChart-spec#5 feature (1)), so that advice-only mode can be switched on once its gates are met.
- Specify Tier 2's authorship, authority, co-review and reversal layer now, so the clinical-acts foundation can be built to receive it, while Tier 2 stays out of force (ruling 67).
- Make the chart, not the AI system, the judge of what authority a write rests on, and fail closed.
- Identify every writer and the AI on whose behalf it writes, record where every addition came from, and record what an AI attempts without authority as a recommendation rather than refuse it (ruling 80).
- Keep every ratified `expanded-patient-intake` requirement intact and keep openChart usable with no AI system at all.

**Non-Goals:**

- Building clinician encounters, orders, prescriptions, diagnoses or treatment plans. That is the prerequisite change.
- The AI system's internals, its models, its vendors or its patient-facing behaviour.
- Stating any legal or regulatory rule. This change states what the recorded review must cover.
- Billing. CF-02, CF-05 and CF-06 of `define-openpractice-boundary` are unchanged.
- Changing any ratified text, or adopting the constitution inside this change.

### 1. Two tiers

### 2. The contract

`openchart-ai-clinical-record-write/1.0` is owned by this change (AW-01). The schema follows the convention of the openPractice-spec contracts that `define-openpractice-boundary` and `define-clinical-message-inbox` pin: JSON Schema draft 2020-12, a URN `$id`, `oneOf` over the messages, every object closed, and `contracts/SHA256SUMS`. The one open object is `act_content.content`, reserved for the foundation's content schemas (AD-17).

| Message | Direction | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `consult.write` | AI system → openChart | Tier 1 consult record (AC-01) |
| `act.write` | AI system → openChart | Tier 2 act; until the foundation exists and a future governed amendment puts Tier 2 in force, recorded as a recommendation with `missing_authority` `tier2_not_in_force` (AA-01, AW-16) |
| `authority.notice` | both | A change in authority, or from openChart a change in the source's standing; from the AI system only a reduction is accepted (AG-08, AW-13) |
| `write.receipt` | openChart → AI system | The answer to every write and notice (AW-06) |
| `act.status` | openChart → AI system | Co-review outcome, reversal, hand-off, cancellation, escalation (AA-07 to AA-11) |
| `consult.reviewed` | openChart → AI system | Review outcome as a code (AC-08) |
| `record.readback_request` | AI system or its relay → openChart | Asks what openChart stored for a record it wrote (AW-12) |
| `record.readback` | openChart → AI system or its relay | The stored record's identifiers, content digest, `received_at`, grant version, state and review status, or a recommendation's or quarantined consult's code; no content (AW-12) |
| `grant.readback_request` | AI system or its relay → openChart | Heartbeat: asks for the state of the source's own authority, with no content (AW-14) |
| `grant.readback` | openChart → AI system or its relay | `grant_current` or `grant_not_current` with its reason, with `source_status`; or `unauthorized` (AW-14) |
| `message.ack` | both | Acknowledges a status or notice |

The codes are closed, and AW-06 fixes the order in which they are checked.
- **Refusal reasons** (`reason`; nothing is stored): `schema_invalid`, `unsupported_major_version`, `unauthorized`, `conflicting_duplicate`, `non_operational_context`, `content_digest_mismatch`, `patient_link_unknown`, `patient_link_mismatch`, `patient_link_in_review` and, for a notice, `notice_would_widen`.
- **Missing authority** (`missing_authority`, on a recommendation): `tier2_not_in_force`, `act_type_unsupported`, the nine grant checks (`no_current_grant`, `grant_paused`, `grant_expired`, `grant_revoked`, `grant_mismatch`, `mode_mismatch`, `mode_not_permitted`, `approver_mismatch` and `legal_review_mismatch`), and `emergency_management_refused`, `reserved_act_refused`, `targets_clinician_record`, `allowlist_entry_unknown`, `allowlist_entry_mismatch` and `conditions_unverified`.
- **Quarantine cause** (`quarantine_cause`, on a quarantined consult): the nine grant checks and `source_cap_suspended`.

Under ruling 80 no authority code is a refusal reason, and the schema refuses a `refused` receipt that carries one (AD-56).

`contracts/examples/` holds 77 synthetic fixtures: 26 that must validate and 51 that must fail, each for one stated reason. The self-test also checks that every valid write names its parties in AW-02's order, which the schema cannot check. `contracts/examples/conformance/` holds 5 more, each valid against the schema but a message openChart never sends (AW-14, AD-45, AD-47). They are part of the contract-test suite (AW-11).

**Known limits of the 1.0 schema.** The schema admits three contradictory shapes that AW-14's conformance rules exclude: a readback with `no_grant` that carries a grant; a readback whose reason disagrees with its grant's state, such as `grant_paused` with state `active`; and a standing notice that carries `new_mode` or `removed_allowlist_entry_ids`, such as `suspension_lifted` with `new_mode` `full_practice`. The schema also does not require `grant_version` on a grant-change notice, which AW-14 says openChart always sends (AD-47). The next revision of the schema should exclude those shapes and require `grant_version`. The ruling-80 revision of the draft 1.0 schema (AD-48) changed only what ruling 80 requires and left these limits as they were (AD-45).

**The ruling-80 revision.** The draft 1.0 schema changed for ruling 80 before ratification, from SHA-256 `97db018318a8f4a1e233e8416c7f07b908d4f55be1e4f995c0e0eba6737c91bc` to `5b429da63267e84011e883027f942298351ff206715aa032e6aebb7a0119de83`. It breaks three kinds of message that were valid before: every `consult.write` and `act.write`, which must now carry `added_by`, `on_behalf_of` and `source`; every `write.receipt` `refused` with an authority code, among them the earlier receipt of a quarantined consult and of every act while Tier 2 is not in force; and every `record.readback` `found`, which must now carry `received_at`. It also adds receipt results (`recorded_as_recommendation`, `quarantined`) and readback states (`recommendation`, `quarantined`) that a receiver validating against the earlier digest refuses. Every other message that was valid before is still valid. Every companion that pins the earlier digest must re-pin (AD-48).

### 3. openChart's own record of authority (ruling 65)

The chart keeps its own grant record and checks every write against it (AG-01, AD-1). The AI system's claim is compared with the chart's record and never substitutes for it. The alternative, accepting the AI system's signed statement of its mode, was rejected: a defect or compromise in the AI system would then create clinical acts in the chart with no independent check, and the chart could not show a clinician what authority a record rested on.

Only the medical director, as openChart-spec#5 AU-01 designates, approves, widens or resumes a grant (AG-02), and only that person records consult review grants (AG-11). A reduction applies at once from any source, the AI system's own notice included; a widening is only ever the medical director's act in openChart (AG-08, AD-6). Grants and legal reviews expire and never renew themselves (AG-09, AD-10).

### 4. Modes and gates

`advice_only` is the default (AG-03). A grant is activated only when the gates are met for its mode (AG-04): the medical director's approval of the mode's scope, a recorded legal and regulatory review covering that mode, and, for the practice modes, Tier 2 built over the foundation and a future governed amendment that puts the mode in force (ruling 67). No such amendment exists, so only `advice_only` can be approved. Ruling 63 is a build order: the modes are built advice, limited, full; enablement need not step through them, but a mode cannot be enabled before its build exists (AD-19).

### 5. How AI output is classified

| Content | Class | Becomes a clinical fact of record? |
|---|---|---|
| A patient's or representative's turn in a consult | Patient-reported, as the ratified intake requirement keeps it | No |
| An AI turn or summary in a consult | AI-generated communication (AC-04) | No, in any mode |
| Consult content that presents itself as an act | AI-generated communication, shown as not accepted as a clinical act (AC-05) | No |
| Diagnosis language or a treatment directive in advice-only content | AI-generated communication; always reviewed when declared `advice_scope_breach`, and a reviewer's breach finding alerts the medical director (AC-05) | No |
| An AI-authored act accepted under a current practice-mode grant | An act of record with author type `ai`, its authority stated, pending co-review (AA-02, AA-12) | Only once Tier 2 is in force, which ruling 67 defers to a future governed amendment, and only as the owner answers Open Question 8 under ruling 151 |
| An AI act that fails an authority check (today, every AI act) | A recommendation: recorded in full, marked `not_authorized_decision` with its `missing_authority`, listed with its content for the responsible clinician, with no effect (AW-16, AA-12) | No. A clinician may adopt it, which creates a decision of the clinician's own, signed off under ruling 151 |
| An AI act refused at steps 1 to 6 of AW-06 | A refusal record with identifiers and codes only (AW-06, AA-12) | No |
| A consult that fails a grant check | Quarantined AI communication, labelled with its coded cause, always reviewed within a fixed window, with fallback recipients; answered `quarantined` (AC-11) | No |
| A clinician's amendment, or a countermand to `discontinued` or `cancelled` | A clinician-authored record | Yes |

### 6. The Tier 1 consult record and the inbox

The consult record is the clinical communication record that MI-12 defines, cited by requirement ID, with origin `ai_consult` (AC-01, AD-3). It holds the full exchange, each turn with its MI-12 author type, plus an optional AI summary labelled as such (AD-2). The alternative, a reference into the AI system plus a summary, was rejected for now: ruling 57 puts every consult "in the chart", a summary written by the same AI would be the doctor's only view of its own advice, and a reference fails when the AI system is unreachable or its store changes.

An inbox item that names the same conversation is associated with the consult record by the conversation reference. The inbox still files no copy (MI-24). Writing a consult record needs neither the inbox enabled nor openPractice, because ruling 53 keeps the AI's exchanges away from openPractice (tension AT-6). This change defines no conversation retrieval: that is `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0`, which ruling 69 assigns to `define-clinical-message-inbox`.

### 7. Review (ruling 57)

openChart selects consults for review, not the AI system (AC-07). The review policy, set by the medical director as part of the grant, has a sample rate, always-review categories and a review window (AG-10). Six built-in triggers cannot be removed: the emergency indicator, no evidence reference at all, a linked act attempt, refused or recorded as a recommendation, or held act content, the declared codes `red_flag` and `advice_scope_breach`, and quarantine (AD-11). A consult that fails a grant check is quarantined, not dropped: its answer already reached the patient, so it stays visible to the patient's doctor (AC-11, AD-33). openChart does not parse consult text to enforce ruling 66. A reviewer who finds diagnosis language or a treatment directive records the finding `advice_scope_breach`, which alerts the medical director at once (AC-08, AD-31). A named clinician holding a consult review grant (AG-11) records the review status, reviewer and outcome (AC-08). An overdue review escalates to the medical director (AD-13). An `unsafe` outcome alerts the medical director at once.

### 8. Tier 2 (specified, out of force under ruling 67): acceptance, reserved acts and emergencies

An act is accepted only under a current practice-mode grant that it matches in every field (AA-03). A limited-practice act must match its allowlist entry, protocol and checkable conditions (AA-04, AD-16). An act that fails any of these checks is recorded as a recommendation, never refused (AW-16). A reserved act is never accepted in any mode unless the grant's legal review cleared it; reservedness is the stricter of openChart's classification and the AI system's declaration (AA-05, AD-15). An act marked as emergency management is never accepted, and no clearance can change that: it is recorded as a recommendation and listed urgently, and the urgent hand-off stands (AA-06; ruling 80, clause 20). A consult with an emergency indicator is recorded and shown urgently, because the record of what the patient was told matters (AC-10, AD-22).

### 9. Co-review and reversal (ruling 62)

Every accepted act enters the co-review queue at once with a due time from the grant's window (AA-07). A named clinician holding `cosign` scope for the act class under openChart-spec#5 AU-02 confirms, amends or voids it (AD-20). openChart-spec#5 CA-07 defines a co-signature model of its own, and the two converge on CA-07 at the Tier 2 amendment (tension AT-11). A void is the foundation's `entered_in_error` and a countermand its `discontinued` (or `cancelled` while `pending_release`). An AI prescription is accepted only on an `open` prescribing-safety gate decision for its exact content, never on the outage path (AA-03; openChart-spec#6 PG-01, ruling 76). An overdue item escalates to the medical director and is never closed by a timer (AA-08). A clinician can void or countermand an AI act; nothing is deleted, and corrections are new dated records (AA-09). An AI act never modifies, discontinues or supersedes clinician-authored content (AA-10, AD-8). Co-review would build on the foundation's signing and co-signature model (ruling 72); that mapping belongs to the future governed amendment. Under ruling 151 each co-review outcome, void and countermand is a practitioner's sign-off of a medical decision (AD-54); how an act that took effect before its co-review meets ruling 151 is Open Question 8.

### 10. Narrowing and pause

A narrowing or pause applies at once (AG-08). Pending AI acts that the grant no longer covers are held and handed to a named clinician, or cancelled by the medical director, and never released on the AI's authority (AA-11, AD-9). "Pending" is the foundation's `pending_release`: signed but not yet in force (its CT-7).

### 11. Identity, context and standalone operation

Every write names its writer and the AI on whose behalf it is written, by its openWallet ID, model and model version, and openChart refuses what it cannot identify (AW-02, AD-49). A write resolves the patient only through an existing `OC Patient External Identifier` row in the AI system's namespace (AW-03, AD-23). There is no demographic matching, no merge and no master identity. Only `operational` writes are recorded; `scenario` and `simulation` are refused with an explicit code (AW-04, AD-18). Every component is optional, and with no AI system registered the write path accepts nothing (AW-10).

### 12. Labels

Every display of an AI-authored record, to a clinician or a patient, labels it AI-authored and states its review or co-review status without implying a prior sign-off (AW-09). Every act shows the authority it rested on (AA-12). A consult record also carries the ruling-66 label: information, assessment and next steps only, not a diagnosis and not a treatment instruction (AC-04). A recommendation is marked "not an authorized decision" with the authority that was missing, and every record kept from a write shows its provenance (AW-09, AW-15).

### 13. What the grant readback reports (AW-14, AD-44)

The readback says `grant_current` only if a well-formed consult within the grant's mode and scope would pass the grant checks now. Each check below is cross-checked against the write outcome, the readback answer and the notice sent. In the Write outcome column, a grant-check code is a consult's `quarantine_cause` (AC-11) and an act's `missing_authority` (AW-16); neither is refused (ruling 80, clause 18). Only the suspensions and the deregistration refuse, at step 2.

| Check | Write outcome | Readback answer | Notice to the source |
|---|---|---|---|
| No AU-01 designation in force (AG-02) | `no_current_grant`: a consult is quarantined (AC-11), an act recorded as a recommendation (AW-16) | `grant_not_current`, `no_medical_director` | `no_medical_director`; restoration shows in the readback, and no notice is sent |
| No grant version in force for the time (none exists, or none has taken effect) | `no_current_grant` | `grant_not_current`, `no_grant`, no grant attached | None while none has existed; a grant that ends sends its own value below |
| The grant is `paused` (AG-08) | `grant_paused` | `grant_not_current`, `grant_paused`, grant in state `paused` | `paused`; `resumed` restores |
| The grant has passed its expiry (AG-09) | `grant_expired` | `grant_not_current`, `grant_expired`, grant in state `expired` | `expired`; a new version, sent as `superseded`, restores |
| The legal review the grant relies on has passed its expiry (AG-05, AG-09) | `grant_expired` | `grant_not_current`, `grant_expired`, grant in state `expired` | `expired`; a current review and a new version, sent as `superseded`, restore |
| The grant is `revoked` | `grant_revoked` | `grant_not_current`, `grant_revoked`, grant in state `revoked` | `revoked` |
| The write names another grant version, configuration version or source authority reference | `grant_mismatch` | Depends on the write. The readback carries the version in force, so the source can match it | `superseded` or `narrowed` when the version changes |
| The write's mode differs from the grant's | `mode_mismatch` | Depends on the write. The readback carries the mode | `narrowed`, with `new_mode`, when the mode is lowered |
| The write is not permitted in the mode | `mode_not_permitted` | Depends on the write (an act under `advice_only`) | None |
| A named approver or legal review differs | `approver_mismatch`, `legal_review_mismatch` | Depends on the write | None |
| Scope removed by a narrowing (AG-08) | The code of the check the write fails | The narrowed version in force | `narrowed` |
| Manual suspension (AW-13), not a grant check | `unauthorized` at step 2 | `unauthorized` | `suspended`; `suspension_lifted` and then a `grant_current` readback restore |
| Cap suspension (AW-13), not a grant check | `unauthorized` at step 2 for a non-urgent consult and every act; an urgent consult is quarantined and answered `quarantined` | The grant answer, with `source_status` `cap_suspended` | `cap_suspended`; `suspension_lifted` |
| Deregistration (AW-13), not a grant check | `unauthorized` | `unauthorized` | `deregistered`, the last notice |

The readback reports the present. A consult is still judged against the grant in force at its `ended_at` (AC-09), so the readback bounds new work and is not a verdict on consults that have already ended.

### 14. Ruling 80 and ruling 151 on the write path

Ruling 80 (section "Ruling 80") means five things for this change:
1. **Identification.** Every write names its writer, `added_by`, which is the registered integration identity that delivers it, and in `on_behalf_of` the AI that produced the content, by its openWallet ID with the model and model version, then, for a relay, the source system. openChart checks the writer against its authentication and the AI's openWallet ID against the registration, and refuses at step 2 what it cannot identify (AW-02, AD-49; ruling 80, clauses 2, 3, 6 and 7).
2. **Provenance.** Every record kept from a write states `received_at`, `occurred_at`, `source`, `added_by` and `on_behalf_of`, permanently (AW-15, AD-50; clauses 4 to 8).
3. **Missing authority never refuses.** Only an identification, integrity, context or patient-link failure, or a suspended writer, refuses a write and stores nothing (AW-06 steps 1 to 6; clause 18). An act that fails an authority check (steps 7 to 9) is recorded in full as the foundation's recommendation record, marked `not_authorized_decision` with its `missing_authority`, answered `recorded_as_recommendation`, listed with its content for the responsible clinician, and has no effect (AW-16, AD-51; clauses 13 to 17). While Tier 2 is not in force, that is every act an AI sends.
4. **A consult record is information.** A consult that fails a grant check is kept in full as a quarantined consult, as ruling 90 requires, and is answered `quarantined`, no longer `refused` (AC-11, AD-52). Consult content that reads like an act is not additionally marked `not_authorized_decision` (AC-05, AD-53).
5. **What does not change.** Tier 2 stays specified and off (AA-01; ruling 67, second sentence; clause 19). An AI never manages an emergency: an emergency act is recorded as a recommendation and listed urgently, and the urgent hand-off stands (AA-06; clause 20). Grants, modes, gates, pause and narrowing, consent, the patient link and consult review all stay (clause 21).

Ruling 151 adds that every medical decision needs the practitioner's sign-off, made by a logged-in user with their openWallet and openSig. On the write path, an AI's addition never becomes a decision through anything the AI system or its relay sends (AW-05, AW-16). Adopting a recommendation is such a sign-off, made through the foundation's path [CAF-REQ: sign-off]. A consult review is a review record, not a sign-off, and a co-review outcome of an AI act would be a sign-off (AD-54). Tier 2's design, an AI act accepted under a practice-mode grant and co-reviewed afterwards, sits against "any medical decision we need the practitioner to sign off". This change does not resolve that: it is Open Question 8, for the owner to answer before limited or full practice is ever switched on.

**Placeholders for the foundation's requirement IDs.** Ruling 80 gives the foundation, `define-clinical-acts-foundation` (openChart-spec#5), the general rule for every addition to the chart. Its text for ruling 80 does not exist yet, so this change cites it by the placeholders below, each to be replaced by the foundation's requirement ID (task 1.6). The field names are the ones every draft uses.

| Placeholder | The foundation's rule it stands for | Field names |
|---|---|---|
| `[CAF-REQ: identification]` | Who may add, and how each writer is identified (ruling 80, clauses 1 to 3) | `kind`, `openwallet_id`, `model`, `model_version`, `identity_basis` |
| `[CAF-REQ: provenance]` | What every addition records (clauses 4 to 8) | `received_at`, `occurred_at`, `source`, `added_by`, `on_behalf_of` |
| `[CAF-REQ: information-and-decisions]` | Information and decisions, and the chart's list of decisions (clauses 9 to 12) | `entry_class` |
| `[CAF-REQ: recommendation-record]` | A decision made without authority (clauses 13 to 15 and 17) | `recommendation`, `not_authorized_decision`, `missing_authority`, `recorded_as_recommendation` |
| `[CAF-REQ: adopt-or-decline]` | Adopting or declining a recommendation (clause 16) | none named |
| `[CAF-REQ: sign-off]` | The practitioner's sign-off of a medical decision (ruling 151) | none named |

### 15. What the public openWallet and openSig specifications settle

Ruling 151 names opensoft/openWallet and opensoft/openSig, both public. This change cites them and adds nothing to them. As read on 2026-11-15:
- **openWallet** (assembly root opensoft/openWallet; specification leg opensoft/openWallet-spec at `a2d554d`, canonical capabilities `openxwallet` and `openxwallet-agent-profile`). A wallet is a signing key, or a declared set of signing keys, anchored to decentralized identifiers and held by a holder of any subject class: "a person, a practitioner, an organisation, or an agent". Exercising a grant requires a signature from the holder's wallet key over the request (requirement "Use requires proof of possession, not presentation"). What a signature evidences is bounded by the custody of the key that signed: only custody that isolates the key from the holder's own execution context evidences that the holder acted. A holder of class agent declares its composition, including its model version, and any change of composition revokes its grants at once. Requirement "The capability is an authority control, never an identity substrate" says that "a wallet identifier SHALL NOT become a subject identifier", and that a wallet never becomes a prerequisite for reconstructing a record, resolving a subject or operating a domain. The specification defines no verification of a professional licence; its agent profile says that patient and practitioner profiles are to arrive as sibling profiles, and none is published.
- **openSig** (assembly root opensoft/openSig; specification leg opensoft/openSig-spec at `23ebbfe`). The specification leg holds no requirements yet. What an openSig attestation is, what it is made over, and how a relying party verifies it are not specified.

What they leave open is recorded as Open Questions 9 to 12, and the openWallet rule on identifiers as tension AT-16. Nothing in this change waits on an answer: an AI holds no decision authority (ruling 80, clause 19), and the sign-off is the foundation's rule.

## What the recorded legal and regulatory review must cover

This change states no legal or regulatory rule. For each mode, the review that the medical director records (AG-05) must address at least these topics. Whether each topic is satisfied, and how, is for counsel and the medical director, not for this change.

1. The mode, and the act classes and act types in its scope.
2. The jurisdictions where the practice and its patients are located, and which rules apply in each.
3. For each act type, whether it may be the AI system's own act, under what conditions, and on whose licence or authority it is made.
4. Which acts must remain a licensed clinician's own act (the reserved list), including an explicit finding on controlled-substance prescribing.
5. Supervision, delegation, standing-order and protocol requirements, and whether after-the-fact co-review and the proposed window are acceptable.
6. Disclosure to patients and their consent for AI consults and AI acts, and the wording of the patient-facing labels.
7. Identification of the author of an AI act on any record or transmission that leaves the practice, such as a prescription or an order.
8. Record-keeping, attribution and retention of AI-authored records.
9. Accountability: who is the responsible clinician of record for an AI-authored act.
10. Emergency handling and escalation duties.
11. Privacy and security requirements for the write path.
12. The review's expiry, and the events that require re-review (a new act type, a new jurisdiction, a model change, a widening).

## Required prerequisite change: the clinical-acts foundation

**`define-clinical-acts-foundation`. Required; drafted (ruling 72)** by another author as openChart-spec#5, branch `change/define-clinical-acts-foundation`, not yet ratified. This change does not edit it. Its feature (1), clinical authority (AU-01 to AU-06), is also a prerequisite of Tier 1, for the clinical roles and authority of AU-01 to AU-05 (AG-02, AG-11), and so is its recommendation record (item 13). Tier 2 is not buildable, and neither practice mode can be enabled, until it is ratified and built and a future governed amendment puts Tier 2 in force (ruling 67). It is also the clinical-encounter capability that `define-openpractice-boundary` Open Question 2 waits for. Ruling 72 says it accepts only `clinician` as author type and provides the signing and co-signature model that Tier 2 builds on. What this change needs of it:

1. **Clinician encounters**, with participants, period and status.
2. **Orders** (for example laboratory, imaging, referral and procedure), with the foundation's closed lifecycle `draft`, `pending_release`, `released`, `completed`, `discontinued`, `cancelled` and `entered_in_error` (CA-03, OR-02). Signing is an event and a signature (CA-04), not a status (its CT-7).
3. **Prescriptions**, with the same lifecycle and a classification that marks controlled substances (RX-01, RX-03). 4. **Diagnoses of record** (problem list and encounter diagnoses), with verification status, kept distinct from patient-reported conditions.

7. **An act-type registry:** act classes and act types as data, with flags for reserved acts and emergency management, which allowlist entries and AA-05 and AA-06 use.
8. **Roles:** clinician, nurse and medical director, with scope per act class. openChart-spec#5 provides them in AU-01 and AU-02, which this change cites as the single definition (AG-02, AG-11).
9. **Amendment, entered-in-error marks and discontinuation** as new dated records (CA-05, CA-06, OR-02); nothing edited in place or deleted.
10. **Content schemas** for each act class, published so that a MINOR version of `openchart-ai-clinical-record-write` can pin them for `act_content`.
11. **Reconciliation with the ratified intake text** that tension AT-1 records, because clinician orders created through an API meet the same sentence.
12. **Billing interplay** with CF-02 and CF-06 of `define-openpractice-boundary`.
13. **Ruling 80's general rule for every addition**, which ruling 80 gives to the foundation: identification, provenance, information and decisions, the recommendation record, adopting or declining a recommendation, and, under ruling 151, the practitioner's sign-off. This change cites each by a placeholder (Decision 14). The recommendation record is also a prerequisite of Tier 1, because the write path cannot be enabled before it exists (AW-16, AD-55).

openChart-spec#5 maps each item in its own minimum-contents table: item 3 is provided in part (its CT-8) and item 5 is not provided (its CT-6). **Further prerequisites for parts of Tier 2:** a later treatment-plan change for `treatment_plan_change` (ruling 73), and `define-prescribing-safety-checks` (openChart-spec#6), with its gate `open`, for any AI prescription (ruling 74; AA-03).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **[An AI act rests on authority the chart cannot verify]** → The chart's own grant record (AD-1), checked field by field; on any mismatch the act is recorded as a recommendation with no effect (AW-16).
- **[The AI system keeps acting after a pause]** → A pause in either system applies at once in openChart (AD-6); no later write is accepted: consults that happened after the pause are kept as quarantined AI communication, always reviewed and listed for the responsible clinician (AC-11), and acts are recorded as recommendations (AW-16).
- **[AI text is mistaken for a clinician's]** → An author type on every turn and act, labels on every surface, the patient's included.
- **[A recommendation is taken for a decision]** → The `not_authorized_decision` marker and the missing authority on every display; no act status, no co-review, never signed, released, transmitted, published or billed, never counted in a check; only a clinician's own decision, signed off under ruling 151, takes effect (AW-16).
- **[A source floods recommendations]** → Each is recorded, but its listings fall under the per-source ceiling and digest (AW-13), and the medical director can suspend the source manually.
- **[An unsafe answer reaches a patient]** → Ruling 57 accepts delivery without sign-off; the review sample, the built-in triggers and the `unsafe` alert are the safety net.
- **[Advice content strays into diagnosis or treatment]** → The source system's own control and declaration, the built-in triggers, the sample and the reviewer's breach finding (AD-31). Whatever the text says, the record is never a fact of record.
- **[A deregistered source's patient goes unlisted]** → Accepted residual (AD-43): a deregistered or unregistered caller cannot raise urgent listings; that source must hand off itself.
- **[A source keeps advising under a lapsed legal review]** → The readback is computed from every grant check that does not depend on a write's content, and a lapsed review sends `expired` (AD-44).
- **[A stale or contradictory message widens a source]** → Conformance rules on what openChart sends, and reader's rules that treat a contradiction as not current, order readbacks after notices and notices among themselves, and widen only on a fresh readback (AD-45).
- **[Co-review backlog]** → Escalation to the medical director, who can narrow or pause.
- **[Two records of authority drift]** → The configuration version must match exactly; drift refuses writes rather than accepting them.
- **[Tier 1 waits on the inbox]** → AD-3; the alternative is a standalone record capability.
- **[A large consult record]** → Attachments by reference and digest; size limits in the schema.
- **[No constitution]** → Planning is blocked until a constitution is adopted.

## Open Questions

Each question is routed in `tasks.md` §2 to the gate it blocks.

1. **Constitution adoption.** openChart's constitution is the unfilled template. This blocks planning.
2. **Owner dispositions** of the tensions still open (AT-5, AT-6, AT-8, AT-9, AT-11 and AT-13 to AT-17) and of AD-2 to AD-56, among them the PROPOSED calls AD-48 to AD-56 that encode rulings 80 and 151. AD-1 is ruled (rulings 65 and 95); rulings 81 and 84 dispose the residual reading under AT-1; ruling 80 resolves AT-7 and AT-10 in substance, pending the inbox's and the foundation's text. These block ratification.
3. **The clinical-acts foundation.** Answered by ruling 72: another author drafted it as openChart-spec#5. Tier 1 needs its feature (1); Tier 2 also needs a future governed amendment (ruling 67).
4. **The responsible clinician.** openChart records no care team today. Which change defines a patient's responsible clinician? Meanwhile, AD-12's fallback to the medical director applies.
5. **The AI system's patient link.** Is it registered through the ratified external-identifier path, or through a governed link contract like PL-02's? Ruling 96 settles who registers it for the medical AI, its relay at enrolment, but not the path. Meanwhile, AD-23 applies.
6. **Retention** of consult records, refusal records and integration exceptions. The owner and counsel decide. Meanwhile, nothing is deleted.
7. **Who routes to the human doctor the patient of an act recorded as a recommendation** (AT-9). The AI system's change decides. Meanwhile, the recommendation, with its content, is listed for a clinician who may adopt or decline it (ruling 80, clause 16).
8. **Tier 2 under ruling 151, for the owner to answer before limited or full practice is ever switched on.** Tier 2's design accepts an AI act under a practice-mode grant and has a clinician co-review it afterwards (AA-03, AA-07; ruling 62). Ruling 80, clause 11 says that an AI's authority would be the mode and scope the medical director approves; ruling 151 says "any medical decision we need the practitioner to sign off". This change does not resolve that, and the future governed amendment of ruling 67 must carry the owner's answer. Meanwhile, Tier 2 is off and every AI act is a recommendation (AA-01, AW-16).
9. **What "the openWallet will have the verification of license" is.** openWallet's public specification defines no verification of a professional licence, and its practitioner profile is not published (Decision 15). Who issues the verification, what it covers, how the chart reads it, and how it relates to the attestations the practice records (AT-17) are open. The sign-off is the foundation's rule; this change needs the answer only for Tier 2's co-review (AD-54). Meanwhile, consult review rests on the AG-11 grant.
10. **What an openSig attestation is.** openSig's public specification leg holds no requirements yet (Decision 15). What it attests, over what content (for example an entry's content digest and its provenance), and how the chart verifies it are open. Meanwhile, nothing on the write path depends on it.
11. **The AI's openWallet ID.** openWallet publishes no format for a wallet's ID, and its canonical specification says a wallet identifier never becomes a subject identifier (AT-16). Is an AI's openWallet ID a stable identifier that the chart may record and compare, and in what form? Meanwhile, openChart treats it as opaque, compares it exactly with the registration, and uses it for nothing else (AD-49).
12. **The AI's model and version, and proof of possession.** openWallet's agent profile makes an agent's composition, its model version included, part of its identity, and revokes its grants when the composition changes; its core requires a signature from the holder's wallet key to exercise a grant. Should openChart check a write's `model` and `model_version` against the AI's declared composition, and should an AI's write carry a signature from its wallet? Meanwhile, openChart records the model and version as the writer states them, the AI's identity is asserted by its registered writer (AD-49), and a later MINOR version can add either check.
13. **Device readings.** Ruling 80 says how a device is identified and what a device reading records (part B, clause 4). No draft defines a path by which device data enters the chart, and this write path admits none: its writers are AI systems and their relays. That would be its own later change.
