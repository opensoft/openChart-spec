## Context

openChart is an original Frappe v15 application, `open_chart`, kept in `opensoft/openChart-code`. Its canonical, ratified capability is [`openspec/specs/expanded-patient-intake/spec.md`](../../specs/expanded-patient-intake/spec.md).

This change touches these facts about what is already built. They were checked at `opensoft/openChart-code` revision `30b9645`.

- **DocTypes.** The built DocTypes are `OC Patient`, `OC Patient External Identifier`, `OC Intake Submission`, and eight statement DocTypes: allergy, condition, document reference, goal, medication, observation, supplement and symptom. None holds a message, a clinician-authored record or a work queue.
- **Roles.** Every built DocType grants permissions to `System Manager` only. No clinician, nurse or medical-director role exists.
- **Events.** `emit_submission_event` uses `frappe.publish_realtime`, which never leaves the site.
- **Medx references.** The ratified intake keeps Medx and HealthLinc references as namespaced external identifiers. A test fixture, `tests/fixtures/medx/binding-03-openchart.yaml`, binds HealthLinc, OpenEMR and openChart references to one `medx_patient_id` ("gate G3"). See tension MT-12.

**The openPractice side** is `opensoft/openPractice-spec` change `define-patient-gateway-and-escalation`, merged by PR #6 and now on `opensoft/openPractice-spec` `main` at `0a82fc2`, still draft and unratified. This change pins it at revision `0a82fc2`. In this document it is called "the openPractice change", and its identifiers are cited as, for example, "openPractice ER-06". Its capability `escalation-routing` (ER-01 to ER-18) owns the contract `openpractice-clinical-handoff/1.0` and its canonical schema. That change's design lists this inbox as a required companion change, with twelve items; they are checked below under "The openPractice change's companion items".

**The conversation-retrieval contract,** `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0`, is owned by this change (ruling 69). Its canonical schema is `contracts/openchart-conversation-retrieval-1.0.schema.json` in this change's directory, listed in `contracts/SHA256SUMS`. HealthLinc and medXfactory implement it (Decision 14).

**Clinical roles and grants, and the general rule for additions,** come from `define-clinical-acts-foundation` (openChart-spec#5), another author's draft, read here at `d9a9c63`. Its clinical-authority capability, AU-01 to AU-07, is the single definition of clinical roles, the medical director and grants in openChart, and ruling 92 places the inbox's authority in AU-06 (Decision 15). Its capability `chart-additions`, AN-01 to AN-14, holds the rule for every addition to a patient's chart under rulings 80 and 151, and its CA-04 the practitioner's sign-off; the inbox follows both (Decision 16). **The AI write path,** `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` (openChart-spec#4), is cited by change id and requirement id only.

**This branch is stacked on `define-openpractice-boundary`.** That change is a draft, and this change cites its PL-01 to PL-16, CF-02, CF-05 and CF-06. This change can be ratified only with or after it.

Brett Heap's rulings are tabulated below. **Rulings are not ratification.** This change stays `Status: draft` until he stamps `Ratified by` in `proposal.md`.

**Constitution status.** openChart's `.specify/memory/constitution.md`, in the assembly root `opensoft/openChart`, is the unfilled Spec Kit template on `main` (checked at revision `d20de75`). There is nothing ratified to check against. This design therefore checks against openChart's `AGENTS.md` and the ratified `expanded-patient-intake` requirements. The plan phase waits on adopting a constitution.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Implement openChart's half of `openpractice-clinical-handoff/1.0` against openPractice's canonical schema, verified by digest.
- Make sure every routed clinical item has a named owner, an acknowledgment, a clock and an escalation, so that an urgent item is never blocked, dropped or left unowned.
- Keep clinical content out of openPractice: obtain conversations by reference from the system that holds them, and reply through HealthLinc.
- Own one neutral conversation-retrieval contract, `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0`, that every system holding patient conversations implements (ruling 69).
- Give openChart one citable definition of a clinical communication record (MI-12), for inbox items and for AI consult records (origin `ai_consult`). Keep AI-authored content always distinguishable from clinician-authored content, and never let a communication become a clinical fact of record.
- Keep every ratified `expanded-patient-intake` requirement intact, and keep openChart free of billing and financial status.
- Record every inbox record that enters a patient's chart as an addition with its provenance, under the foundation's general rule (ruling 80), and send no reply to a patient without the practitioner's sign-off (rulings 151 and 158).

**Non-Goals:**

- Encounters, diagnoses, procedures or billable facts. CF-06 is unchanged.
- AI drafting of clinical replies, or any agent action in the inbox.
- AI-authored orders, prescriptions or diagnoses, and the limited-practice and full-practice modes of ruling 59. They wait for openChart-spec change `define-ai-authored-clinical-records`, which is being drafted (ruling 64; Decision 13). Until its Tier 2 is in force for an act type, AI-authored content is information, or a recommendation with no effect (MI-26).
- An administrative work queue in openChart.
- Defining clinical roles, the medical director or grants. They come from `define-clinical-acts-foundation` AU-01 to AU-07 (Decision 15).
- Defining HealthLinc's or medXfactory's internals. Their sides are stated at contract level only: each implements `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0`.
- openChart's own link contract with HealthLinc (ruling 39), which needs its own change (Open Question 3).
- openChart's receiving side of MedxEHR's relay of the medical AI's records (ruling 70), which belongs to `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` (Open Question 4).
- Changing any ratified text, or adopting the constitution inside this change.

## Decisions

### 1. Contract reuse

openChart uses only the messages in the pinned schema, and defines no message of its own toward openPractice (MI-01). Toward the systems that hold conversations, it uses only its own retrieval contract (Decision 14).

| Message | Direction | openChart's use |
|---|---|---|
| `handoff.offer` | openPractice → openChart | Creates one inbox item per `handoff_id` (MI-04) |
| `handoff.ack` | openChart → openPractice | `accepted` once the item exists durably with a named owner and a running clock; `rejected` with a schema code (MI-04, MI-18) |
| `handoff.urgency_raised` | openPractice → openChart | Raises urgency and moves deadlines only earlier (MI-10) |
| `handoff.status` | openChart → openPractice | `responded`, `clock_expired`, `returned_not_clinical`, `content_unavailable`, `closed` (MI-06) |
| `clinical_need.marker` | openChart → openPractice | A clinician's attributed, coded clinical-need marker (MI-25) |
| `handoff.clinician_urgency_raised` | openChart → openPractice | A clinician's raise to `priority` or `urgent`, with openChart's new `respond_by` (MI-10) |
| `message.ack` | both | openChart acknowledges raises. openPractice acknowledges statuses and markers, with no `handoff_id` for a marker that names no handoff. |

The pinned schema carries `conversation_source`, `conversation_ref`, `clinical_need.marker` and the reject reason `origin_not_verified`. openPractice uses `origin_not_verified` to reject a marker whose urgent origin it cannot verify (openPractice ER-18). openChart never sends that reason.

The pin at `0a82fc2` also carries:
- `conversation_source` `healthlinc` for any conversation HealthLinc holds: a coach conversation, a direct message to the doctor or an administrative thread;
- the offer field `identity_state` (`linked`, `link_in_review` or `unlinked`). The schema allows `link_in_review` and `unlinked` only for `priority` and `urgent` offers;
- for an `unlinked` offer, `unlinked_identity`, with exactly `legal_name`, `birth_date` and `administrative_sex` from the `openpractice-ehr-patient-link/1.0` snapshot, and no `link_id` or `ehr_patient`;
- `handoff.clinician_urgency_raised`;
- the `handoff.urgency_raised` sources `coach_triage` and `medical_ai_triage`, which replace `healthlinc_triage`, and `patient_checklist`, a patient's raise through HealthLinc's red-flag checklist (ruling 71), alongside `staff`, `agent_proposal` and `routing_rule`.

`link_id` is optional on `handoff.ack`, `handoff.urgency_raised`, `handoff.status` and `handoff.clinician_urgency_raised`. `message.ack` must name a `handoff_id`, a `link_id` or both. openChart's messages about an `unlinked` handoff carry the `handoff_id` and no `link_id`; those about a handoff with a link carry both (MI-06).

### 3. Where the conversation comes from

The handoff is reference-only. openChart fetches the conversation by `conversation_ref` from the system that `conversation_source` names, never from openPractice, with `conversation.retrieve` under its own retrieval contract (MI-03; rulings 51 and 69; Decision 14):

| `conversation_source` | Held by | Reference | Retrieval |
|---|---|---|---|
| `healthlinc` | HealthLinc, for any conversation it holds: a coach conversation, a direct message to the doctor or an administrative thread | `conversation_ref`, namespace `healthlinc` | Required to enable the inbox |
| `medxfactory` | medXfactory, for a medical-AI conversation | `conversation_ref`, namespace `medxfactory` | Required where the practice uses medXfactory's medical AI (MD-12) |

The schema enforces that the namespace matches the source. `healthlinc_message_ref` is HealthLinc's reference for the patient's request. openChart keeps it in the record and uses it for split requests (MI-16).

The reply always goes to HealthLinc as `reply.deliver` against `reply_path_ref`, whichever system holds the conversation, as openPractice ER-05 also states (design call MD-16). The medical AI's records reach the chart only through MedxEHR, which relays them with receipts; medXfactory does not write to openChart directly (ruling 70, refining ruling 53). That write path belongs to `define-ai-authored-clinical-records`, and this change only cites it. For a medXfactory-held conversation, the inbox keeps per-turn metadata keyed by `turn_id`, the `snapshot_ref`, the `consent_ref` and the retrieval provenance, and never a copy of any turn's text (MI-12, MI-24, design calls MD-17 and MD-27).

### 4. Acceptance, clock and deadlines

`handoff.ack` `accepted` is system acceptance: the item exists durably, it has a named owner, and its clock is running (design call MD-1). It does not mean a clinician has read the item. Clinician acknowledgment is an explicit act inside openChart, and escalation runs on it (MI-09).

openChart owns the clock (ruling 43). Deadlines come from an openChart response policy that the medical director approves. Until one is approved, the offer's own `ack_by` and `respond_by` apply. After approval, the earlier of the policy's deadline and the offer's applies. Nothing ever extends a deadline (design call MD-2). The openPractice change's default windows (its design call DC-3) are proposed defaults in an unratified change, not facts. This change adopts no number of its own.

openPractice treats `clock_expired`, or silence past `respond_by`, as `clinical_response_overdue`. It then alerts the office manager and sends a tracked alert to its own on-call clinician (its ER-13). For an urgent item it also runs the fallback with emergency guidance (openPractice ER-07, ER-09). openChart therefore behaves as follows (MI-05, MI-06, design call MD-15):
- its effective deadline never falls after `respond_by`;
- it sends `clock_expired` the moment that deadline passes, and `responded` as soon as HealthLinc accepts the reply, each in the same transaction as its cause;
- for an `urgent` or `priority` item that nobody can own, it ends that deadline at once and sends `clock_expired` (MI-08, design call MD-34).

### 5. Ownership, cover and escalation

- Every item has exactly one named owner, who holds the foundation's `clinician` or `nurse` role (AU-01) and `own` over `clinical_inbox_item` for its lane, `physician` or `nurse`, under AU-06's lane rule (MI-07). The one exception is an item with no possible owner, because no available holder exists for its lane or its fallback lane (nobody holds `own` there with an enabled account and without being marked absent): it stays open, listed and re-alerted, and an `urgent` or `priority` one is reported `clock_expired` at once (MI-08, design call MD-34).
- Until a lane policy is approved and the medical director records the nurse-lane scope (AU-06), every item enters the physician lane (design call MD-3).
- openChart keeps its own cover roster, an ordered list per hour and lane, with the medical director as the last fallback. The inbox cannot be enabled with a gap, and a grant lapse that opens one is closed by MI-08's warnings, alerts and fallbacks (design call MD-4).
- An unacknowledged urgent item escalates to its next rostered holder, never back to the owner who failed to acknowledge, then to the medical director, and keeps re-alerting. It never drops, parks or closes (MI-09).
- An expired item stays queued, open and owned until a clinician replies or a holder of `close` records a closing outcome (MI-07). A holder of a `clinical_inbox_item` grant for its lane can find it by its routing id (`handoff_id`), including openPractice's on-call clinician named in its tracked alert, when they hold one (MI-09; MT-7).
- No agent owns, acknowledges, answers, re-routes or closes an item (design call MD-10).

### 6. Urgency

openChart never lowers urgency, by any path. A raise moves deadlines only earlier, and a raise that arrives before its offer is applied when the offer arrives (MI-10). A clinician may raise an item to `priority` or `urgent` inside openChart. openChart reports every such raise as `handoff.clinician_urgency_raised`, with its new `respond_by` and an opaque `raised_by`, over the durable delivery of PL-14 (design call MD-8). The openPractice change then applies three rules (its ER-03, DC-30 and DC-42):
- it shadows the new deadline, never later than its current one;
- a raise to `urgent` gets its urgent path, with emergency guidance if the deadline is missed;
- a raise to `priority` gets a tracked on-call alert, with no guidance for the patient. A raise to `urgent` on an item already answered or closed reopens it inside openChart (design call MD-9).

### 7. Reply, record and authorship

- The reply goes to the patient through HealthLinc, as `reply.deliver`, only after the clinician signs it off under the foundation's CA-04 (ruling 158; MI-11, design call MD-36). `responded` is reported as soon as HealthLinc accepts it with `message.ack` `received` (MI-11, design call MD-15). Until openWallet and openSig can do their part, no reply can be signed off, so none is sent (ruling 152; Decision 16).
- **MI-12 is the single, citable definition of a clinical communication record in openChart.** Each record has an origin: `handoff` for an inbox item, or `ai_consult` for a consult record that `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` writes, whose provenance and full exchange that change defines (design call MD-28). It gives every retrieved turn, keyed by `turn_id`, one author type: `patient` (or `representative`), `ai`, `clinician`, `staff`, `system`, or the fail-safe `unknown`. It also gives the turn a source token: `healthlinc`, `medxfactory` or `openpractice_agent`. An `ai` turn names its source: HealthLinc's coach, medXfactory's medical AI, or an openPractice agent's administrative answer with its answer type and version or its approver. A patient's own turn is never shown as AI content. Every urgency source maps to an author type, and `patient_checklist` maps to `patient` (design call MD-24). MI-12 also states the provenance the record holds, and the rule that a clinical communication is never a diagnosis or a clinical fact of record. MI-13 and MI-14 cite it. `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` references it rather than defining a second record (ruling 64). Nothing is silently overwritten, and a correction is a new version. Each record in a patient's chart is an addition with the foundation's provenance (Decision 16, design call MD-35). The storage shape is decided in planning.
- AI-authored and `unknown` content is never recorded, shown or delivered as clinician-authored (MI-13).
- Patient-reported text and AI text never become a diagnosis, a clinical fact or a billable fact (MI-14, design call MD-11).
- Replies honor confidential-communication instructions (PL-16). An unfiled item's reply stays inside the HealthLinc conversation. A withheld reply keeps the item open (MI-22, design call MD-13).
- If HealthLinc reports through `reply.delivery_failed` that an accepted reply was not delivered, `responded` is not final. The item reopens and stays owned, an `urgent` item escalates, and the owner is pointed to a permitted channel (MI-11, design calls MD-21 and MD-26). openPractice is not told under the handoff contract 1.0 (MT-15).

### 8. Re-routing and splitting

- **An administrative item** is returned with `returned_not_clinical` and is never answered. openChart keeps no administrative queue (MI-15).
- **A mixed item** has its clinical part answered in openChart. openChart asks HealthLinc, with `split.request`, to split the item and resubmit the administrative part to openPractice (MI-16, design call MD-7). The schema has no status for a partial return (MT-6).

### 9. Identity decision

A handoff resolves the patient only through the links that exist (MI-17, ruling 39):
- the `openpractice` row for the `link_id` (PL-02), on an offer marked `linked`, together with the `ehr_patient` triple under openChart's issuer (PL-03);
- where one exists, the openChart reference that the conversation's holder reports for the account (`ehr_patient` in `conversation.result`), as a cross-check only.

The offer's `identity_state` is an input, never a resolution. Where it and openChart's own link state differ, the more uncertain governs. There is no demographic matching. `unlinked_identity` (`legal_name`, `birth_date`, `administrative_sex`) is shown as unverified and never used to match or file, and no Medx or medXfactory reference is used (design calls MD-5, MD-19).

| Situation | `routine` | `priority` or `urgent` |
|---|---|---|
| `linked`; `active` row; triple matches; the holder, if it reports one, agrees | Accept and file | Accept and file |
| `link_in_review` (identity review or demographic hold) | Refused by the schema: `schema_invalid` | Accept unfiled; show the uncertainty; open identity review; escalate under MI-09 |
| `linked`, but openChart holds the row in `identity_review` | Reject `link_in_review` | Accept unfiled; show the uncertainty; open identity review; escalate under MI-09 |
| `unlinked`, with `unlinked_identity` | Refused by the schema: `schema_invalid` | Accept unfiled; show the three fields as unverified; open identity review; escalate under MI-09 |
| `linked` but `link_id` unknown to openChart | Reject `link_unknown` | Accept unfiled; open identity review; escalate under MI-09 (MT-1, resolved) |
| `linked` but the row is `revoked` or `superseded` | Reject `link_revoked` (the schema has no superseded code) | Accept unfiled; open identity review; escalate under MI-09 (MT-1, resolved) |
| `linked` but `ehr_patient` does not match the row | Reject `ehr_patient_mismatch` | Accept unfiled; open identity review; escalate under MI-09 (MT-1, resolved) |
| After acceptance: the holder names a different patient, or the link enters review or is revoked | Stays accepted; becomes unfiled; identity review | Same |

The openPractice change's ER-06 offers only `urgent` and `priority` items on a link in review or with no link. At the same time, openPractice runs its fallback for an urgent item, or sends a tracked on-call alert with no guidance for a priority item. A `routine` row marked `linked` in this table covers only a mismatch between openPractice's and openChart's views. An unfiled item is filed only when a link resolves the patient. No human files it by judgment until the owner rules (design call MD-6, Open Question 7).

### 10. Boundaries

- openChart receives no billing, hold or financial status, and never gates an item on such status (MI-19).
- openChart keeps no administrative queue (MI-15).
- With no gateway configured, the inbox is inert. Enabling it needs four things: an openPractice integration identity; retrieval from HealthLinc under the retrieval contract, plus retrieval from medXfactory where the practice uses its medical AI; HealthLinc's declared `reply_delivery`, `delivery_failure_callback` and `split`; and full cover. Disabling it withdraws the handoff authorization (MI-20, design calls MD-12 and MD-23).

### 11. Clinical-need markers

Ruling 50 lets a clinician keep a patient's clinical messaging and medical-AI consults open for continuity of care. openPractice ER-18 also lets a clinician mark an appointment as clinically needed, so that a payment hold never blocks it. Both are clinical judgments that openPractice applies, and both travel as `clinical_need.marker` (MI-25):
- kinds `messaging_continuity` and `appointment_needed`;
- a `need_id` that openChart mints and reuses for the withdrawal;
- action `mark` or `withdraw`;
- `marked_by`, an opaque clinician reference, and `marked_at`;
- for `mark` only, `valid_until` and `origin`;
- `related_handoff_id` when the origin is `urgent_escalation`;
- no reason or note.

Recording a marker needs no practitioner's sign-off. A withdrawal before its `valid_until` needs one, and otherwise a marker ends by its `valid_until` (design call MD-39, PROPOSED; Open Question 17). openChart never learns whether a hold or lapse exists (MI-19). The marker rules beyond the schema are design call MD-18.

### 12. Standalone operation

openChart must operate without MedxFactory, MedxEHR or openPractice, as the ratified text states. It also operates without HealthLinc or medXfactory, with no conversation source configured. Every inbox component is optional (MI-20, PL-15). The standalone golden-intake tests remain the regression gate.

### 13. AI practice modes (rulings 59, 64, 65, 67 and 157)

The medical director sets medXfactory's AI to advice only, limited practice or full practice. medXfactory's change `define-patient-medical-ai-connection` owns the mode framework and its gates (rulings 56 to 58). Ruling 66 trims ruling 56 with an advice-only patient-consult class whose answers may reach the patient without prior sign-off. Ruling 67, as `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` records it, keeps limited and full practice specified but off. Ruling 80, clause 19, keeps the rule that no AI holds decision authority today, and ruling 157 has an AI only ever make recommendations until a practitioner signs.

This change does not cover AI-authored orders, prescriptions or diagnoses. openChart-spec change `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` is being drafted for them (ruling 64), alongside MedxEHR-spec change `define-medical-ai-record-overlay` on the MedxEHR side. Ruling 59 stands, so until Tier 2 of `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` is in force for an act type, MI-26 applies and fails closed. Ratification alone does not end the hold: that change's AA-01 keeps Tier 2 out of force until `define-clinical-acts-foundation` (openChart-spec#5) is ratified and built and a future governed amendment puts Tier 2 in force (ruling 67), and under ruling 157 limited and full practice as designed would also need a new ruling. Meanwhile:
- AI-authored content is information, recorded under MI-12, or, on that change's write path, a recommendation marked not an authorized decision, with no effect;
- on the paths the inbox controls, content that presents itself as an order, a prescription or a diagnosis is held as AI-authored information, marked as not accepted as a clinical act. On that change's write path the inbox defers to it: an `act.write` is not refused for want of authority, and is recorded in full as the foundation's recommendation record, with no effect, for a clinician to adopt or decline (its AW-16; design call MD-20);
- the limited-practice and full-practice modes are not buildable in openChart.

Ruling 65 places the chart's own record of the medical director's authorization, and the check of every AI write against it, in `define-ai-authored-clinical-records`. Ruling 70 sends the medical AI's records to openChart through MedxEHR's relay, whose openChart side also belongs there. This change cites both and defines neither. It answers that change's tensions AT-5 (MI-26's hold and its account of the write path), AT-6 (MI-12's origins and the `ai_consult` provenance, MD-28), AT-7 (MD-20) and AT-15 (MD-37).

### 14. The conversation-retrieval contract (ruling 69)

openChart owns one neutral contract, `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0`, for every system that holds a patient conversation. HealthLinc and medXfactory implement it (H-1 to H-3, M-1). Its canonical schema is `contracts/openchart-conversation-retrieval-1.0.schema.json`, SHA-256 `774eff0df90e4cb188d081f8b9b6330aa7b92430070df1ad9933abf624d60c42`, listed in `contracts/SHA256SUMS`. MI-02 and MI-03 pin it and own it, and MI-23's tests run against a double of it. It follows the handoff schema's style: the same envelope, closed objects, a random-UUID `message_id` as the idempotency key, coded errors only, and no PHI in any reference (design call MD-22). Unlike the handoff schema, it checks every identifier and time by pattern, never by `format`, so every validator applies the same rules. Its timestamp pattern is the foundation's, character for character, so a turn's `authored_at` can be an addition's `occurred_at`; a day that the month does not have, such as 30 February, passes the pattern and is refused as `schema_invalid` by a check beside the schema (design call MD-22, PROPOSED).

| Message | Direction | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| `capabilities.request` | openChart → holder | Nothing |
| `capabilities.result` | holder → openChart | Nothing. The holder answers a request, or sends it unprompted when its capabilities change. |
| `conversation.retrieve` | openChart → holder | Nothing: every holder supports retrieval |
| `conversation.result` | holder → openChart | Nothing |
| `reply.deliver` | openChart → holder | `reply_delivery` |
| `reply.delivery_failed` | holder → openChart | `delivery_failure_callback` |
| `split.request` | openChart → holder | `split` |
| `message.ack` | both | Nothing. It acknowledges a reply, a callback, a split request or an unprompted declaration, and carries every refusal. |

- **Capability declaration.** A holder declares its versions and optional capabilities in `capabilities.result`. The latest declaration by `declared_at` governs. openChart never sends a message for a capability the holder has not declared, and a holder that receives one anyway refuses it with `capability_not_supported`.
- **Per-turn vocabulary.** Each turn carries `turn_id`, `position`, `author_type` (MI-12's types), `source`, `authored_at`, `request_turn` and plain-text `content`, and a request turn also carries `request_ref`. It may carry `in_reply_to` and `shared_basis`, and an `ai` turn may carry `ai_mode`, `disclosure_code`, `model_ref`, `advice_statement_ref` (design call MD-30) and `emergency_guidance` (design call MD-33). A result may carry `origin_conversation_ref` (design call MD-32). An `openpractice_agent` turn must carry exactly one of `agent_answer_type` and `approved_by`, and a `clinician` turn must carry `reply_id` (design call MD-24). An `ai` turn may also carry `ai_identity` (design call MD-37).
- **Source tokens.** `healthlinc`, `medxfactory` and `openpractice_agent`. A token is added only in a MINOR version.
- **Request turns and consent.** `conversation.retrieve` carries the item's `urgency` and, in `request_ref`, the offer's `healthlinc_message_ref`. `conversation.result` echoes the urgency and requires `consent_ref` and at least one turn. At least one turn is a request turn (`request_turn` true), written by the patient or a representative, carrying `request_ref`, with text that has a character other than whitespace. openChart refuses with `result_mismatch` a result in which no request turn's `request_ref` matches the retrieve's. For `priority` and `urgent`, a request turn carries `shared_basis` `urgent_escalation`, and the result carries `patient_told_at`. For a conversation HealthLinc holds, ruling 86 also shares every turn that triggered the escalation, which no schema can identify, and shares any other turn only as `patient_confirmed`. Beyond that, a result may report `turns_withheld` and may mark each turn's `shared_basis` (`consent_scope`, `patient_confirmed` or `urgent_escalation`); any `urgent_escalation` turn also needs `patient_told_at` (design calls MD-25 and MD-29).
- **Errors.** One set of seventeen codes: `schema_invalid`, `unsupported_major_version`, `unauthorized`, `idempotency_conflict`, `capability_not_supported`, `conversation_not_found`, `reference_not_found`, `result_mismatch`, `consent_not_granted`, `reply_path_invalid`, `temporarily_unavailable`, and the six delivery-failure reasons. `failure_reason` is limited to those six (design call MD-26).
- **Versioning and issuers.** MAJOR.MINOR, compatible exactly within a MAJOR, as PL-12 requires for the boundary contracts. A sender uses the highest MINOR both sides have declared. A breaking change needs a new MAJOR and a new digest, recorded in this change and in each implementing change. Every issuer is a configured, stable identifier, unique across deployments and never derived from a host path, and references are compared as (issuer, namespace, value) triples, as PL-03 requires.
- **Standalone.** openChart works with no holder configured (MI-20). The contract adds no dependency to any other openChart operation.
- **Content.** Plain text only in 1.0, with no attachments (design call MD-27). Content travels only between a holder and openChart, never through openPractice.
- **AI identity.** An `ai` turn may carry `ai_identity`: the AI's `openwallet_id`, with the `model` and `model_version` that produced the turn (ruling 80, part B; ruling 183). The schema refuses it on any other turn and keeps it a closed object, but every property is optional and is checked only as a string within its length bound: 200, 128 and 64 characters. openChart judges the values, in the forms the foundation's AN-02 gives an AI party, when it identifies the turn, so an incomplete or badly formed identity makes only that turn an unidentified AI turn, whose text openChart holds outside the chart, and never hides the rest of the conversation (design call MD-37, PROPOSED). The object stays closed and refused on other turns, PROPOSED, because a closed object keeps PHI out of it. What still fails the whole result: an extra property in `ai_identity`, a value that is not a string or is longer than its bound, and `ai_identity` on a turn that is not `ai`. The previous revision added `ai_identity`, and the digest changed from `6b2157b3…` to `9472b1dd…`. This revision loosens it and checks identifiers and times by pattern, and the digest changes from `9472b1dd…` to `774eff0d…`. A message whose identifiers and times have the shapes the contract has always named stays valid; a value that passed only because a validator did not assert `format`, such as a `message_id` that is not a UUID, is now refused, as is a time with a lowercase `t` or `z` or more than nine fractional-second digits.
- **Self-test.** `contracts/selftest.py`, with its synthetic examples in `contracts/examples/`, checks the schema as the foundation's and the AI write path's self-tests check theirs: every valid example validates, and every invalid one fails for its stated reason only. It also runs openChart's checks beside the schema against the examples: a real calendar day, a result's match with its retrieve (MD-29), and the identification of each AI turn (MD-37), against a synthetic stand-in for the holder's registration. It passes on jsonschema 4.10.3 and 4.26.0 (task 1.5).
- **Known limits of the patterns.** Three values still pass the schema. Only the spec's wording and these conformance tests stand against them:
  - **KL-1.** Request text that is only a zero-width space (U+200B) passes `\S`, because that character is not whitespace. Each holder's contract tests show that a request turn holds the request as written (H-1, M-1). openChart's tests show that the owner can record such a turn as a placeholder, which is a failed retrieval (MD-29, MI-23). The next schema revision should require a visible character, for example by refusing text made only of whitespace and zero-width or other format characters.
  - **KL-2.** A `disclosure_code` such as `jane_roe_hiv_positive` matches the code pattern. Each holder's contract tests show that every code comes from its fixed catalogue of disclosure texts and is the same for every patient shown that text (H-1, M-1). openChart's tests show that it never writes a `disclosure_code` to a log, notification, diagnostic or key (MI-21, MI-23).
  - **KL-3.** An origin value such as `JaneRoe-1970-01-01` matches the opaque pattern. Each holder's contract tests show that the value is minted opaque and never derived from patient data (H-1, M-1). openChart's tests show that it never logs the value and never uses it to resolve a patient (MD-19, MD-32, MI-23).

### 15. Clinical roles and grants (`define-clinical-acts-foundation`)

AU-06 carries the inbox's scopes: ruling 92 places inbox authority in that single grant model, set by the medical director, and makes clinical roles and authority their own small first feature, so the inbox does not wait for encounters and notes. This change cites them by requirement ID, uses AU-06's action, record-class and lane names exactly, and defines no role or grant of its own. This answers the foundation's tension CT-9.

### 16. Additions, sign-off and AI identity (rulings 80, 151 and 152 to 194)

Ruling 80 lets any human or system the chart can identify add to a patient's chart, records where each addition came from, and enters a decision made without authority as a recommendation with no effect. Ruling 151 has every medical decision signed off by the practitioner. This change follows the foundation's general rule (`chart-additions`, AN-01 to AN-14, and CA-04, read at `d9a9c63`) and the AI write path, and re-derives neither:
- **Additions (MI-12, MD-35).** A filed `handoff` record, each turn whose text openChart keeps, each signed-off reply and each `ai_consult` record is an addition with the foundation's provenance: `received_at`, `occurred_at`, `source`, `added_by` and `on_behalf_of`. A reference held as several values, such as a turn's `conversation_ref`, `snapshot_ref` and `turn_id`, becomes the foundation's single `source.ref` as one digest, as MI-12 states (MD-35, PROPOSED). openPractice and each holder are registered systems that the practice can suspend (ruling 159; MI-01, MD-38). Each kind of record of information has a closed shape published with its digest, and text in a message never authorizes a decision (the foundation's AN-06 and CD-65).
- **Identity review (MI-18).** Ruling 80, clause 18 still refuses an addition whose patient cannot be matched, so an unfiled item is no part of any chart. It stays an inbox item, and an urgent or priority one still reaches a clinician.
- **The reply (MI-11, MD-36).** Ruling 158 requires the practitioner's sign-off on a reply, made in openChart (ruling 186) under CA-04, with CD-50's statement form over the reply record. Until openWallet and openSig can do their part, no reply can be signed off (ruling 152): it stays a draft and nothing is sent to the patient. The item stays open and owned, MI-09 escalates it while no clinician has acknowledged it, `clock_expired` brings openPractice's overdue path at the deadline, and the urgent hand-off and emergency guidance never depend on a reply. The work that CA-04's checks need is being drafted in openWallet-spec and openSig-spec. Whether the inbox should be enabled for live patients meanwhile is Open Question 16.
- **Housekeeping and markers (MI-07, MI-25, MD-31, MD-39, MD-41).** Owning, acknowledging and reassigning need no sign-off (ruling 158). Raising, returning and splitting need none either, PROPOSED (MD-41). A close after a signed-off reply or a re-route needs none; a close that ends an item with no reply needs the sign-off, PROPOSED and put to the owner (MD-41; Open Question 22), so until a sign-off can be made no item can be closed without a reply. Recording a clinical-need marker needs no sign-off; withdrawing one before its `valid_until` needs it, PROPOSED and put to the owner (MD-39; Open Question 17), so meanwhile a marker runs to its `valid_until`. An inbox action, reply or marker attempted without its grant takes no effect, and is recorded as a recommendation marked not an authorized decision, never refused (the foundation's AU-06).
- **AI content (MI-13, MI-26, MD-20).** An AI's content is information or a recommendation. An `act.write` on the AI write path is recorded as the foundation's recommendation record, with no effect (its AW-16), and Tier 2 as specified needs a new ruling (ruling 157).
- **AI turns and the quarantines (MI-12, MI-24, MD-37, MD-40).** A retrieved AI turn whose text openChart keeps is an addition, and carries the AI's `openwallet_id`, model and model version (ruling 183) in the retrieval contract's optional `ai_identity`, judged in the forms of the foundation's AN-02 and covered by the holder's registration. An unidentified AI turn's text is held in quarantine outside the chart under the foundation's AN-01 minimum, shown to the clinician handling the item, released into the record only once its AI's identity is complete and covered, and never on MI-12's record; the administrative role is alerted once per holder and cause (ruling 194). The inbox owns that hold; the AI write path owns its held writes. A consult quarantined in the chart (ruling 90) is on MI-12's record with one of ten causes; a write held in quarantine outside the chart is never on it, and the AI write path lists it.
- **Schema refusals (MI-02, MD-38, MT-27).** A message under the inbox's two contracts is not an addition when it arrives, so a schema refusal refuses no addition; ruling 188 governs additions.

## Design calls for owner review

Each item below goes beyond the rulings, and each is proposed, not ruled. Where an item implements a ruling, it cites the ruling, and its mechanics stay a proposed design call. Where a ruling is silent on something consequential, the binding text fails closed and cites the design call.

**For the owner first, in plain words.** The calls added for rulings 80, 151 and 152 to 194 with the most consequence:
- **No reply reaches a patient until openWallet and openSig can do their part (MD-36).** Replies stay drafts; the item stays open and escalated, and openPractice's overdue path runs at the deadline. Whether the inbox should be enabled for live patients meanwhile is Open Question 16.
- **No item can be closed without a reply until openWallet and openSig can do their part (MD-41).** Taking, acknowledging and reassigning need no sign-off (ruling 158), and raising, returning and splitting are proposed to need none. A close that ends an item with no reply records a clinical judgment and is proposed to need the sign-off, so meanwhile such an item stays open (Open Question 22).
- **A clinical-need marker can be recorded with no sign-off; an early withdrawal needs one (MD-39).** Recording widens access to care, and a sign-off would block it until openWallet and openSig exist. Withdrawing before `valid_until` narrows access, so it is proposed to need the sign-off; until one can be made, a marker runs to its `valid_until` (Open Question 17).
- **An AI turn's text is kept only with its AI's identity (MD-37).** Otherwise its text is held outside the chart, in front of the clinician handling the item, as ruling 194 asks, and the practice is told once per holder and cause to fix the registration. Until HealthLinc sends `ai_identity`, every coach turn is held this way. The retrieval contract checks `ai_identity` only for length, so a bad identity never hides the conversation.
- **An action without its grant becomes a recommendation (MD-31).** It takes no effect, is never refused, and is listed for someone who holds the grant.
- **The inbox's schema checks refuse no addition (MD-38, MT-27).** A message under the inbox's contracts is not yet an addition, so ruling 188 is read as not reaching those checks.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **[A missed urgent message]** → A named owner from acceptance. Escalation to the next rostered holder, never back to the owner who failed, and then to the medical director, with repeated alerts. An `urgent` or `priority` item that nobody can own is reported `clock_expired` at once (MD-34). No rejection on identity grounds. `content_unavailable` and `clock_expired` sent at once, so openPractice's fallback runs on time. An expired item stays queued and visible by routing id. `priority` items with identity problems are escalated like urgent ones. A clinician's raise reaches openPractice. A failed urgent delivery reopens the item.
- **[Wrong-patient filing]** → Resolution only through links, no demographic matching, unfiled items, and `unlinked_identity` (three snapshot fields) shown as unverified and never matched.
- **[Clinical content leaks into openPractice]** → Reference-only handoff. Markers carry no reason. No administrative text is sent. Splits go through HealthLinc. The medical AI's records travel through MedxEHR's relay (ruling 70).
- **[AI text mistaken for a clinician's]** → An author type and a named source on every piece of content (MI-12). No AI drafting, and no agent action.
- **[An AI-authored order or prescription acted on]** → In an inbox item, MI-26 holds it as AI-authored information, marked as not accepted as a clinical act, until Tier 2 of `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` is in force for its act type. On that change's write path, its AW-16 records every act write meanwhile as a recommendation with no effect (MD-20, MT-14).
- **[No reply can reach a patient until openWallet and openSig can do their part]** → A reply stays its author's draft and nothing is sent (MI-11, MD-36). The item stays open and owned, `clock_expired` brings openPractice's overdue path at the deadline, and the urgent hand-off and emergency guidance never depend on a reply. Whether to enable the inbox for live patients meanwhile is Open Question 16.
- **[An AI turn kept in the chart without its identity]** → An `ai` turn's text is kept only with a complete, well-formed `ai_identity` whose `openwallet_id` the holder's registration covers; otherwise its text is held outside the chart, in front of a clinician, and the practice is alerted once per holder and cause (MD-37).
- **[One bad AI identity hides the conversation]** → The contract checks `ai_identity` for length only, and openChart judges it per turn, so only that turn is held (MD-37).
- **[An item ended, or access narrowed, with no clinical check]** → A close that ends an item with no reply, and a marker withdrawal before its `valid_until`, need the sign-off, so until one can be made neither happens (MD-41, MD-39).
- **[An action without authority lost without trace]** → It takes no effect, but it is recorded as a recommendation, and its author is told at once (MD-31).
- **[Double alerts]** → openChart's escalation and openPractice's tracked alerts can both fire for one item. This is accepted for safety.
- **[Two clock policies drift]** → The earlier deadline governs (MT-4).
- **[Unfiled records accumulate]** → They are listed for identity review. Manual filing is Open Question 7.
- **[Confidentiality breach through notifications]** → PL-16 restriction codes go to HealthLinc. An unfiled item's reply stays in the conversation.
- **[A clinically relevant turn withheld under consent]** → Every result carries the request turn, and a priority or urgent item's request turns are always shared; a result without them is a failed retrieval, reported as `content_unavailable` (MD-29). Other withheld turns show as a partial view with their count (MD-25). Residual risk, recorded as MT-16.
- **[A holder's capability lapses]** → openChart sends nothing a holder has not declared, keeps every item owned, and tells the owner and the medical director (MD-23).
- **[A request turn that hides the question]** → The schema refuses whitespace-only request text and a request turn without `request_ref`, and openChart refuses a result whose request turns carry no matching `request_ref`. A placeholder, or text made only of zero-width characters (KL-1), under the matching reference is caught only by the holder's conformance and by the owner, who can record the retrieval as failed (MD-29).
- **[No live inbox without AU-06]** → The inbox fails closed: it cannot be enabled until `define-clinical-acts-foundation`'s feature (1) is built with AU-06 in force (ruling 92; MD-31), so openPractice routes no clinical item to it.
- **[A grant lapse empties a roster slot]** → MI-08's warnings, alerts and fallbacks keep a human reaching every item, and no offer is accepted without a named owner (MD-4).
- **[Contract drift]** → The digest pins, and tasks 1.3 and 1.5.
- **[No constitution]** → The plan phase is blocked until a constitution is adopted.
