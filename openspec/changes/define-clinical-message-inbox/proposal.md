## Why

openPractice is becoming the patient app's gateway and router (ruling 34). Clinical questions go to openChart, the doctor's clinical interface, where the doctor reads and answers them. openPractice keeps only a routing record, with no clinical content.

HealthLinc is the patient's gateway to their doctor (ruling 51). Through it, the patient can reach their human doctor, through openPractice to openChart, or medXfactory's medical AI. HealthLinc's own in-app AI is a coach that gives general information only (rulings 51 and 54). The medical AI's records reach the chart through MedxEHR, which relays them with receipts. medXfactory does not write to openChart directly, and nothing passes through openPractice (rulings 53 and 70).

The openPractice side is `opensoft/openPractice-spec` change `define-patient-gateway-and-escalation`, on `opensoft/openPractice-spec` `main` at `0a82fc2`, still draft and unratified. Its capability `escalation-routing` (ER-01 to ER-18) defines the reference-only contract `openpractice-clinical-handoff/1.0`. Its design lists an openChart clinical message inbox as a required companion change, and ruling 47 asked for this change to be drafted.

openChart has no inbox today. Its built DocTypes are `OC Patient`, `OC Patient External Identifier`, `OC Intake Submission` and eight patient-reported statement families. None holds a message, a clinician-authored record or a clinical work queue.

This change specifies openChart's half of the handoff contract. It reuses every message and code in openPractice's canonical schema, pinned by digest, and defines no message of its own toward openPractice. It also defines the one conversation-retrieval contract that HealthLinc and medXfactory implement (ruling 69).

## What Changes

- **Inbox and contract.** openChart receives routed clinical items under `openpractice-clinical-handoff/1.0`, from a dedicated integration role. It validates every message against the canonical schema, pinned by path and SHA-256 digest at openPractice-spec `main` revision `0a82fc2` (MI-01, MI-02).
- **Content by reference, under openChart's own contract.** openChart owns `openchart-conversation-retrieval/1.0`, a neutral contract whose schema and digest live in this change, and HealthLinc and medXfactory implement it (ruling 69). Under it, openChart fetches the escalated conversation by `conversation_ref` from the system `conversation_source` names: HealthLinc for any conversation it holds (a coach conversation, a direct message to the doctor or an administrative thread), or medXfactory for a medical-AI conversation. Each turn comes with its author type and source, and an AI turn may state the mode and disclosure under which it reached the patient. The retrieval names the patient's request by its reference. The result carries the patient's sharing-consent reference and always that request, under the same reference and as the patient wrote it; a placeholder is a failed retrieval. For a priority or urgent item, that request is always shared with the patient told, and a result without it is a failed retrieval. For a HealthLinc conversation, ruling 86 adds that a turn is shared only once the patient has confirmed it, except the escalated question and, on a priority or urgent item, the turns that triggered the escalation, which are always shared with the patient told. Reply delivery, the delivery-failure callback and split are capabilities a holder declares. openChart never fetches content from openPractice. When retrieval fails, the item stays open and owned, and openChart reports `content_unavailable` (MI-02, MI-03).
- **Acknowledgment and clock.** openChart acknowledges every offer, idempotently. It owns the queue and the response clock (ruling 43), with windows from an openChart policy that the medical director approves. Until one is approved, openChart uses the offer's own deadlines; openPractice's proposed defaults are not adopted as facts. No deadline is ever extended (MI-04, MI-05).
- **Status back to openPractice.** openChart reports `responded`, `clock_expired`, `returned_not_clinical`, `content_unavailable` and `closed` through the durable outbound delivery of PL-14 (MI-06).
- **Ownership and cover.** Every item has one named owner, who holds the `clinician` or `nurse` role (AU-01) and `own` over `clinical_inbox_item` for its lane, `physician` or `nurse`, from `define-clinical-acts-foundation` (openChart-spec#5; AU-06, where ruling 92 places inbox authority). AU-06's lane rule decides which role may hold a grant in each lane. Closing an item with an outcome needs `close` over `clinical_inbox_item`, after which openChart sends `closed`. Replies need `reply` over `clinical_communication`, and markers need `mark` over `clinical_need_marker`. Anyone without the grant fails closed. The cover roster names only grant holders, and a grant lapse that empties a slot alerts the medical director and the administrative role at once and falls back to the medical director. An offer is never accepted without a named owner. Reassignment, absence and roster cover keep it owned. An urgent or priority item that nobody can own is reported `clock_expired` at once, so openPractice's fallback runs. An unacknowledged urgent item escalates to the next rostered holder, never back to the owner who failed to acknowledge, and then to the medical director. No agent acts in the inbox (MI-07 to MI-09).
- **Urgency.** openChart never lowers urgency. It applies `handoff.urgency_raised` and moves deadlines only earlier, including a patient's raise through HealthLinc's red-flag checklist (`patient_checklist`, ruling 71), which it records as the patient's. A clinician's raise to `priority` or `urgent` is reported to openPractice as `handoff.clinician_urgency_raised`, with openChart's new deadline. openPractice never moves its shadow deadline later. Only a raise to `urgent` gets openPractice's full fallback with patient guidance; a raise to `priority` gets a tracked on-call alert (MI-10).
- **Reply and record.** The clinician's reply goes to the patient through HealthLinc. MI-12 is the single, citable definition of a clinical communication record, for inbox items and for AI consult records (origin `ai_consult`), which exist without the inbox or openPractice. It gives each piece of content an author type (patient, AI or clinician, among others, with `unknown` as a fail-safe that is never clinician-authored), and an AI author names its source: HealthLinc's coach, medXfactory's medical AI or an openPractice agent. A patient's own turn is never shown as AI content. It records provenance, and it rules that a clinical communication is never a diagnosis or a clinical fact of record. AI-authored and clinician-authored content are always distinguishable. If HealthLinc reports that an accepted reply was not delivered, the item reopens, and an urgent one escalates. Patient-reported and AI text never becomes a diagnosis, a clinical fact or a billable fact. The medical AI's records reach the chart only through MedxEHR's relay (ruling 70), so the inbox keeps a medical-AI exchange by reference, with per-turn metadata and no text (MI-11 to MI-14, MI-24).
- **Re-routing.** A clinician returns an administrative item without answering it. A mixed item is split through HealthLinc, and only its clinical part is answered. openChart keeps no administrative queue (MI-15, MI-16).
- **Identity.** A handoff resolves the patient only through the links that exist (ruling 39; PL-02, PL-03). The offer's `identity_state` is an input, and there is no matching. `unlinked_identity` (legal name, birth date and administrative sex only) is shown as unverified and never matched, and no Medx or medXfactory reference is used. An unresolved identity goes to identity review. An `urgent` or `priority` item is accepted unfiled, with the uncertainty shown, escalates, and still reaches a clinician. Only a `routine` offer is rejected with the schema's code (MI-17, MI-18).
- **Boundaries.** openChart receives no billing, payment-hold or financial status (ruling 41). It must operate without MedxFactory, MedxEHR or openPractice, as the ratified text says, and also without HealthLinc or medXfactory. With no gateway configured, the inbox is inert (MI-19, MI-20).
- **Clinical-need markers.** A named clinician can record and withdraw a `messaging_continuity` marker (ruling 50) or an `appointment_needed` marker. openChart attributes it and sends it to openPractice as `clinical_need.marker`, as codes only: a `need_id`, mark or withdraw, `valid_until`, and an origin, with the urgent handoff's id for an `urgent_escalation` origin (MI-25).
- **AI practice modes.** The medical director sets medXfactory's AI to advice only, limited practice or full practice (ruling 59); medXfactory's change owns that framework (rulings 56 to 58). This change does not cover AI-authored orders, prescriptions or diagnoses. openChart-spec change `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` is being drafted for them, with MedxEHR-spec change `define-medical-ai-record-overlay` (ruling 64). Ruling 59 stands, so until Tier 2 of that change is in force for an act type, AI-authored content is communication only, with its source named. Ratification alone does not end the hold, because that change refuses every AI act write until `define-clinical-acts-foundation` (openChart-spec#5) is built and a governed amendment puts Tier 2 in force. In an inbox item, an AI-authored order, prescription or diagnosis is held as communication and creates no clinical act; on that change's write path, the inbox defers to it. Ruling 67 keeps the practice modes specified but off. Ruling 65 places the chart's check of every AI write against the medical director's authorization in `define-ai-authored-clinical-records` (MI-26).
- **Safety.** No PHI in logs, errors, notifications, diagnostics or keys. Replies honor confidential-communication instructions (PL-16). Contract tests cover the boundary (MI-21 to MI-23).
